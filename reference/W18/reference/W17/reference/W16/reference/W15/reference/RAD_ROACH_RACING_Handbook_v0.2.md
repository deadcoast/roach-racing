# RAD ROACH RACING
## WASTELANDER LANISTA
### The Wasteland Hobbyist’s Handbook — Foundation v0.2

**Build something fast. Make it yours. Win the race.**

Rad Roach Racing is the wasteland’s great shared obsession. People catch extraordinary insects, breed prized bloodlines, browse Wrangloid shops, assemble ridiculous living racers, argue over their modifications, and turn up on race day to see whose roach is fastest.

For some, it is a cheap kit and a few races after work. For others, it is a room full of breeding tanks, a workshop full of experiments, a business, or a campaign to reach New World City. A beautiful collection, a breakthrough breeding line, an outrageous custom build, and a championship-winning roach are all things worth having.

The player enters that entire hobby. Racing supplies its public spectacle, competitive ambition, and shared language; the surrounding sandbox supplies many ways to participate and become exceptional.

*This foundation replaces v0.1. The established premise, Wrangloids, and championship route come from the concept brief. Additional names and mechanics below are proposed worldbuilding. The sect anatomy is fictional; performance values remain to be developed and tested.*

---

## 1. What carries over from Pocket Circuit Racing

PCR supplies the pleasures and functional structure of a build-and-race hobby. RRR expresses them through living specimens, field knowledge, breeding, grafting, wasteland commerce, and outrageous races.

The Mutant League comparison establishes the relationship: the originating sport remains understandable while the participants, spectacle, culture, and possibilities acquire their own identity. RRR’s translated parts must work together as a racer, but its world does not have to reproduce a car game’s menu restrictions.

| PCR foundation | RRR expression |
|---|---|
| Choosing a car or kit | Choosing a rad-roach host, a complete graft kit, or the beginnings of a custom project. |
| Shopping for parts | Visiting a Wrangloid for live donors, prepared sects, breeding stock, kits, and expertise. |
| Manufacturers and product lines | Breeder houses, stabilized donor lines, signature kits, and celebrated independent Wrangloids. |
| Collecting desirable components | Finding exceptional specimens, acquiring rare forms, breeding valuable traits, and assembling a personal sect collection. |
| Building and customizing | Preparing, positioning, grafting, finishing, and tuning the racer yourself. |
| Improving performance | Better biological stock, better components, better assembly, better preparation, and better racing decisions. |
| Local meets and rivalry | Shop-track regulars, neighborhood champions, faction clubs, traveling challengers, and public grudges. |
| Modular circuits and championships | Home-built tracks, club venues, specialist divisions, and the road to the world Derby. |

There is real breadth in the reference hobby: Tamiya supports beginner assembly, advanced modification, and dedicated painting/detailing, while SEGA’s Pocket Circuit includes parts acquisition, fastest-time competition, and local rivals.[^1][^2][^3]

**The 1:1 mapping is a completeness check for the racing foundation. It is not a ceiling on the sandbox.**

## 2. Thirty years of Rad Roachin’

The founders’ story is told in every club, and every club insists somebody’s grandfather knew one of them.

First came the bullhorn beetle: one racer pressed its horn into his roach’s carapace, watched the tissue incorporate it, and brought an entirely new problem to the next race. Then came the mantis pincers. Then the grasshopper legs—and the humiliation of watching the third friend jump clean over the argument and win.

Thirty years later, children reproduce those three builds in beginner kits. Champions commission extraordinary versions of the same parts. Collectors argue about what the original insects actually looked like. Wrangloids advertise “founder-style” stock at prices that make old hobbyists furious.

The roaches that survived the nuclear winter have become competitors, collectibles, family breeding projects, and the basis of a sprawling trade. Their universal insect-part assimilation remains the fundamental biological premise.

Racing stays a small-creature hobby even when its audience becomes enormous. Competitors travel in cases; assembly happens on benches; insects live in terraria. World City magnifies the spectacle with grandstands, cameras, announcers, and elaborate courses.

A **lanista** builds, prepares, and campaigns roaches. A **Wrangloid** makes a living from the insect trade. One person can be both—or neither, and still be an enthusiastic collector or casual racer.

# PART I — THE LIVING HOBBY

## 3. Inside a Wrangloid’s shop

A **Wrangloid**—wasteland dialect for *wrangler*—is a successful hobbyist whose knowledge of finding, catching, keeping, and breeding insects became a career. The distinction is experience and livelihood. Anyone can attempt the same work.

The shop resembles a specialist fish store: rows of living enclosures, handwritten stock cards, breeding tanks behind the counter, unusual specimens held individually, and regular customers studying the latest arrivals.

Whole insects are the center of the business. Prepared sects, starter kits, care supplies, transport cases, and workshop services grow around them.

One customer wants the prettiest specimen. Another wants a breeding pair. A third has brought in a capture and wants to know whether its unusually thick forelimbs are worth anything. Someone at the back is timing a roach fitted with the shop’s latest line.

### What is actually for sale

**Live stock** includes ordinary hobby insects, wild captures, selected breeding animals, distinctive color forms, and expensive proven lines. Two specimens of the same species can differ in size, useful traits, condition, appearance, and price.

**Prepared sects** offer a convenient route from shopping to assembly. The buyer can inspect the actual graft, its donor identity, its tested properties, and its condition.

**Kits** combine a host and a coordinated selection of grafts. Beginner tins offer an approachable first build; signature kits reproduce a famous style; specialist sets might contain everything needed for a hopper conversion.

**Services** include identification, appraisal, breeding arrangements, boarding, preparation, fitting assistance, and performance testing. Paying for expertise provides convenience and access to knowledge; it does not make participation dependent on hiring a professional.

A shop’s reputation has substance. A breeder who accurately describes stock and produces consistent offspring earns trust. A trader selling beautiful but poorly performing specimens develops a different clientele—or a bad name.

### A specimen, not an anonymous loot icon

A tank might read:

> **SALTGLASS MANTIS — FIELD-CAUGHT ADULT**  
> Long-reach forelimbs. Fast closing response. Right grasping spine chipped.  
> Breeding history unknown. Demonstration available.

Its appeal depends on the buyer. A breeder may value the reach, a collector its translucent markings, and a racer the usable left-side graft. “Rare” does not answer all three questions.

## 4. Finding your own stock

Every hobbyist can leave town and search for insects. A Wrangloid knows more productive places, recognizes better specimens, and returns with useful catches more consistently.

Field-catching is an activity with discoverable knowledge: habitats, activity periods, feeding traces, shed shells, calls, weather, and local rumors. A useful discovery provides both a specimen and a better understanding of where similar specimens might be found.

**Kilnback scarabs** gather in warm, abandoned furnace cavities. Their shell quality and muscle development interest different buyers. **Switchyard hoppers** shelter along overgrown rail embankments; their reach and spring characteristics vary. **Glasswing dragonflies** patrol old cooling ponds, where finding one is easier than bringing it home intact.

A beginner can follow a shopkeeper’s lead and return with common usable stock. An expert can learn to recognize an overlooked variant, protect its valuable features during capture, and establish a source other hobbyists have not discovered.

Knowledge is earned through observation, experimentation, people, and records. Looking in the correct place can succeed before a journal formally records it. Championship rank does not grant permission for insects to exist.

A collection ledger records where a specimen came from, what has been observed, and what remains uncertain. Finding, catching, keeping, breeding, and preparing are distinct actions; discovering an insect does not automatically turn it into every possible part.

## 5. Breeding: producing the next desirable line

Breeding improves actual biological stock. Its rewards include stronger performance traits, more consistent offspring, distinctive appearance, valuable breeders, and lines people recognize by name.

There are two connected projects. **Host breeding** develops roach bodyforms and native characteristics. **Donor breeding** develops insects whose useful anatomy becomes sect material.

A player might select grasshoppers for powerful takeoffs, then select within that line for more consistent joint response. Another might breed mantises with unusually long arms and striking white coloration. A competitive breeder and a collector can value different descendants of the same pair.

The proposed inheritance model distinguishes inherited traits, development, and present condition. Inspection reveals visible features immediately; testing reveals performance; breeding records improve predictions about offspring. Unknown properties are displayed as unknown rather than disguised as exact measurements.

For this foundation, installed grafts are build components, not automatically inherited copies. Reproducing a famous racer involves its host line, donor lines, and assembly recipe. That keeps breeding and building valuable to each other.

A beginner uses a labeled breeding setup and understandable pairing guidance. An enthusiast compares generations. A dedicated Wrangloid maintains pedigrees and sells selected offspring. These are levels of involvement in the same system.

Preparation has an inventory consequence: a removed limb is no longer also attached to an unchanged donor; preparing a vital organ consumes that specimen. A living breeder and a jar of its parts are different assets. The player can retain prized parents and use descendants to supply future builds.

Buying remains an equally legitimate route. A racer who loves racing can buy excellent stock; a breeder who dislikes championship campaigning can build a successful business supplying it.

## 6. Rarity, quality, and tiers

**Better stock can be genuinely better.** A superior drive graft may deliver more speed at comparable demand. A better shell may provide the same protection at lower mass. Desirable discoveries and breeding successes should produce real upgrades as well as alternative specialties.

| Property | What it tells the hobbyist |
|---|---|
| **Species / form** | What anatomy and behaviors the specimen can supply. |
| **Specimen quality** | How well its useful traits are developed and perform. |
| **Condition** | Its current maturity, damage, and readiness for the intended use. |
| **Pedigree** | What is known about ancestry and the repeatability of inherited traits. |
| **Rarity** | How unusual or difficult to obtain that form is. |
| **Preparation quality** | How successfully a particular graft was prepared and configured. |
| **Performance tier** | A readable summary of tested performance within its component family. |

Proposed public performance bands are **Hobby, Club, Circuit, Premier, and Apex**. They describe measured components, not required player levels. An extraordinary wild capture can yield a high-tier sect; a common, carefully developed line can also become exceptional.

A trophy-colored insect can be expensive without being fast. A plain insect can be an outstanding donor. A champion can possess both beauty and performance.

Race class, competition level, price, and component tier remain separate. No universal Graft Point budget is established by this handbook.

# PART II — BUILD YOUR ROACH

## 7. The kit on your workbench

A **sect** is a usable piece of insect anatomy. A **sect module** is that anatomy prepared as an installable component or connected assembly. A whole donor is the living source, with its own identity and possible uses.

The starting roach is already a functioning animal. Its native anatomy supplies every essential racing function. Building replaces, extends, and rearranges those functions; the beginner does not have to replace nine organs before anything can move.

A Wrangloid starter tin contains a host, coordinated prepared sects, a fitting guide, and finishing materials. A self-sourced kit contains the player’s own captures and preparations. Both lead to hands-on assembly.

The player lays out the build, compares components, positions the grafts, watches the host incorporate them, adjusts posture and alignment, and applies colors, numbers, and markings. The result should visibly reflect those decisions.

Beginner guides offer tested assemblies. Experts can modify geometry and combine donor sources. Saved build sheets record specific specimens, arrangements, and settings; a shared recipe can specify substitute ranges where identical biological stock is unavailable.

Tamiya’s beginner packs provide a useful accessibility model: an assembly kit and selected upgrade parts can arrive together, ready for a manageable first project.[^4] RRR can offer that convenience while leaving field sourcing and custom breeding fully available.

## 8. The core 1:1 parts catalogue

The following roles cover the car-building foundation. PCR’s game menus and physical Mini 4WD do not contain identical options; the core mapping and the additional physical-hobby components are distinguished below.

**These are functional categories, not a demand that every insect be cut into one item per menu slot.** A complete hopper assembly can include limbs, leverage, spring joints, and an active jump. Advanced builders can work on its subcomponents. Each physical contribution is accounted for once.

### Body / car selection → Host and bodyform

**Gutterbred** is the familiar low, broad-backed starter roach, with a flexible working body. **Needleback** has a narrow, tapered profile prized for fast, slender builds. **Broadback** offers a stout thorax and generous working area for muscular and heavily modified racers.

Host selection gives the project its foundation and recognizable silhouette. Shell shape, natural coloration, proportions, and lineage also make hosts worth collecting.

### Frame → Thoracic bracing

**Roach Flexrail** uses overlapping flexible plates. **Scarab Boxbrace** fuses thick interlocking support plates around the thorax. **Locust Truss** supplies slender, lightweight support struts.

These change structural support, body flex, load distribution, and how strongly other grafts can work against the host. The distinction follows the structural and layout role of a miniature-car chassis, rather than equating every frame change with armor.[^5]

### Motor → Drive musculature

**Roach Flat-Six** is a balanced, six-lobed muscle assembly. **Cicada Redline** is a ribbed, rapidly contracting bundle prized for high stride cadence. **Scarab Torque Bundle** is compact and thick, delivering powerful working strokes for acceleration and loaded racers.

Motor choice in the reference hobby distinguishes high-speed, high-torque, and balanced output; the RRR equivalents preserve those useful differences through fictional donor musculature.[^1]

### Gears → Hip leverage and gait couplings

**Mole-Cricket Lowstep** uses short, stout levers for forceful starts. **Tiger-Beetle Longthrow** uses elongated linkage arms for greater travel per working stroke. **Planthopper Lockstep** uses paired toothed couplings to coordinate limb timing.

These determine how available drive is delivered. The functional translation is gearing and coordination, not an additional source of power. In the reference hobby, changing gears likewise adjusts acceleration and speed characteristics.[^1]

### Battery → Metabolic reserves

**Roach Reserve** is a compact general-purpose storage organ. **Honeybee Nectar Crop** is a translucent, amber-filled sac adapted for rapid energy delivery. **Waxmoth Fatbody** is a pale, layered reserve favored for sustained running.

Reserve capacity, delivery, and replenishment provide the battery-equivalent tuning space. Different preparations of the same donor family can improve sprint output, total capacity, or both.

### Tires → Foot-contact sects

**Sticktoe Pads** are broad, compliant gripping surfaces. **Tiger-Beetle Needles** are narrow, hard contact tips for quick steps. **Mole-Cricket Cleats** are blunt digging toes that purchase on loose track surfaces.

The visible contact surface affects traction, release, and footing. Front, middle, and rear pairs can be mixed. The insect implementation uses steps and gripping feet rather than copying wheel behavior literally.

### Side stabilizers → Balance and support sects

**Mantis Counterbraces** unfold laterally to catch a developing lean. **Beetle Guardknuckles** provide rounded support points near the shell edges. **Fly Gyropods** are paired sensory stalks that help the adapted host coordinate balance corrections.

One option physically braces; another helps the animal respond. Their benefits should appear in posture and movement, giving spectators something recognizable to notice.

### Suspension → Landing joints

**Pleural Bellows** are folded compression membranes at the limb roots. **Locust Springknees** offer longer elastic travel. **Scarab Cushions** are compact, dense impact pads for substantial loads.

They govern compression and landing response. A complete grasshopper leg can combine this role with a jump mechanism; spring support and deliberate takeoff remain separately understandable functions.

### Front wing / aero attachment → Shell vanes

**Beetle Browfoil** is a shaped plate over the head. **Locust Split-Vanes** are paired adjustable blades alongside the front shell. **Moth Fanbrake** is a pleated surface that opens to check speed and pitch.

These translate aerodynamic attitude control. Their effects belong to RRR’s stylized racing physics. Powered flight is a larger locomotion system with its own wing muscles and control anatomy.

## 9. Advanced kit components

The physical Mini 4WD hobby supplies additional useful reference categories beyond the core game-menu mapping: rollers, braces, dampers, brakes, and related attachment parts are independently configurable.[^6][^7]

| Physical-hobby component | RRR equivalent | What the player works on |
|---|---|---|
| Wheels and hub geometry | **Limb spars:** Roach Stubs, Tiger-Beetle Stilts, Scarab Splaylegs. | Leg reach, stance, leverage, and clearance. |
| Shafts, bearings, and joints | **Joint sleeves:** Lacewing Sleeves, Roach Bushings, Beetle Cups. | Smooth articulation, alignment, sealing, and transmission losses. |
| Rollers and mounts | **Rail guides:** Beetle Cheeks, Roach Runners, Scarab Shoes. | Glancing or sliding along a course wall without snagging. |
| Brakes | **Ventral grips:** Moth-Felt Pads, Sticktoe Gripstrips, Cricket Spurs. | Where, when, and how strongly the racer slows. |
| Mass dampers | **Rebound organs:** Beetle Pearl, Roach Sidepods, Larval Keel. | Constrained internal masses that reduce rebound after landing. |
| Body finishing and decoration | Shell stains, natural color lines, number plates, breeder marks, and custom ornament. | The racer’s appearance, identity, and presentation. |

In the reference parts, brakes limit excessive jump speed while mass dampers reduce bouncing; RRR preserves that difference instead of treating every handling component as the same stability bonus.[^7][^8]

## 10. The unmistakably RRR sects

Horns, grasping arms, jumping legs, armor, and flight are part of the sport’s identity from the outset. They are integrated into builds, kits, donor markets, breeding projects, and track design.

### Offensive anatomy

**Bullhorn Ram:** a thick, forward-curving beetle horn, grafted with its supporting base. It hooks, lifts, and shoves during a charge. Shape, reach, stiffness, and mounting angle distinguish specimens.

**Mantis Catchclaws:** folded grasping arms with long spined inner edges. They intercept attacks, seize opponents, and rip at exposed anatomy. Reach, closing speed, and control matter as much as sheer size.

**Stag Gatecrashers:** broad branching jaws that create an unmistakable front silhouette. They can clamp, sweep, and force room in a crowded pack.

**Bombardier Boiler:** a paired gland-and-outlet assembly producing a short-range chemical burst in RRR’s fictional biology. Rearward and flank configurations support different attacking styles.

These actions can cause meaningful race damage. Their animation, position, and effects make the spectacle readable: a claw actually catches something, a horn actually displaces a rival, and an injured running limb changes how that roach moves.

### Mobility and protection

**Grasshopper Hoppers** supply powerful, folded hind legs for overtaking, evasion, gaps, and airborne moves. **Click-Beetle Kickback** supplies a snapping recovery hinge. **Dragonfly Quadwing** supplies four articulated wings for maneuvering; **Locust Longwing** favors sustained forward flight.

Flight assemblies include the required drive and wing-root coordination. Basic walking remains available; richer ground-and-air integration is a build possibility rather than a universal prohibition.

**Roach Raceskin**, **Scarab Laminate**, and **Ironclad Mantle** represent light, medium, and heavy protective constructions. Their coverage, articulation, shape, and quality are visible parts of the build.

### Building freedom

Universal assimilation allows inventive arrangements. A horn does not automatically prohibit every foil; a mantle does not automatically prohibit every wing. Split plating, different graft positions, and altered support structures can create workable combinations.

When a combination fails, show the physical reason: blocked movement, insufficient support, excessive loading, or interference. The player can then revise the construction. Event legality is a separate question.

**Tin Can Special** is the affordable tin behind the counter. A player can assemble it, name it, decorate it, and immediately enjoy local racing. It is also a foundation for further collecting and modification.

**Last Laugh** begins with a memorable hopper capture or an enticing shop specimen. Its owner might race it immediately, retain related stock for breeding, or chase an even better jump line. It can be fast, spectacular, and personally meaningful at once.

**Rent’s Due** is an unmistakable Open-class bruiser built to charge through traffic and win. Its split foil illustrates an engineered horn-and-aero combination. A Fireworks derivative uses light carapace and a qualifying offensive configuration.

**Skyhook** belongs to a different racing spectacle: aerial packs, elevated turns, and threading gates. Its build remains traceable to the same power, energy, structure, handling, and assembly roles.

# PART III — TRACKS, RACING, AND THE WIDER WORLD

## 12. A circuit-building hobby of its own

RRR tracks can be collected, assembled, modified, hosted, and raced. The real Mini 4WD reference supports slot-together straights and interchangeable curve, wave, and lane-change sections.[^9][^10]

Wasteland versions use standardized scrap fittings, pressed channels, shaped shell inserts, industrial tubing, and extravagant club-made features. A home circuit and a championship installation share an assembly language, allowing players to start small and build outward.

| Reference section | RRR module | Racing possibilities |
|---|---|---|
| Straight | **Gutter Run** | Flat-out acceleration, passing, drafting-style race behavior, and charges. |
| Curve | **Drum Bend** | Sweeping pack movement and contested lines. |
| Tight turn | **Valve Hairpin** | Braking, tight passes, and rapid acceleration out. |
| Wave | **Radiator Weave** | Quick direction changes and gaps opening in the field. |
| Bank | **Dish Bank** | High-speed, tilted corners with conspicuous side-by-side racing. |
| Climb / descent | **Boiler Rise / Spillway Drop** | Loaded climbing, acceleration, and pitch changes. |
| Jump | **Broken-Gutter Gap** | Long jumps, airborne overtakes, and contested landings. |
| Lane changer / crossover | **Switchyard Crossover** | Position changes and compact over-and-under routes. |
| Bridge / elevation supports | **Pipe Rack** | Stacked layouts, clearances, and elevated racing. |
| Start / timing | **Release Rack / Tally Gate** | Starts, laps, splits, records, and finishes. |

Straight and bend modules can carry smooth enamel, loose ash, textured metal, or shallow sludge inserts. Surface choice gives feet, clearance, body shape, and preparation room to matter.

RRR-specific pieces include **Pack Runs** with room to fight and pass, **Squeeze Chutes** that compress traffic, **Hopper Spurs** with launch-and-landing routes, and **Flight Hoops** that construct three-dimensional courses. Locomotion-specific bypass pieces keep a course usable for the intended entrants.

Guided lanes are one available layout style, not a requirement on every track. A home course might be divided; a Fireworks venue might favor broad shared sections. Containment and mandatory gates define valid progress while still permitting climbing, jumping, or lateral moves where the event allows them.

Modularity also creates a non-racing specialty. Players can become admired course builders and hosts: collecting pieces, devising memorable sections, testing layouts, staging club events, and improving their venues.

## 13. Race classes and the Derby route

The race is decided by finishing order after completing the required course. Circuit championships use the results of their races. Violence changes what happens during a race; it does not replace the finish line with a kill count.

| Division | Established identity |
|---|---|
| **Open** | The most famous and competitive class. Extreme combinations, weapons, armor, and experimental builds. No universal equipment-point ceiling is imposed. |
| **Flyers** | Sustained-flight competitors race other flyers. Flight is a division boundary, including for Open events. |
| **All-Out Offensive / Fireworks** | A meaningful minimum offensive build and light carapace. The celebrated violence-heavy spectacle. |
| **Speedsters** | No weapon modules. Native biting, collisions, and bodily contact remain possible. Speed is the main attraction. |

Clubs can also offer boxed-kit races, beginner heats, or matched-spec events. A newcomer can begin with a horn kit or a hopper kit as readily as a plain runner, in an appropriate event.

The **What’s Left of the World Derby Circuit** connects local clubs to the sport’s largest stage. Its championship path remains:

**Locals → Regionals → Factionals → faction representation in New World City → the What’s Left of the World Derby.**

Open entry welcomes ambitious builders at the appropriate qualifying stage. World City representation is earned through the circuits. A wealthy trader may buy excellent stock; that does not buy a place in the final.

These competitive milestones govern championship participation. They do not automatically lock insect-catching, breeding, collecting, commerce, or workshop experimentation behind racing wins.

## 14. Race day

The shop shuts its breeding-room door. Transport cases open on the benches. Someone recognizes a rare horn; somebody else accuses its owner of paying too much. A beginner borrows advice from a regular. The announcer introduces a roach the crowd remembers from last week.

Preparation establishes the build and its racing tendencies. The creatures supply movement, contact, recoveries, and surprises. The extent of live player intervention remains an implementation choice; this foundation does not impose a fixed pool of command tokens.

The camera and commentary follow the lead, position changes, close calls, and final charge. Build decisions are visible in that action: a hopper clears the pack, a ram opens a line, or newly bred drive musculature produces an obvious burst of pace.

Afterward, the result comes first: who won, how close it was, which records changed, and what the crowd remembers. Trophies, prize stock, money, sponsorship attention, and rival challenges give the outcome substance.

A simple performance summary is available to everyone. Detailed comparisons, section times, gait behavior, and consumption traces are available to players who enjoy analysis.

The injury, recovery, and permanent-loss model requires a separate decision. This handbook does not quietly promise either invulnerable collectibles or mandatory loss of beloved racers.

## 15. A sandbox with several routes to success

A circuit victory can make a donor line fashionable. A breeding breakthrough can attract a champion’s attention. A field discovery can turn a novice into a supplier. A spectacular track can make a previously unremarkable settlement a destination.

Those connections should exist in the world’s actual characters and economy. Racers buy, breeders supply, shops acquire and sell stock, and clubs stage events. A change in racing fashions should change what shops seek; a newly available donor line should appear in other builders’ projects. These relationships have observable consequences.

**The casual racer** buys a coordinated kit, enjoys local events, and upgrades whenever something appealing appears.

**The collector** pursues unusual hosts, donor forms, signature kits, colors, provenance, and impressive displays.

**The field enthusiast** learns habitats, finds valuable insects, and builds a reputation for discoveries.

**The breeder or Wrangloid** develops stock, fulfills orders, establishes lines, and earns a living within the hobby.

**The builder** experiments with graft arrangements, distinctive silhouettes, mechanical ideas, and presentation.

**The competitive lanista** develops a stable, studies opponents, wins circuits, and pursues the world Derby.

**The trackmaker or host** builds places other hobbyists want to visit and race.

These are interests and livelihoods, not exclusive character classes. A single player can move between them freely. Routine care, recordkeeping, and familiar assembly tasks can be streamlined as involvement grows, while detailed control remains available.

The reward cycle can begin anywhere: a shop visit, a race, a capture, a hatch, a trade, a new construction, or a rival’s boast. Each can produce something desirable and a reason to pursue the next project.

The ambition is a world where somebody says, “My roach won the race,” and everyone nearby wants to see it—then asks what they built it from, where they found those parts, and whether any of that stock is for sale.
