# RAD ROACH RACING — WORLD-CONSTRUCTION DOSSIER
## Agent reference layer 0.1 • 9 September 2026

**Purpose:** supply the contextual machinery behind an established racing hobby: products, specimens, institutions, markets, history, people, places, race administration, culture and consequences. This supplements the v0.2 handbook; it does not replace its component catalogue.

The packet contains primary-source observations, author-established premises, working handbook proposals, explicit unresolved decisions and a connected fictional reference district. Numerical gameplay balance, actual graft viability and simulated economic behavior have not been established.

## 1. Separate four kinds of knowledge

| Record type | Authority | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Author canon | Governs RRR | Anyone can catch and breed insects; a Wrangloid turns expertise into a livelihood. |
| Source-world evidence | Describes its stated source only | Tamiya publishes organizer resources for physical Mini 4WD events. |
| Working or new proposal | Usable design branch, not automatically canon | Saltstep No. 4 is a particular breeder's commercial hopper line. |
| In-world claim | Something an inhabitant says or believes | A customer claims every offspring of a winning donor line will be equally good. |

A widely repeated claim is not automatically true. An authoritative statement about Tamiya is not automatically a law of RRR. A polished paragraph in an earlier generated handbook is not proof the author independently approved that mechanic.

For each assertion, preserve its scope, source, revision, dependencies and truth status. Keep an unknown as an unknown, rather than turning a missing value into zero, impossible or forbidden.

**Chronology anchor:** the sport's thirty-year development follows the founding rivalry. The apocalypse date remains unspecified. These are different clocks.

These sources establish the existence of activities and institutions. They do not prove which psychological mechanism makes the hobby compelling or provide a forecast for RRR's market.

### Source boundaries that prevent bad translations

Tamiya's physical cars use fixed gearing, rolling contact and course guidance; the relationships between their parts are not identical to walking, gripping insects. [S02] Preserve the contribution and the tuning decision while developing the creature-specific behavior.

Likewise, the real event label **Open** is not permission for unrestricted hardware: participant categories and equipment regulations are different dimensions. [S07, S14] RRR's Open identity comes from the author.

## 3. Translate the product industry, not only the product

A living hobby still needs things that ordinary people can recognize, request, compare and purchase again. Its novelty is that products and organisms are related but distinct.

**Donor form → breeding line → brood lot → individual specimen → prepared sect → installed build → race history.**

Each step changes what exists and what can honestly be promised.

A **Switchyard hopper** is a fictional donor form already proposed in v0.2. **Saltstep No. 4** is a new proposed line selected by one Wrangloid. A brood lot is a particular production cohort. **H17** is one animal. Its prepared hind-leg pair becomes a particular graft instance. Installed on **Second Breakfast**, it belongs to that roach's build version. A published victory belongs to that entrant and configuration, not automatically to every descendant of the donor line.

A **kit** is a coordinated recipe plus the physical stock included in one sale. It can be supplied repeatedly without pretending every living specimen is identical. Preparation and selection can deliver a published range of fit and performance. A beginner chooses the kit; an expert compares its individual components.

### Product categories for RRR

| Category | What is offered | Desire and world consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Everyday live stock | Ordinary usable insects | Affordable participation, experimentation and repeat visits. |
| Selected donor line | Stock bred toward useful traits | Performance improvement and confidence in consistency. |
| Breeding stock | Animals valued for future descendants | Longer projects, pedigree records and commercial opportunity. |
| Prepared sect | An actual ready-to-fit component | Convenience, known geometry and less preparation work. |
| Coordinated kit | Host and matched components with a guide | A comprehensible first purchase and a branded build identity. |
| Signature edition | A presentation or recipe associated with a notable builder | Aspirational ownership, recognizable silhouettes and collectible packaging. |
| Historic revival | Reconstructed older kit or preserved line | Nostalgia, restoration projects and debates over authenticity. |
| Display specimen | An insect valued for appearance or provenance | A collector market that can overlap performance without requiring it. |

A line's name, a kit's branding and a host's personal name should remain distinct. Someone can own a Last Laugh kit-built racer named Second Breakfast, fitted with Saltstep stock, without those becoming three contradictory species names.

## 4. Living inventory needs a lifecycle

The same biological object can be valuable in several ways, but it cannot occupy contradictory physical states at once.

| State or operation | Record the consequences |
|---|---|
| Field capture | Origin, provisional identification, stage, observed anatomy and owner. |
| Observation or testing | What was measured, under which conditions, and what remains unknown. |
| Breeding | Parentage evidence, new offspring identities and declared developmental model. |
| Selection | Which specimens are retained, offered whole or prepared for parts. |
| Preparation | Which anatomy leaves the donor, what the graft contains and what remains. |
| Sale | Actual ownership transfer, condition, buyer-visible claims and provenance. |
| Installation | Host identity, graft identity, placement and build version. |
| Removal or damage | Changed condition and availability, according to the chosen biological model. |

The v0.2 rule that acquired grafts are not automatically inherited is a working proposal. Universal assimilation establishes graft compatibility, not universal crossbreeding or copying completed racers into offspring.

### Life stage can generate genuinely different products

In the researched *Issus* mechanism, nymphal hind-leg gears synchronize movement and disappear at the final molt into adulthood. [S13] This supports an important design opportunity: a donor's useful anatomy may depend on stage, rather than every stage being a stronger version of the previous one.

A proposed **Clockleaf planthopper** could therefore supply a paired synchronizer as a juvenile, with a different adult role. Its breeder might retain adults as parents while raising juvenile donor stock. The graft application is fictional; the research does not demonstrate a transplanted gearbox.

Every new donor record should specify appearance, habitat, stage, field clues, useful anatomy, selection traits, care needs, preparation options and what changes when it matures. An impressive name and a stat bonus are insufficient contextual data.

## 5. The Wrangloid economy

A Wrangloid can sell what anyone is allowed to catch because expertise, selection, reliable availability, breeding records, preparation, enclosure capacity and transport all have value. Their knowledge does not require an exclusive profession lock.

Separate a market's **asking price**, **actual transactions**, **measured performance**, **appearance**, **pedigree evidence**, **availability**, **condition**, and **buyer purpose**. The same insect can be a mediocre race donor and a desirable breeder or collector specimen.

### Candidate value model

Use a multidimensional description rather than a universal rarity-to-price conversion:

`buyer value = f(usable traits, condition, breeding evidence, scarcity, appearance, provenance, convenience, buyer goals)`

This is an architectural statement, not a calibrated economic formula. Different buyers assign different importance to those inputs; transactions additionally depend on funds and available supply.

### Where continuing demand comes from

Improved builds, new donor lines, additional racers, alternate classes, kits, display projects, gifts, experimental grafts, local events, commissions and expansion of breeding facilities can all create demand. Repairs may contribute once the injury model is decided. Forced permanent death is not required to make commerce relevant.

A winner can create interest without proving the donor line caused the win. A shop can announce a future brood without manufacturing immediate adults. A broker can specialize in rare forms without being a better breeder than the neighborhood Wrangloid.

Keep the physical economy understandable. If a merchant prepares an insect's hind legs, it cannot remain for sale elsewhere as the same intact insect. If the merchant acquires another specimen, that is another identity and acquisition record.

### A commercial ladder that does not become a player-class ladder

Home hobbyist → reliable supplier → specialist Wrangloid → recognized breeder line → regional wholesaler or signature producer is a possible career story. An individual can also stay happily at the first stage. Racing success, breeding expertise, wealth, fame and collector reputation need not rise together.

## 6. Geography must produce more than differently colored opponents

Build a settlement from its ordinary livelihood, resources and connections, then show how its hobby develops from them. A town with protected warm water can support a different donor supply from a windy freight interchange. That is proposed fictional ecology, not a claim that fallout inevitably produces particular insects.

Three connected sample places demonstrate useful differences:

**Tinwater Junction** maintains pumps and supports carriers. Its shops favor dependable stock and practical kits. The Foundry Club is a communal venue with benches, stored sections and a display shelf. Interiors can be clean and carefully maintained even when the street is worn.

**Cinderhouse** operates heat-recovery workshops and a cooperative brood business. Its breeders are known for strong support plates and sturdy transport cases. Its engineers are a market presence, not a rule forcing every resident to build heavy racers.

**Saltglass Exchange** is a packing, brokerage and display center. Its traders handle striking specimens, provenance-led resales and long-distance commissions. It imports ordinary stock as well; a collector economy does not consist entirely of rare trophies.

These are proposed reference locations, not a fixed world map, designated starting area or replacement for New World City.

### Factions need institutional differences

Represent a faction through what it funds, values, controls and rewards. One might support public clubs, another subsidize breeding research, another sponsor traveling champions. Their resource bases and public cultures can create recognizable tendencies without imposing species exclusivity or identical personalities.

For each faction, specify why winning matters, how representatives are selected, who pays for travel, which records it recognizes and what happens to a successful returning lanista. Shared race recognition can be narrower than diplomatic friendship.

## 7. A thirty-year sport needs a usable past

Only the founding progression and thirty-year interval are established. The internal eras below are proposals for an agent's historical scaffolding.

| Relative sport years | Proposed development | Surviving evidence in the present |
|---|---|---|
| Year 0 | Horn, pincers and hopper rivalry | Competing oral versions, claimed relics, founder-style builds. |
| Years 1–5 | Neighborhood imitation and informal meets | Unusual homemade fittings, inherited notebooks, arguments about the first local champion. |
| Years 6–12 | Reliable preparation and commercial breeding | First recognizable kit families, old shop tins, breeder seals and copied assembly sheets. |
| Years 13–20 | Inter-settlement events and agreed race records | Traveling trophies, course plans, result books and disputes over standards. |
| Years 21–29 | Faction campaigns and a mature commercial circuit | Sponsor colors, professional workshops, signature lines and famous rivalries. |
| Present, roughly Year 30 | World Derby prestige and many established hobby cultures | Retired champions, nostalgic adults, newcomers and businesses with differing histories. |

Not every settlement adopts the hobby on the same year. A recently connected town can be enjoying its first commercial kits while World City celebrates a long-standing rivalry.

Historic editions need provenance: original specimen, descendant line, authentic old packaging, restored build or modern reproduction are different claims. A museum piece should not become genuine because a seller calls it genuine.

## 8. Ordinary culture gives the sport texture

A mature hobby has recognizable objects, habits and occasions. In RRR these can be directly useful to both story generation and gameplay.

**Assembly night:** a beginner arrives with a tin; someone lends a tool; a regular shows off a self-caught donor. The shop gains a customer, the newcomer gains acquaintances and a local rivalry can begin without a formal quest board.

**Brood announcement:** a breeder posts the next selected batch. Buyers ask for racers, breeders, a particular appearance, or a specimen matching an existing build. Reserved stock and general stock are visibly different.

**Course reveal:** a club posts a plan assembled from its available pieces. People argue about the long straight and bring different builds. A historic-layout evening lets older racers recount a famous result.

**Display evening:** a beautiful build earns attention without requiring a championship. Its creator may become known for shell finish, graft integration or an outrageous silhouette.

**Race issue:** a publication records winners, shows builds, prints advertisements and corrects a mistaken pedigree claim. It is both a source of information and a collectible record.

### Ready-to-use fictional artifacts

> **KIT LID:** LAST LAUGH — CLUB HOPPER TIN. Host and selected hind-leg pair enclosed. Build it yourself. Give it a better name.

> **TANK CARD:** SWITCHYARD HOPPER H19. Field capture. Long-legged appearance; lineage unrecorded. Demonstration pending.

> **WORKBENCH ADVICE:** “Keep the pair together until you know why you're splitting them.”

> **RESULTS BOX:** SECOND BREAKFAST TAKES THE DASH. Ivo Pell wins with a shop-bought hopper conversion. Two Nets is taking inquiries for the next selected brood.

> **DISPLAY INVITATION:** FINISH, FIT AND FLAIR. Bring the roach, the tin, or the work in progress. Race trophies welcome; not required.

> **CORRECTION:** Last issue identified a prepared Saltstep set as a wild capture. The donor was bred at Two Nets. The owner's field-caught specimen is a separate animal.

Prefer vocabulary that grows out of actual practices. “Brood drop,” “tank-tested,” “tin build” and “fieldstock” are candidate hobby terms because each points to something people do. Do not turn every ordinary noun into a mutant pun.

## 9. Race administration is part of the fictional world

A recognizable sport needs an understandable answer to who entered, what they brought, which course they completed and why the result counts. Combat freedom can coexist with those answers.

Store these dimensions separately:

| Dimension | RRR data |
|---|---|
| Locomotion division | Flyers with flyers; define the treatment of sustained flight, hops and mixed assemblies explicitly. |
| Equipment policy | Open, Speedsters, Fireworks or a particular club's stated format. |
| Competitive tier | Local, Regional, Factional or world championship. |
| Participation | Registration, capacity and any declared newcomer or kit event rules. |
| Format | Heat, time trial, championship race or a future proposed format. |
| Course | Versioned geometry, gates, surfaces, lap requirements and legal paths. |
| Start and finish | Start procedure, valid completion, ordering and tie procedure. |
| Contact and recovery | Allowed actions and explicit handling of an incapacitated or displaced racer. |
| Inspection and disputes | Conditions actually being checked, witnesses and the procedure for objections. |
| Qualification | Which result advances whom and to which later event. |

The user-defined championship route remains Locals → Regionals → Factionals → faction representation at New World City. A local social race is not automatically a qualifier unless its event record says so.

Winner identity, host identity and winning build version must be preserved separately. A famous roach can change owners or parts while retaining a career; a reproduction of its winning recipe is a different object.

## 10. Modular course data must describe an actual construction

A named list of exciting sections is a layout concept. To reconstruct it as a modular course, an agent needs more precise data.

**Per piece:** entrance and exit frames, dimensions, legal travel paths, support points, joining geometry, surface, collision geometry, elevation, wall/ceiling treatment and visual identity.

**Per layout:** physical piece instances, positions and rotations, directed connections, lane mappings, alternative routes, checkpoint sequence, footprint, measured distance, support inventory and version.

**Per event:** which routes are legal for its locomotion division, laps, contact policy, start area, finish gate, retrieval policy and declared conditions.

The Tamiya layout sheet's one-set example is labeled approximately 3.1 by 1.4 metres with roughly 20 metres of travel across three laps. [S11] It demonstrates why physical footprint, race distance and number of laps must be different fields. Those dimensions are source-world data, not recommended RRR dimensions.

A built course is a directed route graph embedded in physical space. Graph connectivity alone does not establish that the joints physically align; a beautiful rendering alone does not establish that the course can be built from its piece inventory.

Players can own, lend, combine, repair and replace sections. Those ownership relationships create communal clubs and traveling meets rather than making every course a world object conjured by a menu.

## 11. The host introduces another design layer: animal agency

A roach needs a fictional account of how it starts, follows a route, responds to obstacles and uses its anatomy. This affects training, senses, handling and live input. It should be developed explicitly, not smuggled in as a small control detail.

Separate **native disposition**, **learned behavior**, **pre-race settings**, **sensory capability**, **momentary state**, and **player intervention**. Only implement distinctions that meaningfully affect play, but know which concept a behavior represents.

For example, a creature can be mechanically quick but hesitate at an unfamiliar surface. That might be learned experience, an observable sensory limitation or a temporary condition; it should not be an arbitrary hidden failure roll presented under whichever explanation fits afterward.

A beginner's kit can supply a dependable host and usable race plan. Advanced breeders or trainers could pursue particular behaviors if that system is adopted. Neither intelligence levels nor command-token counts are fixed by the existing concept.

Likewise, human attitudes toward racers are world data. A roach can be a beloved individual, prized breeding asset and competition entrant. The final injury and recovery model must support the intended mix of attachment, violent spectacle and accessible hobby participation.

## 12. Connected reference scenario

The packet supplies named records so an agent can trace consequences without inventing unrelated explanations each time.

**Jess Rill**, a pump attendant, finds **H19** at the **Switchyard Verge**. The unusual stripe and long-looking legs are observable. Exact performance and parentage are unknown. Jess is allowed to keep, trade or investigate it without becoming a licensed Wrangloid.

**Mara Venn** at **Two Nets Live Stock** keeps her selected **Saltstep No. 4** line. She prepares a different animal, **H17**, into a matched leg set. H17's record changes; it is no longer an unchanged whole insect on the shelf. The retained breeding animals remain separate identities.

**Ivo Pell** buys the prepared set and builds it onto his roach **Second Breakfast**. Ivo does not maintain donor tanks. His build and its provenance are still fully part of the same living hobby.

At the **Foundry Dash**, Second Breakfast wins in this illustrative narrative. This is an authored scenario, not a simulated performance claim. The event records the build version and result.

**Nera Kest**, editor of **Benchline**, verifies and publishes the result. **The Case Runners** carry the next issue to **Saltglass Exchange**. Interested buyers ask Two Nets about future stock. A distant breeder who has not received the news does not magically react.

Someone claims that every Saltstep offspring must now be a champion. The world records that as gossip, not established genetics. Mara may use the success in an advertisement while accurately describing the limits of her breeding evidence.

This one chain supplies exploration, collection, retail, assembly, racing, status, publishing, trade and a potential disagreement. Each activity has its own immediate attraction, and the relationships give it consequences elsewhere.

## 13. The records an agent should retrieve

| Entity | Necessary contextual fields |
|---|---|
| Place | Ordinary livelihood, food/water/energy, habitat, trade, clubs and local knowledge. |
| Person | Livelihood, goals, expertise, resources, relationships, possessions and known claims. |
| Donor form | Appearance, habitat, stages, signs, useful anatomy, breeding traits and care. |
| Lineage | Founder evidence, breeder, selection goals, history, measured variation and availability. |
| Specimen | Identity, owner, origin, stage, anatomy, condition, observations and unknowns. |
| Graft design / instance | Function and preparation recipe versus actual donor, condition and ownership. |
| Host / build | Persistent animal versus a particular component configuration and appearance. |
| Kit / offer | Reproducible assembly recipe versus actual stock, claims, terms and asking price. |
| Venue / layout | Facilities and physical sections versus their current arrangement. |
| Ruleset / event / result | Declared rules, particular meeting and immutable classification record. |
| Organization | Purpose, resources, members, suppliers, sport role and political limits. |
| Publication / claim | Publisher and audience versus what is said, supported, corrected or still uncertain. |

Use stable identifiers, not names as globally unique keys. A seller can rename a kit; the same individual should not lose its history. A brood-line label can be copied dishonestly; the copy should not inherit authentic provenance.

For knowledge-rich generation, retrieve a small connected neighborhood of these records. A shop scene needs the actual stock and people in that shop, not every species in the world. A new donor design needs its environment and biological role before its sale description.

## 14. Decisions that propagate across the world

| Unsettled decision | Why it matters | Current working direction |
|---|---|---|
| Post-winter survival and food web | Donor existence, ordinary life and trade require a coherent background. | Preserve roach survival; explicitly resolve other surviving or returning life without inserting an unapproved origin story. |
| Absolute scale | Cases, tools, tracks, viewpoints and enclosure density depend on it. | Hand-carried hobby scale is a v0.2 visual proposal; numeric scale remains open. |
| Assimilation and preparation timing | Determines spontaneous field grafts, kit convenience and how often people rebuild. | Same underlying mutation for raw and shop-prepared sects. |
| Storage and removal | Determines collecting, used parts and reversible building. | Track states distinctly; choose viability rules before balancing supply. |
| Inheritance | Controls the difference between a bloodline and a copied complete build. | v0.2 acquired-graft separation remains a proposal. |
| Life stages | Influences what is caught, bred and prepared. | Design per donor rather than a universal maturity ladder. |
| Injury, aging and loss | Shapes attachment, spectacle, repairs and replacement demand. | No global permanent-death or invulnerability promise is assumed. |
| Agency and live input | Shapes training, senses, observation and race control. | Explicit design decision; no preselected token system. |
| World time | Connects breeding, trips, markets and the season. | Distinguish world time from active-play time and offline progression. |
| Communications and transport | Governs availability, fame and the spread of techniques. | Candidate local handbills, carriers and uneven regional broadcasts. |
| Faction jurisdiction | Gives representation and recognized victories real meaning. | Sporting recognition need not imply political unity. |
| Rule boundaries | Keeps Open freedom compatible with meaningful racing. | Separate event and course validity from build creativity. |

An unresolved field is not a reason to stop producing unrelated content. Work under a named provisional branch, keep the dependencies visible and avoid claiming that branch was already author-approved.

## 15. Quality checks for generated world content

A novice must be able to buy a kit, assemble it, race and win without becoming a breeder. A field specialist must be able to discover something excellent before becoming a champion. A beautiful collectible need not be a poor racer, and an improved component need not be assigned a contrived drawback.

A donor preparation must conserve object identity and update anatomy. A published win must identify the correct host and configuration. A seller's boast must not become verified world truth simply because it is vividly written. A source-world restriction must not silently become an RRR restriction.

A new shop should connect to suppliers, customers, livelihoods and some local social practice. A new donor should connect habitat, appearance, behavior, useful anatomy, breeding and a reason someone desires it. A new course should have a buildable layout and a reason people gather there. A faction should have institutions and individuals, not only a palette and stat preference.

A large world can be maintained with aggregate distant markets and more detailed nearby actors, but it should preserve important ownership, provenance and result records. That is a proposed implementation approach, not a requirement to simulate every distant insect every frame.

**The desired result is continuity:** an agent can follow a specimen from its habitat to a shop, a workbench, a race and a story people repeat afterward—and each step still refers to the same world.
