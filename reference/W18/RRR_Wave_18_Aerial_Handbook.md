## 1. Translate the working decisions, not a car-shaped silhouette

SEGA supplies the local acquisition/build/rivalry reference; Tamiya supplies distinct physical component and modular-course functions. Powered flight, directed gland offense and self-righting are RRR additions, not features retroactively attributed to a source menu. [W18.S01](RRR_Wave_18_Research.md#w18-s01) · [W18.S02](RRR_Wave_18_Research.md#w18-s02) · [W18.S03](RRR_Wave_18_Research.md#w18-s03) · [W18.S04](RRR_Wave_18_Research.md#w18-s04) · [W18.S05](RRR_Wave_18_Research.md#w18-s05)

| Source function | RRR subject | Decision retained |
|---|---|---|
| Body / chosen vehicle | The same persistent host and native plan | Loaded mass, proportions, native articulation and the space available for roots. A flight conversion does not create a different animal. |
| Frame | Native support plus deliberately arranged thoracic/root bracing | Transmit wing-root and contact loads without binding the required motion. Support is not general armor. |
| Motor | Flight-configured drive musculature | Supply useful work at a cadence and force the wing transmission can use. A wing outline is not an extra motor. |
| Gears | Wingstroke yoke: leverage and coordination declared separately | Choose stroke travel/force and coordinate the working surfaces. A phase adjustment is not a source of power. |
| Battery | Actual reserve and delivery interfaces | Carry usable energy, meet required delivery and account for fill and changing mass. High capacity cannot bypass a restrictive outlet. |
| Tires | Launch and landing contact preparations | Feet transfer work and regain purchase on actual surfaces. Airborne traction is not invented to keep a familiar stat relevant everywhere. |
| Side stabilizer | Native sensing, optional Gyropods and real control authority | Detect a disturbance and use available wing, limb or body action. A sensor supplies neither lift nor a support surface. |
| Suspension | Receiving limbs and landing couplers | Accommodate touchdown and restore running/perching. They do not soften arbitrary mid-air collisions globally. |
| Front wing | Browfoils, vanes and passive trim surfaces | Alter aerodynamic loading and attitude through actual air interaction. They are not a complete powered-flight array. |
| Appearance | Natural pattern, shell finish, breeder and maker identifiers | Keep the same host and grafts recognizable in a case and in motion. An edition can be desirable without a performance buff. |
| Rollers / stays — physical-hobby extension | Retained native shell or Railshoes on supported ground transitions | Actual rail contact only; an airborne gate is not a magnetic wall-following guide. |
| Brakes / dampers — physical-hobby extension | Ground-contact brakes and landing/rebound preparations | Ventral material acts on a reached surface; aerial slowing requires an aerodynamic or propulsive change. A Keel addresses its relevant oscillation, not all disturbances. |
| Modular course — physical-hobby extension | Owned frames, supports, portals, decks and declared route volumes | Construct real geometry with usable transitions; route lines on a drawing are not physical rails. |
| RRR-native extensions | Powered arrays, Kickback, Boiler and related prepared anatomy | Retain the original derivative sport. These need no fabricated PCR menu equivalents to justify their existence. |

**Flight changes the value of existing parts without changing their meaning.** Feet work during ground contact. Sensing requires a response. A reservoir supplies its actual contents. A vane interacts with air. The wearer remains the same living host. The dedicated wing array is additional anatomy, not a relabeling of the source game’s front-aero slot.

## 3. The complete aerial and special-action construction

<a id="w18-system-flight"></a>

### A Flyer is a complete working construction

`W18.SYSTEM.FLIGHT`

**Problem.** A wing could otherwise become a flight-permission item whose advertised agility, lift and endurance come from hidden bonuses.

**Definition.** The actual host, available work, wing-root delivery, deforming surfaces, support, supply and control jointly determine sustained flight. Native ground functions remain present unless explicitly replaced. The complete entry kit solves an ordinary working arrangement without asking a novice to invent a flight-control system.

**Earlier reference.** [W14.ANATOMY.JOINS](reference/W17/reference/W16/reference/W15/reference/RRR_Wave_14_Foundations.md#w14-anatomy-joins) · [W14.PART.QUADWING](reference/W17/reference/W16/reference/W15/reference/RRR_Wave_14_Foundations.md#w14-part-quadwing) · [W16.DONOR.GLASSWING](reference/W17/reference/W16/RRR_Wave_16_Foundations.md#w16-donor-glasswing) · [W15.ENTRY.01](reference/W17/reference/W16/reference/W15/RRR_Wave_15_Foundations.md#w15-entry-01)

**Research.** [W18.S02](RRR_Wave_18_Research.md#w18-s02) · [W18.S03](RRR_Wave_18_Research.md#w18-s03) · [W18.S06](RRR_Wave_18_Research.md#w18-s06) · [W18.S07](RRR_Wave_18_Research.md#w18-s07)

<a id="w18-flight-01"></a>

**W18.FLIGHT.01 — A powered-flight array receives energy and control through declared interfaces. There is no flight stat that overrides an unpowered, obstructed or unsupported array.**

<a id="w18-flight-02"></a>

**W18.FLIGHT.02 — A Longwing reference array contains forewings and fan hindwings with distinct roles; a Quadwing contains four individually situated working wings. Neither name is shorthand for four independent copies of the same force.**

<a id="w18-flight-03"></a>

**W18.FLIGHT.03 — Loaded shape, sweep and interaction matter. Increasing span, stiffness, beat cadence or phase alignment is not unconditionally beneficial; genuine multi-property improvements remain allowed.**

<a id="w18-flight-04"></a>

**W18.FLIGHT.04 — Native contact and articulation remain usable for launch, perching and touchdown. They need not be replaced simply to populate a complete equipment menu.**

<a id="w18-flight-05"></a>

**W18.FLIGHT.05 — Sensing can support earlier correction only if the construction has a movable, powered or suitably passive response. A brace against air cannot substitute for a wing-control action.**

<a id="w18-flight-06"></a>

**W18.FLIGHT.06 — The kit guide identifies supplied and retained-native functions, starting reserve and ordinary first-use support. Complete means no hidden necessary organ purchase before the advertised introductory course.**

<a id="w18-flight-07"></a>

**W18.FLIGHT.07 — A bench observation, complete-array trial and actual race answer different questions. The reference may promise a supported operating range, not victory against every competitor.**

**Worked interpretation.** A buyer opens a Skyhook Courtyard tin containing a conditioned Quadwing, selected flight drive, yoke, appropriate root support and reserve. They fit the actual assemblies, set the guide’s ordinary trim, name the same host and enter a broad portal loop. Owning a pond, a sensory laboratory or a champion pedigree is not required.

**Boundary.** Exact aerodynamic scales and flight parameters remain a later calibration task. This is a reference construction, not an implemented flight simulator.

<a id="w18-system-delivery"></a>

### Drive, yoke and control each have a job

`W18.SYSTEM.DELIVERY`

**Problem.** A familiar “gear” label could conflate muscle cadence, wingbeat, stroke leverage, timing and the host’s ability to command an adjustment.

**Definition.** A flight drive supplies work. A yoke routes it through a specified range of motion. Coordination sets relationships between working surfaces; control changes a permitted relationship at the permitted time using actual anatomy. A complex assembly can contain several roles without being counted several times.

**Earlier reference.** [W14.MAP.GAIT](reference/W17/reference/W16/reference/W15/reference/RRR_Wave_14_Foundations.md#w14-map-gait) · [W17.ASSEMBLY.04](reference/W17/RRR_Wave_17_Foundations.md#w17-assembly-04) · [W15.BENCH.05](reference/W17/reference/W16/reference/W15/RRR_Wave_15_Foundations.md#w15-bench-05)

**Research.** [W18.S02](RRR_Wave_18_Research.md#w18-s02) · [W18.S07](RRR_Wave_18_Research.md#w18-s07)

<a id="w18-delivery-01"></a>

**W18.DELIVERY.01 — The flight-drive preparation identifies its output junction and duty. An efficient donor supplies a useful possibility; insufficient reserve delivery still limits the installed system.**

<a id="w18-delivery-02"></a>

**W18.DELIVERY.02 — Yoke leverage states input and output motion, working force and travel. It is an operating range, not a higher-number-is-better gene.**

<a id="w18-delivery-03"></a>

**W18.DELIVERY.03 — Crossphase coordination changes fore/hind timing where the array permits it. More synchronization is not a universal aerodynamic upgrade: a useful phase depends on duty and the complete array.**

<a id="w18-delivery-04"></a>

**W18.DELIVERY.04 — A manually adjusted yoke remains at its fitted setting during the race unless an actual adjustable mechanism and controller are included. A slider in the guide does not create free active transmission.**

<a id="w18-delivery-05"></a>

**W18.DELIVERY.05 — Ground and aerial work can share supply. Separate drive bundles, a routed branch or retained native ground muscle must be declared; no second fully tuned drivetrain appears from a single unqualified input.**

<a id="w18-delivery-06"></a>

**W18.DELIVERY.06 — A root bridge transmits load and preserves permitted motion. Bracing every hinge rigidly can defeat control even while increasing static stiffness.**

<a id="w18-delivery-07"></a>

**W18.DELIVERY.07 — Control information does not copy the donor’s whole behavior or sensory brain. The integrated host remains the actor; acquired wings do not know a new course by species name.**

**Worked interpretation.** A Longwing with adequate muscle work but a poorly matched yoke is not cured by adding another reservoir. A fitter first checks whether the desired stroke is reached without binding, then compares the revised setting under the same supply and load.

**Boundary.** These are functional interfaces. They do not mandate a detailed gene solver, an input on every wingbeat, or a specific electronics-like controller architecture.

<a id="w18-system-air"></a>

### Energy, airflow and useful flight modes

`W18.SYSTEM.AIR`

**Problem.** Course speed could be confused with air-relative work; a tailwind, glide or hovering pose could become free propulsion.

**Definition.** The public reference distinguishes how quickly a racer progresses past course landmarks from how its surfaces move relative to the air. Wind is an external condition with direction, not a regional buff. Climbing, maneuvering and sustained support use the actual construction and supplies.

**Earlier reference.** [W14.ANATOMY.RACE_READINGS](reference/W17/reference/W16/reference/W15/reference/RRR_Wave_14_Foundations.md#w14-anatomy-race_readings) · [W15.RACING.07](reference/W17/reference/W16/reference/W15/RRR_Wave_15_Foundations.md#w15-racing-07)

**Research.** [W18.S16](RRR_Wave_18_Research.md#w18-s16)

<a id="w18-air-01"></a>

**W18.AIR.01 — Airspeed and course-relative speed are separate. A fast downwind pass cannot alone certify high inherited drive or wing efficiency.**

<a id="w18-air-02"></a>

**W18.AIR.02 — Hover, controlled slow flight, cruise, climb and glide are capabilities of a tested complete construction, not automatic rights granted by a wing family.**

<a id="w18-air-03"></a>

**W18.AIR.03 — Gliding can trade existing motion or height against travel; it does not replenish fuel or provide indefinite support. Work needed to gain the prior height remains accounted for.**

<a id="w18-air-04"></a>

**W18.AIR.04 — Slowing in the air requires appropriate force or resistance. A ventral pad that touches nothing cannot cause aerial braking, and lower drag is not the only possible useful response.**

<a id="w18-air-05"></a>

**W18.AIR.05 — An announced airflow source or natural condition has location, direction, variability and an event operating allowance. It never quietly favors a named lineage or changes after reading hidden player genes.**

<a id="w18-air-06"></a>

**W18.AIR.06 — A material wind change can trigger the published reinspection, pause or cancellation rule for everyone affected. Disclosure and comparable opportunity are required; exact turbulence reconstruction is not imposed on the book.**

<a id="w18-air-07"></a>

**W18.AIR.07 — Supply forecasts distinguish usable contents, maximum delivery and planned duty. A temporary drop in demand is not an additional energy source, and a gauge cannot promise an unobserved future race outcome.**

**Worked interpretation.** On the Reed Sweep course, a Longwing can carry pace around a broad outer arc while a more maneuverable Quadwing chooses a shorter but more demanding turn. In a following airflow the first may look faster relative to the ground without proving a superior motor; both still need the required next gate.

**Boundary.** No aircraft coefficient or weather simulation is transplanted into the insect scale. The statement fixes meaning before numerical modeling.

<a id="w18-system-sport"></a>

### Flyers with Flyers, without making flight an equipment loophole

`W18.SYSTEM.SPORT`

**Problem.** Wings can be used as decoration, passive aero, active hopping aids or sustained flight. Names alone cannot determine a fair locomotion division.

**Definition.** Classify what the ready installed construction can actually do. Aerial competition is parallel to ground competition; Open, Speedsters and Fireworks describe equipment policy within an appropriate locomotion field, not a sequence of superior species.

**Earlier reference.** [W14.SPORT.BOUNDARIES](reference/W17/reference/W16/reference/W15/reference/RRR_Wave_14_Foundations.md#w14-sport-boundaries) · [W15.SPORT.05](reference/W17/reference/W16/reference/W15/RRR_Wave_15_Foundations.md#w15-sport-05) · [W14.SPORT.LADDER](reference/W17/reference/W16/reference/W15/reference/RRR_Wave_14_Foundations.md#w14-sport-ladder)

**Research.** [W18.S05](RRR_Wave_18_Research.md#w18-s05)

<a id="w18-sport-01"></a>

**W18.SPORT.01 — Repeated powered aerodynamic support or propulsion independent of ground contact places an enabled construction in the Flyer division. A roach cannot enter ground racing by promising to flap only once.**

<a id="w18-sport-02"></a>

**W18.SPORT.02 — A ground-division hop expends its supported launch and returns to a legal contact. Passive gliding is permitted only under the announced ground route/airborne-travel policy; it cannot erase required course progress.**

<a id="w18-sport-03"></a>

**W18.SPORT.03 — Flight organs physically disconnected or fixed as passive material can be evaluated by that actual state. A peaceful race-plan label or folded-looking animation is not deactivation.**

<a id="w18-sport-04"></a>

**W18.SPORT.04 — Flyer Speedsters exclude dedicated operative weapons, not every unusual-looking limb. Flyer Fireworks require working offense and light protection. Flyer Open has no universal part-point ceiling.**

<a id="w18-sport-05"></a>

**W18.SPORT.05 — Launch format, recontact rules, missed-gate treatment, between-round changes and finish conditions are public before the entry commitment. A complete Flyer does not automatically require a vertical launch or hover.**

<a id="w18-sport-06"></a>

**W18.SPORT.06 — Ground titles do not by themselves certify an aerial racer or skip its qualifying results. The Locals–Regionals–Factionals–New World City framework can be applied to each recognized division without merging their results.**

<a id="w18-sport-07"></a>

**W18.SPORT.07 — Accessible local Flyer practice and kit entry do not require an earlier ground championship or ownership of all source nurseries. Later qualification controls the relevant competition only.**

**Worked interpretation.** A Last Laugh roach that clears one competitor through a ballistic jump can remain in a suitable ground event. Adding a powered Longwing array changes its locomotion capability. Neither the same host identity nor the maker’s product label changes that distinction.

**Boundary.** Exact capability-test distances and times need later sporting calibration. The current qualitative boundary is not permission for officials to invent an undisclosed arbitrary threshold.

<a id="w18-system-recovery"></a>

### Recover the body without erasing the race

`W18.SYSTEM.RECOVERY`

**Problem.** Kickback can be trivialized as an instant reset or exaggerated as guaranteed self-righting from any posture.

**Definition.** A recovery is a physical sequence that uses actual support and clearance. Returning to a running stance does not return the lost distance, refill supplies, undo injury or move the racer to an earlier favorable position.

**Earlier reference.** [W14.ACTION.RIGHT](reference/W17/reference/W16/reference/W15/reference/RRR_Wave_14_Foundations.md#w14-action-right) · [W16.MATERIAL.ELASTIC](reference/W17/reference/W16/RRR_Wave_16_Foundations.md#w16-material-elastic) · [W17.AMEND.03](reference/W17/RRR_Wave_17_Amendments.md#w17-amend-03)

**Research.** [W18.S08](RRR_Wave_18_Research.md#w18-s08) · [W18.S09](RRR_Wave_18_Research.md#w18-s09)

<a id="w18-recovery-01"></a>

**W18.RECOVERY.01 — Native-leg righting remains a valid option when the host can do it. Kickback is a specialized alternative, not a purchased permission to recover.**

<a id="w18-recovery-02"></a>

**W18.RECOVERY.02 — The working sequence is support assessment, latch seating, loading, release, recontact and stabilization. Visible motion and available energy must agree.**

<a id="w18-recovery-03"></a>

**W18.RECOVERY.03 — Body support must permit the hinge to work. A rigid frame crossing its required flex region must be adapted through real geometry, not ignored during the recovery animation.**

<a id="w18-recovery-04"></a>

**W18.RECOVERY.04 — A greater snap can move the body farther without producing a better landing. Select useful recoil and control within the intended host/contact conditions.**

<a id="w18-recovery-05"></a>

**W18.RECOVERY.05 — A powerless, suspended or pinned body cannot invent a supporting surface. On an aerial catch deck, Kickback can be useful after actual contact; it does not re-ignite flight in midair.**

<a id="w18-recovery-06"></a>

**W18.RECOVERY.06 — Routine readiness and rest can be handled by the chosen preparation/behavior profile. No global per-use damage lottery or guaranteed no-damage rule is established.**

<a id="w18-recovery-07"></a>

**W18.RECOVERY.07 — A recovery that leaves the legal route follows the same re-entry rules as any other displacement. The organ never awards checkpoints or finish credit.**

**Worked interpretation.** A Broadback resting inverted on a low open platform can use a suitably installed hinge if its roots and support allow rotation. The same construction under a tight crossbeam can lack clearance. An exposed native leg or a different shell/brace arrangement may be the useful response instead.

**Boundary.** The study-derived latch sequence supports the mechanism description, not guaranteed recovery rates or an implemented articulated solver.

<a id="w18-system-discharge"></a>

### Boiler: a reachable pulse, not a remote attack score

`W18.SYSTEM.DISCHARGE`

**Problem.** A spray can be reduced to damage plus cooldown, losing its distinct material, mounting and positional decisions.

**Definition.** A Boiler has contained material, pulse generation, outlet articulation and an activation/recovery sequence. It affects material actually reached by the discharge under the adopted contact/damage model. On a Flyer the host’s posture, air motion and its own sweep envelope also affect use.

**Earlier reference.** [W14.PART.BOILER](reference/W17/reference/W16/reference/W15/reference/RRR_Wave_14_Foundations.md#w14-part-boiler) · [W16.MATERIAL.GLAND](reference/W17/reference/W16/RRR_Wave_16_Foundations.md#w16-material-gland) · [W14.SET.FIRE](reference/W17/reference/W16/reference/W15/reference/RRR_Wave_14_Foundations.md#w14-set-fire)

**Research.** [W18.S11](RRR_Wave_18_Research.md#w18-s11) · [W18.S12](RRR_Wave_18_Research.md#w18-s12)

<a id="w18-discharge-01"></a>

**W18.DISCHARGE.01 — Store fill, chamber readiness, outlet response and material replenishment are different states. Time after a pulse does not automatically create replacement contents.**

<a id="w18-discharge-02"></a>

**W18.DISCHARGE.02 — One burst can contain internal pulses; these are not separate free player attacks. A second outlet shares actual supply unless a second reservoir is physically included.**

<a id="w18-discharge-03"></a>

**W18.DISCHARGE.03 — The permitted discharge direction follows the mounted outlet’s range and host control. It does not inherit omnidirectional aiming regardless of surrounding limbs or wings.**

<a id="w18-discharge-04"></a>

**W18.DISCHARGE.04 — A pulse has a finite route and duration. A rival outside it receives no effect because the attack animation completed; an intersecting non-target can be exposed under the same stated rules.**

<a id="w18-discharge-05"></a>

**W18.DISCHARGE.05 — Mounts must be assessed against the host’s moving wing and limb envelope, not only a static inspection pose. A useful rear-facing ground pattern can be poorly placed on a Flyer.**

<a id="w18-discharge-06"></a>

**W18.DISCHARGE.06 — Closing the outlet stops further output; it does not delete an already emitted plume. A clean line or a restrained plan can be better than discharging into occupied air.**

<a id="w18-discharge-07"></a>

**W18.DISCHARGE.07 — Ordinary Fireworks play can use a ready supplied gland and refill support. The donor’s parasitoid lifecycle and specialist receiving test remain optional supplier work.**

**Worked interpretation.** An Airflare flyer gives up a crowded inside line rather than fire through its own hindwing sweep. On the next straight a real rearward encounter permits a short deterrent pulse. The maneuver still aims to improve finishing position, not earn attack-score points.

**Boundary.** No chemical recipe, fixed damage value, lethal effect or broadly approved hazardous-transport standard is specified. Fictional local contact consequences remain separate from global injury and loss policy.

## 4. Eight complete-assembly reference sheets

These sheets clarify existing arrays and preparations, plus the exact yoke and support patterns needed by an aerial kit. They are not eight new mandatory equipment slots. Native support can remain; included roots, couplers and material are counted once.

<a id="w18-prep-longwing"></a>

### Locust Longwing / complete array

`W18.PREP.LONGWING`

**Role.** Powered surfaces and articulated roots

**Source and related entries.** [W18.DONOR.LONGWING](RRR_Wave_18_Foundations.md#w18-donor-longwing) · [W14.PART.LONGWING](reference/W17/reference/W16/reference/W15/reference/RRR_Wave_14_Foundations.md#w14-part-longwing)

**Supplied anatomy.** Two narrow forewings and two folding fan hindwings, their preserved useful roots and the specified attachment interfaces. The structural pairing is left/right, with fore/hind identity retained. A surface-only pack states that it excludes the living root and is not a ready flight array.

**Useful observations.** Supported sweep, loaded form, usable root travel, equivalent-duty output and folding condition. Separate source asymmetry from preparation bias. Higher unloaded span or a straighter displayed wing is not a complete quality claim.

**Builder contribution.** Locate roots around the center of supported mass, reserve their whole sweep and choose an appropriate Return-link yoke. Do not force a fan shut merely to fit a narrow frame while pretending powered support remains unchanged.

**What it does.** Open through its allowed geometry, maintain the selected working stroke, change attitude with available control, then fold only under a mode where the loss or change of support is accounted for.

**Material and receiving.** Use a source-appropriate fold cradle with independent root support and protected margins. A damaged side can be rematched to real replacement stock if the resulting complete array is assessed again.

**Customer purpose.** Longline makers and owners who need repeatable forward flight with a manageable carrying form.

**Boundary.** Complete means the listed surfaces and roots; drive, reserve and yoke are separate unless the tin explicitly includes them. Not a powered version of a passive Browfoil by name alone.

<a id="w18-prep-quadwing"></a>

### Dragonfly Quadwing / matched flight array

`W18.PREP.QUADWING`

**Role.** Four working surfaces and roots

**Source and related entries.** [W16.DONOR.GLASSWING](reference/W17/reference/W16/RRR_Wave_16_Foundations.md#w16-donor-glasswing) · [W14.PART.QUADWING](reference/W17/reference/W16/reference/W15/reference/RRR_Wave_14_Foundations.md#w14-part-quadwing)

**Supplied anatomy.** Four source-identified narrow wings and roots arranged as the actual front/rear, left/right array. Every wing need not originate from one donor, but a mixed array needs a whole-array response and fit assessment.

**Useful observations.** Root travel, paired balance, relevant deformation and performance under the intended coordination and burden. A source-matched damaged pair is not necessarily better than a correctly rematched pair.

**Builder contribution.** Provide continuous working clearance and the chosen Crossphase relationship. A recipe does not assume every shape can fold above the abdomen. Gyropods are optional only where native information and control already meet the advertised ordinary construction.

**What it does.** Initiate the supported stroke, establish flight, change relative drive/control where physically available, and prepare actual recontact. Hover is demonstrated at a stated loaded condition rather than awarded to every dragonfly label.

**Material and receiving.** Hold in a permitted open contour case, keeping membranes off restraints and living roots appropriately supplied. Freshly emerged whole stock has its own expansion needs and is not transported as a finished array.

**Customer purpose.** Skyhook courtyard and controlled-gate builders, plus rematching specialists.

**Boundary.** Wing counts and source identities do not each supply another independent full drive or reservoir.

<a id="w18-prep-flightdrive"></a>

### Flight-configured drive bundle

`W18.PREP.FLIGHTDRIVE`

**Role.** Motor function with a different output connection

**Source and related entries.** [W14.PART.REDLINE](reference/W17/reference/W16/reference/W15/reference/RRR_Wave_14_Foundations.md#w14-part-redline) · [W14.PART.TORQUE](reference/W17/reference/W16/reference/W15/reference/RRR_Wave_14_Foundations.md#w14-part-torque) · [W16.DONOR.CICADA](reference/W17/reference/W16/RRR_Wave_16_Foundations.md#w16-donor-cicada) · [W16.DONOR.SCARAB](reference/W17/reference/W16/RRR_Wave_16_Foundations.md#w16-donor-scarab)

**Supplied anatomy.** A selected contractile preparation with output attachments appropriate to the wingstroke mechanism. It may use established Redline or Torque-source stock if the measured duty and actual preparation fit; a single name is not a universal flight restriction.

**Useful observations.** Useful force, cadence, efficiency, readiness and mass under the named duty. Continuous support and bursts can require different operating points; endurance is not established by one fast pulse.

**Builder contribution.** Supply the bundle, brace its output and connect it to the yoke without pinching adjacent motion. A basic walking branch or retained native drive is stated independently.

**What it does.** Delivers work into the actual transmission. Throttle/effort adjustments describe the available range, not new energy. A loss of supply or outlet readiness limits work before a fictional flight meter intervenes.

**Material and receiving.** Buffered active-root handling and the agreed ordinary response check. Re-preparing a ground drive records labor, changed attachments and retest rather than cloning the original.

**Customer purpose.** A Flyer buyer can purchase a matched drive/yoke service; a muscle breeder can supply useful ordinary output without cultivating wings.

**Boundary.** A flight preparation is an output/duty adaptation, not automatically an upgrade over the same source in ground racing.

<a id="w18-prep-crossphase"></a>

### Wingstroke Yoke / Crossphase pattern

`W18.PREP.CROSSPHASE`

**Role.** Gait/transmission and relative timing

**Source and related entries.** [W18.PREP.QUADWING](RRR_Wave_18_Foundations.md#w18-prep-quadwing) · [W14.MAP.GAIT](reference/W17/reference/W16/reference/W15/reference/RRR_Wave_14_Foundations.md#w14-map-gait)

**Supplied anatomy.** A prepared lever-and-coupling assembly at the four working roots, built from the actual array junctions and separately supplied interface material where required. It is a construction pattern, not a newly discovered insect organ. Donor roots used here remain counted once.

**Useful observations.** Input/output travel, useful force range, fore/hind phase, left/right response, resistance and mass. The actual observations specify the assembled array; no scalar “synchronization score” chooses every duty.

**Builder contribution.** Set a supported ordinary phase and lever range. A variable pattern identifies the movable control elements; the basic pattern has a fixed prepared setting. Native host control must be connected to any active adjustment.

**What it does.** Coordinates working strokes within supplied work. Changing the phase can favor another force or efficiency regime when the actual array supports it; it cannot duplicate energy or guarantee every maneuver.

**Material and receiving.** A root bridge protects geometry without keeping the array loaded. Replacement couplings are actual stock; rematching creates a new assembled measurement, not a different genealogy.

**Customer purpose.** Skyhook fitters, research breeders testing root/fan interactions and customer-stock adaptation services.

**Boundary.** Clockleaf Lockstep is not an automatic universal substitute: paired jump release and cyclic four-wing timing are different requirements.

<a id="w18-prep-returnlink"></a>

### Wingstroke Yoke / Return-link pattern

`W18.PREP.RETURNLINK`

**Role.** Gait/transmission for the Longwing array

**Material and receiving.** Unloaded root supports, protected stops and a clear folded/open diagram. Structural folding wear and living interface readiness remain separate inspection points.

**Customer purpose.** Longline makers and owners changing between sustained travel and more responsive approaches.

**Boundary.** The return motion is part of the working cycle; it is not free recovery energy that can be spent again as propulsion.

## 5. Flight and recovery actions: the animation must describe the same body

The sequence defines meaning, not a mandatory solver, frame count or direct-control scheme. The host acts from actual cues and allowed prior familiarization. A referee’s knowledge of required gates is not implanted as an omniscient course list in the animal. Source inspirations: [W18.S06](RRR_Wave_18_Research.md#w18-s06) · [W18.S07](RRR_Wave_18_Research.md#w18-s07) · [W18.S08](RRR_Wave_18_Research.md#w18-s08) · [W18.S16](RRR_Wave_18_Research.md#w18-s16).

<a id="w18-action-launch"></a>

### Release, unfold and establish flight

`W18.ACTION.LAUNCH`

**Requirements.** A supported ready host, permitted launch procedure, sufficient working clearance and the declared ground/wing drive.

**Physical sequence.** Release on the common signal; move or rise through the offered launch volume; deploy in the permitted sequence; establish the needed support before entering the first scored gate.

**Builder or race-plan decision.** A ready complete build may use a takeoff run or tested lift from a perch. The format supplies equivalent launch opportunity and publishes the choice before entry; vertical takeoff is not mandatory for every Flyer.

**Failure and interference.** A wing strikes its own root support, a run ends before usable lift, or a supply ceiling prevents sustained support. The reason names the construction, not a missing arbitrary flight level.

**What must be readable.** Folded and powered states are visibly different; an insect tossed by a handler is not shown as a successful self-powered launch.

<a id="w18-action-cruise"></a>

### Hold an efficient working passage

`W18.ACTION.CRUISE`

**Requirements.** Sustained useful work, compatible yoke, supported surfaces and enough legal airspace.

**Physical sequence.** Settle into a working stroke and attitude; progress through the corridor; adjust only where the actual air, load or route requires it.

**Builder or race-plan decision.** Choose a broader efficient line or a shorter demanding line. A very high beat setting can waste energy if its extra work does not become useful course progress.

**Failure and interference.** Emptying reserves, limited delivery, root interference or unsuitable loaded shape can degrade pace. They require different next parts or preparations.

**What must be readable.** Body travel and actual wing/root motion agree. Speed is visible; diagrams and diagnostics do not replace a fast-looking racer.

<a id="w18-action-climb"></a>

### Trade work and path for altitude

`W18.ACTION.CLIMB`

**Requirements.** Useful upward force or redirection within the construction’s current state and a reachable exit route.

**Physical sequence.** Prepare the climb; spend or redirect actual available work; gain the height; transition without assuming all original forward pace survives.

**Builder or race-plan decision.** Take an earlier wider climbing approach or a more direct higher-output route. A high-capacity reserve cannot compensate for a delivery-limited climb.

**Failure and interference.** The build can lack support or room to complete the transition even when a high gate appears reachable in a static outline.

**What must be readable.** The climb has a beginning and a transition into onward travel; height is not a free checkpoint reward.

<a id="w18-action-turn"></a>

### Redirect through an aerial bend

`W18.ACTION.TURN`

**Requirements.** Control authority, supported root motion and a route wide enough for the body plus working sweep.

**Physical sequence.** Reorient useful force and attitude; change direction through the chosen path; restore the next useful working state.

**Builder or race-plan decision.** A broad fast arc can compete with a tighter slower path. The shorter drawn line is not always the faster traversed route.

**Failure and interference.** Body center clears a hoop while a working wing clips it, or the requested turn exceeds the configuration’s response. The gate cannot rotate the racer magnetically.

**What must be readable.** The wing roots and body attitude express the maneuver; a ground sprite following an invisible spline does not define the intended action.

<a id="w18-action-pass"></a>

### Pass above, below or beside an actual opponent

`W18.ACTION.PASS`

**Requirements.** Legal shared airspace, achievable closing motion and a collision-free or valid contact route for the complete working bodies.

**Physical sequence.** Choose a feasible opening; commit enough motion to clear it; avoid or resolve actual contact; rejoin the onward route with the whole relevant envelope.

**Builder or race-plan decision.** A vertical separation may cost climbing work while a lateral pass adds distance. Folding temporarily is possible only where its resulting flight mode can physically support the maneuver.

**Failure and interference.** A target moves, the gap closes or a wide sweep catches an opponent. No positive relative distance in a top-down picture guarantees an unobstructed pass.

**What must be readable.** Depth cues, shadow/height markers and gate references keep who passed whom legible without needing constant camera shake.

<a id="w18-action-slow"></a>

### Reduce air-relative or course-relative pace

`W18.ACTION.SLOW`

**Requirements.** Actual force redirection or resistance, and adequate remaining support for the planned state.

**Physical sequence.** Prepare an appropriate attitude or stroke change; dissipate or redirect motion; settle into a reachable approach.

**Builder or race-plan decision.** A wider return can preserve efficient flight; a more direct deceleration can save path length. A ventral brake participates only after physical ground contact.

**Failure and interference.** Slowing below the build’s supported mode can lose height. A printed “maneuverable” label does not guarantee stationary hovering.

**What must be readable.** Changing wing and body action precedes deceleration; no contact skid is drawn in empty air.

<a id="w18-action-glide"></a>

### Use a finite unpowered interval

`W18.ACTION.GLIDE`

**Requirements.** Available height/motion, usable passive surfaces and a permitted route.

**Physical sequence.** Reduce powered action; maintain the available passive attitude; trade existing motion or height for progress; resume useful power or make contact before support is exhausted.

**Builder or race-plan decision.** A suitable descent can save expenditure. A tight gate may instead need active response that the folded or unpowered state cannot supply.

**Failure and interference.** Remaining airborne forever without an external source, or gaining height solely because a glide cooldown ended, contradicts the construction.

**What must be readable.** The motion differs from flapping; losing height or speed is readable. Displayed remaining fuel is not increased by the absence of consumption.

<a id="w18-action-touchdown"></a>

### Recover a surface after flight

`W18.ACTION.TOUCHDOWN`

**Requirements.** Reachable receiving area, usable attitude, actual feet/joints and room for the wing state after contact.

**Physical sequence.** Approach and align; prepare the receiving limbs; contact and absorb load; settle; fold or continue ground movement as required.

**Builder or race-plan decision.** Choose a longer controlled arrival or a shorter more demanding approach. Optional Springknees, contacts or damping improve their particular phase rather than all flight.

**Failure and interference.** A wing is trapped under the body, contact skids or the receiver is occupied. Landing does not teleport the racer to a free slot.

**What must be readable.** Contact, compression, rebound and renewed movement are distinct. A finish gate can record a valid finish before a separate post-finish receiving area, as the format states.

<a id="w18-action-retry"></a>

### Recover a missed gate without reversing into the pack

`W18.ACTION.RETRY`

**Requirements.** A declared unscored recovery pocket, remaining flight/ground capability and a legal approach back to the missed gate.

**Physical sequence.** Enter the marked return path without receiving gate credit; reapproach the missed gate in the valid direction; resume only after correct crossing.

**Builder or race-plan decision.** Attempt the legal retry or retire for retrieval when supply or condition makes it unsuitable. The event publishes this before entry.

**Failure and interference.** Crossing the wrong face, skipping the gate or being carried forward by retrieval cannot satisfy the course. Backtracking directly into oncoming entrants is not the default repair.

**What must be readable.** The missed gate and legal return are visible; the result does not hide a miss behind a mysterious time penalty.

<a id="w18-action-clear"></a>

### Right on a real recovery surface

`W18.ACTION.CLEAR`

**Requirements.** A reached catch/landing deck and either native support or a suitable Kickback construction.

**Physical sequence.** Establish contact; right using available anatomy; check working readiness; re-enter by the published retry route or retire.

**Builder or race-plan decision.** Use self-recovery when possible; operator retrieval follows retirement rather than silently restoring place. A body still flying has not yet reached this opportunity.

**Failure and interference.** Too little deck clearance, a bound hinge or unavailable supply prevents that recovery method. Another reachable method may remain.

**What must be readable.** Native-leg levering and a thoracic snap are different visible actions. The clock and lost position continue to matter.

## 7. Aerial circuit grammar and owned pieces

A physical piece has connectors, support needs, aperture, material and ownership. A layout specifies their placement and the air volumes between them. An event chooses a version, permitted branches, laps, release, recovery and finish procedure. A saved layout is knowledge; constructing it needs the actual pieces. [W18.S04](RRR_Wave_18_Research.md#w18-s04) · [W18.S05](RRR_Wave_18_Research.md#w18-s05)

**Passage and clearance.** A gate has a valid entry face and an actual opening. Progress requires passage of the racer through that opening in the intended direction, not touching a distant scoring sphere. A working envelope describes what may occupy surrounding space; a timed folded transition must be evaluated as that real movement, not prohibited by a maximum outline alone. Contact with a frame can occur and have consequences without automatically becoming a skipped gate; going around it is not passage. The downstream implementation must preserve those distinctions.

**Route and assembly agreement.** The start leads into the first gate; every ordinary lap can reach the next lap; every permitted branch has a complete ordered sequence and a known merge. Supports may cross in projection only with real separation. Gate orientation, return spaces and approach volumes must remain visible from useful player/spectator viewpoints. A layout that cannot provide two plausible response families needs redesign or an explicit specialist-event label.

**Recovery and ranking.** Lap completion and valid gate progress precede any estimate of distance to finish. A shorter branch is not a free extra gate. Missed gates use a declared safe retry or retirement. Operator retrieval never advances the racer. The published finish boundary decides victory; a separately identified receiving apron cannot become a hidden final scoring condition.

<a id="w18-course-release"></a>

### Release perch and takeoff deck

`W18.COURSE.RELEASE`

**Reference role.** Starting section; the launch procedure is a new aerial implementation.

**Physical construction.** A stable deck or perch with marked individual release positions, common sight of the signal, wing-clearance space and a clear run or rise into the first approach. The support, surround and any safety screen are physical pieces with ownership.

**Route function.** Introduces the racer before the first scored gate. It does not provide unlimited throw velocity or allow one entrant to start a lap ahead. An event can provide a shared run-up or a supported stationary launch, with the choice announced.

**Question for the build.** Can this complete construction establish flight without striking its own apparatus or another entrant?

**Plausible responses.** Use the common takeoff run with useful ground contact and timed deployment. / Use a demonstrated lift-from-perch mode when the same launch format permits it.

**Source and livelihood connection.** Ordinary landing feet, deployment fitting and a complete ready-to-fly entry tin; no mandatory premium wing family.

**Design failure to reject.** A forced vertical start silently excluding every cruise-oriented kit from a supposedly general beginner race.

**Source and livelihood connection.** Body-scale fit, appropriate root motion and wing-envelope demonstrations; a narrower native body does not shrink bought wings.

<a id="w18-course-descent"></a>

### Descending return

`W18.COURSE.DESCENT`

**Reference role.** Descent and approach-control section.

**Physical construction.** An upper entrance and lower exit, with a visible receiving approach beyond. The curve and containment leave room to trade height, speed and response.

**Route function.** Moves the race to a lower level without demanding an automatic landing unless the course card states one.

**Question for the build.** Can the racer use or dissipate the resulting motion and still reach the next passage?

**Plausible responses.** Use a longer passive or lightly powered glide with actual loss of height. / Actively redirect and slow within a more direct line.

**Source and livelihood connection.** Loaded surface form, control response and an appropriate approach setup; ventral pad material is irrelevant until contact.

**Design failure to reject.** Treating downward travel as both unlimited free speed and unlimited reserve recharge.

<a id="w18-course-window"></a>

### Airflow window

`W18.COURSE.WINDOW`

**Reference role.** Surface-specific section reinterpreted as a declared environmental interaction.

**Physical construction.** A framed zone with visible streamers and, for controlled indoor events, a physically powered and guarded airflow source. Intake, exhaust, screens and side openings have actual positions.

**Route function.** A designated part of the legal route, not a region-wide stat modifier. Direction and the permitted operating variation are on the course card.

**Question for the build.** Can the working array handle the announced crossflow or choose a route that encounters it differently?

**Plausible responses.** Compensate with sufficient control and a suitable body attitude. / Use a wider sheltered lane or legal upstream approach, paying its actual path cost.

**Source and livelihood connection.** Responsive roots, useful sensing and working correction; source selection can investigate these without receiving a wind-resistance aura.

**Design failure to reject.** A surprise gust chosen to defeat the currently fitted build, or a fan that moves air without any acknowledged power/support.

<a id="w18-course-crossover"></a>

### Over-under crossing frame

`W18.COURSE.CROSSOVER`

**Reference role.** Bridge or lane crossover, now explicitly aerial.

**Physical construction.** Two distinguishable route channels at different elevations with visible supports and separation. The support structure and the nearby climb/return paths are included in the object brief.

**Route function.** Paths can cross in projection while remaining separated. The plan states whether each channel is sequential or belongs to an alternative branch.

**Question for the build.** Can the design maintain route recognition and useful vertical separation without hidden collisions?

**Plausible responses.** Follow the announced upper channel using its preceding climb. / Follow the declared lower branch where the event offers one; do not jump between channels to skip gates.

**Source and livelihood connection.** Trackmakers, section carriers and course inspection; route knowledge does not create extra physical beams.

**Design failure to reject.** Calling two intersecting drawn lines a bridge when the assembled supports or swept volumes still collide.

<a id="w18-course-gallery"></a>

### Passing gallery

`W18.COURSE.GALLERY`

**Reference role.** Shared straight/brawl space, not fixed lanes.

**Physical construction.** A broad contained air volume with legible near/far and upper/lower references. Open sides for viewing are separated from actual spectator access by the venue’s appropriate boundary.

**Route function.** Permits real side-by-side or vertically separated travel between known entry and exit faces.

**Question for the build.** Can a construction overtake, defend a line or use legal contact while making forward race progress?

**Plausible responses.** Pass laterally with enough closing motion and full working clearance. / Use a legal vertical separation, spending and recovering its actual height/energy.

**Source and livelihood connection.** Alternative wing/root preparations, forceful or agile approaches, offense in eligible divisions and readable race staging.

**Design failure to reject.** A corridor too narrow for the advertised class to interact, or limitless vertical escape that bypasses every later challenge.

<a id="w18-course-fork"></a>

### Signed route fork and merge

`W18.COURSE.FORK`

**Reference role.** Alternate course sections with published reconnection.

**Physical construction.** A common split, two explicitly gated branches and a merge region visible early enough to choose a path. Both branches and all supports require real owned pieces.

**Route function.** A legal branch is a complete listed gate sequence from split to merge. Finishing one branch satisfies that segment; changing halfway does not satisfy the missing gates.

**Question for the build.** Can different constructions exploit different lines without a brand or species check?

**Plausible responses.** Choose a longer broad branch preserving efficient pace. / Choose the shorter more responsive branch with its actual turn and clearance demand.

**Source and livelihood connection.** Reusable course plans and several viable kit families; instructors can demonstrate the difference with the same ordinary host.

**Design failure to reject.** An item-ID gate disguised as terrain, or a branch that is shorter, easier and safer in every meaningful condition.

<a id="w18-course-apron"></a>

### Return apron, catch frame and retry pocket

`W18.COURSE.APRON`

**Reference role.** Landing section plus an explicitly authored recovery procedure.

**Physical construction.** A receiving deck with clear approach, contact surface, wing stow space, perimeter support and an offset catch/retry pocket. A soft-looking net is not presumed harmless or available unless it is part of the actual venue.

**Route function.** Can be required touchdown within a route or a post-finish receiving area; the card states which. A marked return path leads from a recoverable miss to the missed gate without racing backward through the field.

**Question for the build.** Can the racer turn a valid arrival or recoverable mistake into useful contact and onward movement?

**Plausible responses.** Make a longer controlled landing using native or fitted receiving limbs. / Use a shorter arrival supported by appropriate joints, contact and response; a tipped roach may use a suitable Kickback only after reaching real support.

**Source and livelihood connection.** Landing sets, grounded brakes where an actual raised approach engages them, recovery fitting, receiving services and course crews.

**Design failure to reject.** Teleporting a retrieved racer into the lead, requiring a recovery organ before the first race, or moving the scored finish after a participant has already crossed it.

<a id="course-layouts"></a>

## 8. Four authored course arrangements

These are qualitative construction references, not geometrically calibrated layouts or recorded races. No absolute dimensions follow from the old prototypes. A maker must specify actual apertures, spacing and support against the selected scale before a layout can become a buildable asset set.

<a id="w18-layout-courtyard"></a>

### Foundry Courtyard Loop

`W18.LAYOUT.COURTYARD`

**Identity.** A complete introductory aerial race with open visual reference and broad turns, not a hovering examination.

| Sequence | Section or route function |
|---|---|
| 1 | Release deck before scoring |
| 2 | Gate 1: level portal |
| 3 | Gate 2: broad left sweep |
| 4 | Gate 3: passing gallery |
| 5 | Gate 4: broad left sweep |
| 6 | Finish portal; the onward corridor reconnects to Gate 1 for further declared laps |
| 7 | Separate post-finish return apron |

**Branch agreement.** No mandatory fork in the introductory plan. The turn volumes admit both a wider maintained-speed line and a tighter responsive line. Physical pieces are separately identified even when two use the same pattern.

**Start and finish.** A common takeoff run serves both ordinary wing families. The complete last-lap finish records crossing order before post-finish landing, which remains a handled safety/receiving operation rather than hidden extra scoring.

**Different ways to race it.** A Skyhook Courtyard build can favor controlled redirection; a Longline can use broad arcs and maintain forward work. Both must demonstrate that they can complete this actual introductory envelope with ordinary suitable stock.

**Biological and commercial demand.** Whole entry tins, normal bench sessions, stage-conditioned arrays and matched drive/yoke services. A buyer can begin through Two Nets without owning any nursery.

**Comparison boundary.** Hold air conditions, reserve fill, host and route version fixed for a component-only comparison. A whole-kit comparison is a different claim. A larger wing is not given a grade-based shortcut.

**Operating conditions.** Sheltered operating air; observed changes beyond the announced allowance pause starts. Fixed laps and any starting-position rotation are printed before entry.

<a id="w18-layout-reed"></a>

### Reed Sweep

`W18.LAYOUT.REED`

**Identity.** A longer aerial course rewarding sustained work and legitimate choices about climbing and approach.

| Sequence | Section or route function |
|---|---|
| 1 | Release deck |
| 2 | Gate 1: level entrance |
| 3 | Crown split: gradual outer climb or steeper direct climb, each with its own required intermediate gate |
| 4 | Common upper portal |
| 5 | Upper passing gallery |
| 6 | Descending return |
| 7 | Airflow window with a broad disclosed avoidance line |
| 8 | Sweeping pair |
| 9 | Finish and reconnect corridor |
| 10 | Post-finish return apron |

**Branch agreement.** The two climb branches are alternate complete sequences; a roach cannot cross only their convenient endpoints. The airflow zone’s longer sheltered side remains within its printed legal volume, not an invisible invulnerability lane.

**Start and finish.** Run-up launch and an airborne final finish; no compulsory hover, touchdown or post-race climb inserted after entry.

**Different ways to race it.** A high-delivery build can choose a steeper rise; an efficient long-travel build can gain height earlier. On descent, a longer glide preserves a different supply budget from a direct actively slowed return.

**Biological and commercial demand.** Delivery-focused reserves, efficient flight drives, Longwing root/fold lines, flight-session comparisons and real route-support work for Case Runners and trackmakers.

**Comparison boundary.** Tailwind ground speed is not isolated inherited efficiency. An animal that happens to start fuller cannot certify the superiority of its source line.

**Operating conditions.** Direction and expected variability of the airflow window are disclosed. Support structures and exit turns must admit the declared entrant envelope.

<a id="w18-layout-lantern"></a>

### Lantern Exchange

`W18.LAYOUT.LANTERN`

**Identity.** A spatial choice course whose excitement comes from visible crossings and contested merges, not hidden height rules.

| Sequence | Section or route function |
|---|---|
| 1 | Release deck |
| 2 | Entry portal |
| 3 | Signed fork: upper Crown route or lower Turning route |
| 4 | Separated over-under crossing |
| 5 | Common passing gallery and merge |
| 6 | Offset portal pair |
| 7 | Broad return |
| 8 | Finish portal and next-lap connection |
| 9 | Retry pockets outside the active merge |

**Branch agreement.** Upper and lower branches reconnect before the common gallery. The upper route gains height and has broader turns; the lower route requires more responsive redirection. All gates have distinguishable faces and ordinal branch signs.

**Start and finish.** The ordinary launch volume is common. Finish requires the actual branch sequence taken and the shared gates. A viewing camera’s projection cannot rank a roach by apparent screen height.

**Different ways to race it.** A racer can invest work to take the open upper line or preserve height while accepting lower tight turns. Neither path is automatically made optimal for a named wing product.

**Biological and commercial demand.** Mixed-source matched arrays, purposeful yoke settings and course-crew inspection. A spectator can follow the occupied branch and anticipate where the field will meet.

**Comparison boundary.** A copied paper plan does not supply the second level’s supports. A rejected overlap is a construction issue, not a fee that lets the player ignore it.

**Operating conditions.** Clear visual identities for the two channels; no sudden fog or unannounced lighting condition used to manufacture difficulty.

<a id="w18-layout-flare"></a>

### Fireworks Air Gallery

`W18.LAYOUT.FLARE`

**Identity.** An offensive aerial race with room for actual encounters, limited discharge, and a finish-first objective.

| Sequence | Section or route function |
|---|---|
| 1 | Common launch corridor with weapons inactive until the printed racing release point |
| 2 | Level portal |
| 3 | Wide encounter gallery |
| 4 | Sweeping pair with an outer passing line |
| 5 | Second level portal |
| 6 | Announced airflow window and separated bypass branch |
| 7 | Broad merge |
| 8 | Finish portal |
| 9 | Separated inactive receiving aprons |

**Branch agreement.** A wider route can avoid an occupied plume path at a real distance cost. This is geometry and timing, not a passive immunity granted by choosing a branch.

**Start and finish.** Light-protection and working-offense inspection occurs before launch. Scoring begins and ends at printed points. A completed course, not damage dealt or surviving last, determines the winner.

**Different ways to race it.** An Airflare build can make a selective rear discharge; a grasping or horn-equipped flyer can choose direct contact if it can maintain supported flight through that action. Withholding a costly attack is a legitimate racing decision.

**Biological and commercial demand.** Finite-output Boilers, lightweight root support, compact contact offense, replenishment services and common ordinary stock. A newcomer can buy a qualifying basic offensive tin; no rare gland is the required entry item.

**Comparison boundary.** A clear plume or hit can interrupt local function according to the future agreed damage model; it does not automatically award points, kills, guaranteed stuns or a victory.

**Operating conditions.** Containment, allowed contact, discharge boundary, plume handling and retrieval are printed; spectators, inactive benches and receiving staff are outside the allowed effects.
