# RAD ROACH RACING
## WASTELANDER LANISTA — WAVE v0.15
### The Joined Hobby: Milestone Review and Progression Polish

**Edition:** 0.15.0 · **Date:** 11 September 2026 · **Phase:** researched world-bible and compendium architecture.

**Core revision:** short, complete pleasures must connect to longer chosen projects. A personally finished racer, a race worth winning, a useful capture, a selected descendant, a paid piece of work and a satisfying collection are complete outcomes—not merely prerequisites for a distant “real” game.

The milestone reviews **0–5**, **5–10** and **10–15** as overlapping design periods. Waves 5 and 10 are interfaces between them, not duplicate releases. Wave 00 denotes the original brief; Wave 10’s written handoff is retained without inventing recovery of its missing executable. Wave 15 is this review and its authored replacements.

The existing world has a strong physical chain: particular insects supply particular anatomy, the player assembles that anatomy, and the same parts enter the race and market. The main cross-period risk is that individually sensible mechanics form a compulsory serial gauntlet. This volume keeps their consequences while removing that compulsory dependency.

**Status:** working compendium amendments. No application is modified. Prior experiments are retained as evidence at their original scope, not promoted into biological laws or proof of engagement. New participation, service and progression designs are reasoned proposals awaiting downstream testing.

## 1. What each period contributes—and what changes at the join

| Period | Retain | Weak join | Developed revision |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–5 — The racer and the kit | Distinct source-component functions, personally assembled hosts, reversible drafts, actual anatomy, recognizable builds, and race outcomes. | The initial complete-kit pleasure can disappear beneath the later requirement to operate collection, certification and production before the next improvement. | Preserve a complete first-project path, bounded ordinary bench sessions, visible cause-and-effect comparisons, and functional intermediate conversions. |
| 5–10 — The living source and productive workshop | Purposeful donors, stage-aware facilities, complementary breeding, unique preparation, stable ownership, parts trade and owned courses. | Separate valid costs, reservations and waiting can accumulate into mandatory administration; protected descendants and unsold output can grow faster than useful demand. | Contextual field evidence, split-brood milestones, target-based replacement reserves, maintenance plans, explicit ordinary outlets and useful progress within qualification bands. |
| 10–15 — Public competition, products and the world reference | Evidence-backed living supply, intact reference racers, alternate-stock kits, actual recipients, component-built rivals, fieldcraft, and current compendium anatomy. | Finite sponsored examples, disconnected customer futures, a negative first maker margin and accumulated prototype constraints can be mistaken for the mature hobby’s intended rules. | Separate maker livelihoods, purpose-funded repeat work, persistent ordinary sport, collection/display completion, clean stopping and explicit authority amendments. |

**Selection is not compulsory specialization.** Buying, catching, breeding, fitting, making, collecting, hosting and racing share objects. They need not share a mandatory order. A player can enter through a complete tin, a compelling capture, a customer commission, a club rival or a visual collection project.

**Risk is not removed.** A capture can fail; an extraordinary donor can be damaged; a brood can lack the needed contribution; a kit can lose money; a race can be lost. The correction is to preserve understandable alternatives and useful intermediate outcomes rather than conceal the risk or force every activity to succeed before any activity becomes enjoyable.

**Authority.** The author’s premise governs. W14 remains the anatomical and institutional reference. W15’s named amendments update the qualitative progression interpretation. Older equations, fixed stock, costs, capacities, event counts and test outcomes stay inside their historical scopes.

## 3. One hobby, several complete loops

| Scale | Complete outcome | Optional next interest |
|---|---|---|
| Action | A part is fitted, a short run finishes, an insect is secured, a brood is inspected, a sale is settled. | Try another useful change, inspect a candidate, race again or stop. |
| Project | A named racer is built; a useful combination is observed; a customer receives a kit; a display is complete. | Develop another body, line, edition, course or customer relationship. |
| Campaign / season | A series is raced, a supply relationship is established, a collection theme is completed, or a venue earns recognition. | Choose further ambition without deleting the work already completed. |

These scales are not fixed durations. The later game must establish suitable session lengths through observation, not derive them from a tiny prototype’s clock.

<a id="w15-bridge-01"></a>
### A part becomes an observable change

**Cross-period connection.** 0-5 → 5-10 → 10-15

**Physical chain.** Actual prepared part → personal fit → useful visible behavior → real result → optional next acquisition or publication

**Identity retained.** The host, physical component, inherited observations and configuration version.

**Complete now.** One useful fit/run or an understood unsuccessful change.

**Longer interest.** A new component, better source stock, a different set or a commissioned construction.

**Optionality.** No breeding, sale or publication is required to enjoy the run.

<a id="w15-bridge-02"></a>
### A field clue becomes a purposeful brood

**Cross-period connection.** 10-15 → 5-10 → 0-5

**Physical chain.** Existing habitat/individual → contextual cue → capture preserving useful anatomy → complementary parent or direct sect → actual kit behavior

**Identity retained.** The donor identity, observed cue, reproductive history and allocated anatomy.

**Complete now.** Return with a useful animal or meaningful source knowledge; no exact rare outcome promised.

**Longer interest.** Improve the missing contribution or establish a repeated preparation line.

**Optionality.** The captured animal can be used, sold or displayed without breeding it.

<a id="w15-bridge-03"></a>
### A mixed generation supports both ambition and an ordinary livelihood

**Cross-period connection.** 0-5 → 5-10 → 10-15

**Physical chain.** Complementary founders → supported brood → observed dispositions → ordinary accepted product or retained candidate → paid useful output or personal assembly

**Identity retained.** Actual parents, distinct offspring, physical products and funded buyer demand.

**Complete now.** One selection milestone or usable intermediate product.

**Longer interest.** A more repeatable line, broader supply, or a better personal racer.

**Optionality.** Research and ordinary production can share a line without requiring every offspring to be sold or certified.

<a id="w15-bridge-04"></a>
### A finished construction becomes somebody else’s project

**Cross-period connection.** 0-5 → 10-15 → 5-10

**Physical chain.** Personal reference build → bounded product promise → different actual host/components → funded kit → recipient assembly/use → optional revised request

**Identity retained.** The reference remains intact; the sold kit has its own actual host and source parts.

**Complete now.** A product is delivered, then has a recognizable downstream use.

**Longer interest.** A signature edition, a new customer, more suitable stock or a revised construction.

**Optionality.** A championship is not required to make a useful kit; a finished delivery does not force another order.

<a id="w15-bridge-05"></a>
### An owned course becomes a social reason to use another build

**Cross-period connection.** 5-10 → 0-5 → 10-15

**Physical chain.** Owned sections → declared layout → practice/rivals or funded event → actual race/display result → next local meeting or voluntary redesign

**Identity retained.** Section identities, laid version, physical racers, programme and actual results.

**Complete now.** A usable constructed course or a completed meeting.

**Longer interest.** Another arrangement, shared section collection, a racing specialty or a hosted occasion.

**Optionality.** Trackmaking does not require a breeding career, and player geometry does not mint official qualification.

<a id="w15-bridge-06"></a>
### A valuable collection remains enjoyable rather than becoming only overhead

**Cross-period connection.** 0-5 → 5-10 → 10-15

**Physical chain.** Chosen specimen/build → finish or collection arrangement → supported care/display → owner-permitted recognition → optional new visual or sporting project

**Identity retained.** The particular animal, natural features, applied work and historical provenance.

**Complete now.** A finished piece worth keeping or a meaningful display.

**Longer interest.** A complementary specimen, restoration, another printed edition or a different race campaign.

**Optionality.** No performance or cash reward is required to justify the collection, and default offline pause permits stopping.

### The first complete project and its optional branches

`W15.SYSTEM.ENTRY`

**Problem.** A coherent universe can still require too many preliminary occupations before the player experiences owning and racing a personally built roach.

**Design.** Deliver a complete first project, then let its actual result create optional reasons to collect, breed, buy, earn or show.

**Prior reference.** `W02.CONTRACT.03`, `W05.BENCH.01`, `W14.ANATOMY.JOINS`, `W14.SET.TIN`

**Research.** [W15.S02](RRR_Wave_15_Research.md#w15-s02) · [W15.S04](RRR_Wave_15_Research.md#w15-s04) · [W15.S05](RRR_Wave_15_Research.md#w15-s05) · [W15.S06](RRR_Wave_15_Research.md#w15-s06) · [W15.S07](RRR_Wave_15_Research.md#w15-s07)

<a id="w15-entry-01"></a>
**W15.ENTRY.01 — An entry tin includes a functioning host, identified preparations, readable placement guidance, ordinary initial care supplies and a clearly advertised first-use opportunity. Omitted functions remain native.**

Purpose: A novice can understand what they bought and finish assembling it without discovering an unadvertised required purchase.

<a id="w15-entry-02"></a>
**W15.ENTRY.02 — The first assembly contains meaningful ownership decisions: choose a visible configuration, fit at least one relevant preparation, and give the racer its chosen appearance or identity. Repeating a saved familiar assembly may later be streamlined.**

Purpose: Preserve building rather than replacing it with either an instant reward or repeated fastening chores.

<a id="w15-entry-03"></a>
**W15.ENTRY.03 — A local first-use route permits a real race or time trial without breeding, exact genetic assays, commercial certification, named lineage or a prior victory. A first victory is not guaranteed.**

Purpose: The participation floor is understandable; competitive success remains earned.

<a id="w15-entry-04"></a>
**W15.ENTRY.04 — Entry products include accessible horn, grasping, hopper and clean-running constructions where the event permits them. Beginner support is not a mandatory weaponless chapter.**

Purpose: The original derby identity is visible early rather than postponed until the end of the game.

<a id="w15-entry-05"></a>
**W15.ENTRY.05 — After a completed activity, present the result and preserve the physical project before suggesting one chosen next objective. Options may include another race, one rebuild, a donor search, breeding or display.**

Purpose: A finished racer is a reward in itself, not just an instruction to buy the next part.

<a id="w15-entry-06"></a>
**W15.ENTRY.06 — Help appears at the relevant decision. An expert can expose anatomy, protocols and ancestry without repeating introductory instruction; a beginner need not read the whole reference to proceed.**

Purpose: Guidance supports actual complexity without turning world breadth into an entrance examination.

<a id="w15-entry-07"></a>
**W15.ENTRY.07 — Affordability of the entry package is evaluated against an explicitly authored ordinary earning opportunity and initial resources. Do not fund it by selling protected founders or dismantling the supplied reference racer.**

Purpose: A complete first route must exist economically as well as in a menu.

<a id="w15-entry-08"></a>
**W15.ENTRY.08 — A project is optional and cancelable. Changing its goal neither edits the population nor reserves every potentially useful specimen forever.**

Purpose: The player chooses what to pursue; the system records rather than commands ambition.

**Worked definition.** A hobbyist obtains Tin Can Special or an appropriate founder-style kit.

**Decision.** Follow the included arrangement or change a visible permitted part; name and finish the host.

**Completion.** The assembled roach enters an ordinary supported local event and has a valid result, including a possible loss.

**Next optional routes.** Buy a known replacement, inspect a rival, search the appropriate donor, begin a line, or keep racing. No route is a mandatory step toward enjoying the existing build.

**Meaningful failure.** An invalid root is explained at the bench; losing the race does not retroactively invalidate the finished kit.

**Material objects.** [W15.OBJECT.PROJECT_CARD](#w15-object-project-card) · [W15.OBJECT.FIRST_TIN](#w15-object-first-tin)

**Limits.** Exact starter prices, supplies and initial event parameters need economy and novice-session calibration. This volume does not guarantee that a supplied kit wins its first event.

### Foundry bench sessions: fast experimentation with real work

`W15.SYSTEM.BENCH`

**Problem.** Separate fees and calendar advances for selection, assay, fitting and practice can punish learning several times for one intended adjustment.

**Design.** Group ordinary preparation and testing into an intelligible workshop visit while preserving consumables, actual anatomy and consequential custom work.

**Prior reference.** `W05.BENCH.10`, `W06.CLOCK.02`, `W06.FIELD.08`, `W14.GRAFT.SWAP`

**Research.** [W15.S03](RRR_Wave_15_Research.md#w15-s03) · [W15.S04](RRR_Wave_15_Research.md#w15-s04) · [W15.S06](RRR_Wave_15_Research.md#w15-s06)

<a id="w15-bench-01"></a>
**W15.BENCH.01 — A bench-session offer states supported hosts, services, care coverage, normal supplies and the time allowance before commitment. It is available as owned-workshop use or a club service.**

Purpose: Costs correspond to performed work rather than a button-count tariff.

<a id="w15-bench-02"></a>
**W15.BENCH.02 — Draft inspection, comparison, selecting a recipe and undoing an uncommitted placement consume no anatomy, money or simulated production time. An actual test or preparation remains a world activity.**

Purpose: A player can consider alternatives without paying for events that never happened.

<a id="w15-bench-03"></a>
**W15.BENCH.03 — Healthy pre-prepared exchange components can be refitted within the declared ordinary service allowance. New raw extraction, re-preparation, missing supplies or damaged interfaces trigger a separate visible quote.**

Purpose: Routine experimentation is accessible while specialized labor remains valuable.

<a id="w15-bench-04"></a>
**W15.BENCH.04 — The visit advances world time according to its declared performed activities, not each interface gesture. Included supplies and test charges are counted once; extra runs outside the allowance require actual time and supplies.**

Purpose: Broods continue and energy is consumed without multiplying rent, travel and preparation costs for one visit.

<a id="w15-bench-05"></a>
**W15.BENCH.05 — A compare card preserves the selected baseline, observed conditions and changed inputs. A stored reference is not another copy of its components and does not allow both builds to be fitted simultaneously with the same tissue.**

Purpose: Rapid comparison retains ownership and causal meaning.

<a id="w15-bench-06"></a>
**W15.BENCH.06 — The primary report leads with finish, position or measured objective. It then offers the most relevant supported observation and competing explanations. It does not infer a gene from one lost corner.**

Purpose: Feedback motivates another deliberate trial without misdiagnosing the entire animal.

<a id="w15-bench-07"></a>
**W15.BENCH.07 — Restoring an earlier build is possible only with still-owned, available components and the required procedure. Historical results remain available even when physical restoration is impossible.**

Purpose: Undo can restore a draft; it cannot reverse a paid sale or regenerate an extracted organ.

<a id="w15-bench-08"></a>
**W15.BENCH.08 — Ordinary first demonstrations can be included in a kit or club visit. Precision load testing, destructive tests and certified batch work remain separate services when their extra information matters.**

Purpose: Pay for useful additional certainty rather than for every first clue.

**Worked definition.** A Needle & Thread owner already holds a compatible alternative contact pair.

**Decision.** Book normal bench use, record a matched reference, swap the ready contacts, run the same short course.

**Completion.** A comparison records actual outcome and which contact observations changed.

**Next optional routes.** Keep the new pair, refit the earlier one, or investigate a different limiting component.

**Meaningful failure.** A worse run costs its real included or additional resources; it does not create another whole visit charge for each UI action.

**Material objects.** [W15.OBJECT.SESSION_CARD](#w15-object-session-card) · [W15.OBJECT.COMPARISON_SLIP](#w15-object-comparison-slip)

**Limits.** The duration/allowance is a service parameter, not unlimited free physical testing. No W15 change is applied to historical per-action fees or old cash ledgers.

<a id="w15-system-racing"></a>
### Useful mechanical opportunities instead of hidden equipment locks

`W15.SYSTEM.RACING`

**Problem.** Correct equations or anatomical descriptions are insufficient if the advertised action rarely works, cannot be read, or only solves a single mandatory obstacle.

**Design.** Give each prominent construction recurring, understandable opportunities to race well, including genuine speed gains and conditional tactical choices.

**Prior reference.** `W14.ACTION.RUN`, `W14.ACTION.HOP`, `W14.ACTION.GRASP`, `W11.FORMAT.01`, `W09.BRAKE.09`

**Research.** [W15.S01](RRR_Wave_15_Research.md#w15-s01) · [W15.S03](RRR_Wave_15_Research.md#w15-s03) · [W15.S11](RRR_Wave_15_Research.md#w15-s11) · [W15.S13](RRR_Wave_15_Research.md#w15-s13)

<a id="w15-racing-01"></a>
**W15.RACING.01 — A promoted component has a named opportunity envelope: relevant load, contact, supply, geometry, approach and available response. A course does not secretly activate its benefit by item ID.**

Purpose: The same mechanism matters wherever its actual conditions occur.

<a id="w15-racing-02"></a>
**W15.RACING.02 — Every published course challenge provides at least two physically plausible response families unless it is explicitly a specialist demonstration. The alternatives need not be equally fast or use identical budgets.**

Purpose: A short landing can invite braking, changed approach/gearing or a different approved route rather than one obligatory rare part.

<a id="w15-racing-03"></a>
**W15.RACING.03 — For a signature action, the reference defines an enabling case, a non-enabling case and a case where altered anatomy or fitting materially changes the outcome.**

Purpose: A Hopper, Catchclaw or rail guide cannot be called successful design because its code branch exists.

<a id="w15-racing-04"></a>
**W15.RACING.04 — A useful hop includes a loading opportunity, actual separation or route advantage, a reachable landing and recovered running. Available power and closing motion remain prerequisites, but the encounter repertoire must contain real opportunities to satisfy them.**

Purpose: Do not hide timing failures behind a jump-win bonus or accept a flagship action that never occurs.

<a id="w15-racing-05"></a>
**W15.RACING.05 — A grasping build describes reach, closing time, support and release. A held rival can cost both racers time; choosing when not to grasp is a legitimate race tendency.**

Purpose: The original mantis response remains a contextual racing tactic, not an unconditional stun.

<a id="w15-racing-06"></a>
**W15.RACING.06 — Display mass, drive, supply, geometry and action as related inputs. Preserve a genuinely faster or more efficient part when it is genuinely better; no compulsory compensating weakness is added to every discovery.**

Purpose: Progress includes superiority as well as specialization.

<a id="w15-racing-07"></a>
**W15.RACING.07 — A post-race explanation cites visible events and measured inputs under stated conditions. A component-only change and a whole-build change are different comparisons.**

Purpose: A close victory remains a victory before it becomes an engineering report.

<a id="w15-racing-08"></a>
**W15.RACING.08 — Courses are authored as racing spaces: clean running, traffic, surface transitions and maneuver windows coexist. Do not add an obstacle merely to sell its exclusive counter.**

Purpose: Challenge should reward construction and decisions rather than enforce a shopping checklist.

**Worked definition.** A Last Laugh build on a mixed club course with a short shared straight and a longer unobstructed landing run.

**Decision.** Choose a lower controllable hop for nearby traffic or remain running when no useful clearance exists.

**Completion.** The observer can identify loading, a real positional change or failed attempt, landing and resumed pace.

**Next optional routes.** Improve synchronization, remount, change preload, adjust the drive/contact combination, or choose a different route.

**Meaningful failure.** A late launch may fail, but the developer must also supply representative encounters where correct timing and the same anatomy can succeed.

**Material objects.** [W15.OBJECT.COURSE_BRIEF](#w15-object-course-brief) · [W15.OBJECT.COMPARISON_SLIP](#w15-object-comparison-slip)

**Limits.** No new race solver, winner or numerical lap comparison is produced in this milestone. Multi-roach contact, aerial motion and control fidelity still require implementation and human observation.

<a id="w15-system-field"></a>
### One source ecology, several depths of collecting

`W15.SYSTEM.FIELD`

**Problem.** Opaque genes, paid assays and several failure filters can make rare hunting a sequence of blind expenses; quick and spatial routes can also become unrelated worlds.

**Design.** Make field expertise improve the decision to acquire a particular insect without manufacturing certainty or compulsory exploration.

**Prior reference.** `W14.BREED.SOURCE`, `W13.OBSERVE.05`, `W03.INTAKE.03`

**Research.** [W15.S05](RRR_Wave_15_Research.md#w15-s05) · [W15.S07](RRR_Wave_15_Research.md#w15-s07) · [W15.S08](RRR_Wave_15_Research.md#w15-s08)

<a id="w15-field-01"></a>
**W15.FIELD.01 — Every collecting method names its actual source population and cohort. Quick and detailed visits use that relationship, or explicitly different nearby populations; one site cannot supply duplicate copies through two interfaces.**

Purpose: The amount of interaction changes; the world identity does not.

<a id="w15-field-02"></a>
**W15.FIELD.02 — Baseline observation reveals visible form, useful life stage, actual damage, accessible anatomy and contextual behavior. A short screen can disclose a bounded response under its conditions.**

Purpose: The player has something useful to notice before buying precision.

<a id="w15-field-03"></a>
**W15.FIELD.03 — Workshop measurements add controlled values; descendant evidence adds information about observed offspring. Neither an appearance nor a field movement claims exact hidden alleles.**

Purpose: Preserve discovery and honest uncertainty rather than making the catalogue omniscient.

<a id="w15-field-04"></a>
**W15.FIELD.04 — The field choice specifies desired anatomy, compatible containment, home capacity and at least one possible ordinary use. A general outing remains allowed and can return without profit.**

Purpose: A rare target sits above useful ordinary possibilities rather than being the only nonzero outcome.

<a id="w15-field-05"></a>
**W15.FIELD.05 — Probability review follows the whole route: encounter, visibility, approach, capture, preserved anatomy, useful stage, reproductive eligibility and eventual target. Do not independently lower every success rate until the product becomes unattainable.**

Purpose: Challenge comes from selected decisions and scarce combinations, not unnoticed multiplication of punitive filters.

<a id="w15-field-06"></a>
**W15.FIELD.06 — A competent method reduces its relevant uncertainty or damage; it does not improve the genome after capture. Individual outcomes stay fixed once the corresponding world event occurs.**

Purpose: Knowledge can make a search better without a generic luck stat.

<a id="w15-field-07"></a>
**W15.FIELD.07 — Buying a measured part, an observed founder or a declared reproductive unit is the reliable acquisition alternative for a player who does not enjoy repeated searches. Neither route grants an intrinsic quality premium.**

Purpose: Rare exploration remains an option, not an obligation for every competitive build.

<a id="w15-field-08"></a>
**W15.FIELD.08 — A return report separates cash changes from secured stock, preserved anatomy, observations and continuing home costs. One useful ordinary animal is not automatically cash, and one informative glimpse is not ownership.**

Purpose: The player can judge what the outing actually achieved.

<a id="w15-field-09"></a>
**W15.FIELD.09 — Useful-stage catches receive an intelligible next-action window and an accessible supported holding/preparation option in the relevant settlement. Its exact limits belong to the donor profile.**

Purpose: A special stage creates a choice instead of an unavoidable surprise expiration after returning.

**Worked definition.** A high-purchase Glassfoot line lacks quick release; the Glazed Overgrowth source is known.

**Decision.** Inspect unloading and peel behavior, preserve pads, and keep a promising moderate-grip animal as a candidate founder.

**Completion.** The same animal returns; a field note says what was seen and does not certify the entire genotype.

**Next optional routes.** Controlled assay, direct pad preparation, breeding, trade or collection.

**Meaningful failure.** A damaged pad may lose direct-use value while the animal’s reproductive eligibility is evaluated separately.

**Material objects.** [W15.OBJECT.FIELD_BRIEF](#w15-object-field-brief) · [W15.OBJECT.DISPOSITION_CARD](#w15-object-disposition-card)

**Limits.** Population biology and exact encounter distributions are donor-monograph work. Quick mode need not guarantee equal catch counts or equal information; it must disclose differences in method, time, cost and preservation.

<a id="w15-system-brood"></a>
### Split-brood projects: research, current use and sale in one line

`W15.SYSTEM.BROOD`

**Problem.** An ambitious breeding target can turn most offspring into apparent failures and turn every batch into more protected mouths to feed.

**Design.** Give a line intermediate achievements and an explicit, bounded disposition plan without trivializing inheritance or automatically choosing all valuable animals.

**Prior reference.** `W03.INTAKE.02`, `W06.BREED.07`, `W08.LINE.05`, `W12.BREEDER.09`, `W14.BREED.PROJECTS`

**Research.** [W15.S08](RRR_Wave_15_Research.md#w15-s08) · [W15.S09](RRR_Wave_15_Research.md#w15-s09)

<a id="w15-brood-01"></a>
**W15.BROOD.01 — A project records the desired eventual construction, current missing contribution and available evidence separately. A candidate parent is judged by the contribution it can plausibly add, not required to be the final product.**

Purpose: Preserve the weak-jumper/good-synchronization opportunity.

<a id="w15-brood-02"></a>
**W15.BROOD.02 — Recognize four distinct milestones: a plausible founder contribution, observed expression in descendants, a useful combined individual, and repeat observed output meeting a named specification. None implies the next has already happened.**

Purpose: Progress becomes visible before an entire line is perfected.

<a id="w15-brood-03"></a>
**W15.BROOD.03 — The brood plan allocates possible mature output among research review, personal components, funded ordinary supply and replacement candidates. Those are goals for real outcomes, not guarantees of counts or sexes.**

Purpose: A mixed generation can serve several purposes without cloning bodies.

<a id="w15-brood-04"></a>
**W15.BROOD.04 — A replacement policy targets a maintained reserve by role and condition rather than adding two permanent keepers from every brood. If the reserve is already filled, additional candidates request a deliberate substitution, display, sale or retention choice.**

Purpose: Reliable production does not automatically become an ever-growing stock liability.

<a id="w15-brood-05"></a>
**W15.BROOD.05 — Research holds take priority over automated ordinary dispatch only through explicit project criteria and consent. Unknown potentially interesting stock may go to Review; the player can choose an ordinary unscreened sales policy.**

Purpose: Automation remains useful without pretending it knows which hidden genomes are worthless.

<a id="w15-brood-06"></a>
**W15.BROOD.06 — Useful nonpremium output has a current purpose named before the final rare target is balanced: ordinary Hoppers, joint-only material, complementary breeders or a customer’s narrower requirement.**

Purpose: A failed ultimate combination need not be a failed whole project.

<a id="w15-brood-07"></a>
**W15.BROOD.07 — Paired reproductive roles, fertility, survival, inherited combinations and product acceptance remain separate. The player may buy a known compatible unit or seek another founder; repeated failure does not conjure a perfect pair.**

Purpose: A reliable acquisition option supports continued agency while biological risk stays meaningful.

<a id="w15-brood-08"></a>
**W15.BROOD.08 — A production claim uses the customer’s actual question and records relevant tested offspring and distinct broods. The measured sample is not automatically an unbiased rate for all future offspring.**

Purpose: A line name can develop trust without becoming a stat or price aura.

<a id="w15-brood-09"></a>
**W15.BROOD.09 — Changing a project creates a new target branch with preserved old observations. Existing siblings can become useful under that new purpose without acquiring new genes.**

Purpose: New ambitions reopen the value of familiar stock and records.

**Worked definition.** A high-return/ordinary-synchrony Hopper founder and an ordinary-return/high-synchrony founder.

**Decision.** Retain the useful complement and set an ordinary component outlet beside the premium combination goal.

**Completion.** A brood produces actual individuals. Some may meet ordinary assembly requirements; others justify further selection; replacements fill only the chosen reserve.

**Next optional routes.** Fit a personal assembly, supply an ordinary kit maker, continue a selected branch, or close the project.

**Meaningful failure.** An all-one-role brood or failed attempt remains real. A pre-existing racer and purchased supplies let other play continue rather than requiring the brood to succeed first.

**Material objects.** [W15.OBJECT.BROOD_CARD](#w15-object-brood-card) · [W15.OBJECT.DISPOSITION_CARD](#w15-object-disposition-card)

**Limits.** No genetic probabilities are newly assigned here; species-specific inheritance replaces neither research nor later balancing. The plan creates useful decisions, not guaranteed profitable or target-matching offspring.

<a id="w15-system-care"></a>
### Facilities as controlled commitments rather than perpetual chores

`W15.SYSTEM.CARE`

**Problem.** More facilities or animals can increase recurring obligations faster than the available strategic choices, undermining both casual ownership and productive specialization.

**Design.** Let players choose research, production or maintenance activity using the same biology and owned facilities, with visible bounded commitments.

**Prior reference.** `W03.CAPACITY.03`, `W06.BREED.07`, `W14.GRAFT.CARE`, `W14.BREED.NURSERY`

**Research.** [W15.S05](RRR_Wave_15_Research.md#w15-s05) · [W15.S09](RRR_Wave_15_Research.md#w15-s09)

<a id="w15-care-01"></a>
**W15.CARE.01 — Research, production and maintenance are operating plans, not exclusive buildings or professions. The same suitable nursery can change plans between actual commitments.**

Purpose: Depth comes from use and allocation, not another forced upgrade tier.

<a id="w15-care-02"></a>
**W15.CARE.02 — Research prioritizes observation and selected candidates; production prioritizes a named accepted-output specification and bounded replacements; maintenance stops new reproductive starts and supports existing stock.**

Purpose: Stopping expansion becomes an intelligible option before a cash crisis.

<a id="w15-care-03"></a>
**W15.CARE.03 — Committed living stages and future holding are reserved through actual supported capacity. An expected low survivor count does not provide physical room for a larger viable cohort.**

Purpose: Simplified presentation must preserve real obligations.

<a id="w15-care-04"></a>
**W15.CARE.04 — The breeder can split or schedule production only where the form’s reproductive and handling profile permits it. Any unselected eggs or young still receive a stated disposition; they do not vanish to fit a forecast.**

Purpose: Scheduling is an operational choice, not a disguised biological deletion.

<a id="w15-care-05"></a>
**W15.CARE.05 — A breeder or racer may pay a suitable service for grow-out, supported holding, preservation or temporary boarding. Ownership, delivered stock, fees, responsibility and return conditions are stated.**

Purpose: Different player interests can access expertise without running every facility themselves.

<a id="w15-care-06"></a>
**W15.CARE.06 — A readiness card states the first real blocker, committed care and the next supported transition. It does not show an unobserved exact survivor count disguised as a precise future bill.**

Purpose: Planning remains useful without leaking the engine’s future knowledge.

<a id="w15-care-07"></a>
**W15.CARE.07 — Routine care executes through selected supplies and services without repeated identical manual inputs. A new problem calls for intervention only when it changes an actual outcome.**

Purpose: Large operations create choices about capacity, stock and customers rather than proportional feeding clicks.

<a id="w15-care-08"></a>
**W15.CARE.08 — Offline world time pauses by default. During in-world absence, agreed services and care progress with the same calendar; no life is frozen selectively to evade a contract.**

Purpose: Stopping the application is safe, but ignoring an in-world living obligation is still a decision.

<a id="w15-care-09"></a>
**W15.CARE.09 — Arrears recovery, aging and lasting injury require separate authored material profiles. This milestone permits no hidden disappearance, universal immortality, automatic regeneration or punitive loss added to solve a balance issue.**

Purpose: Consequences remain grounded in the intended world rather than emergency design patches.

**Worked definition.** A successful ordinary-stock line has filled its replacement reserve and lacks funded orders for the next batch.

**Decision.** Switch to maintenance or select a smaller supported commitment; optionally book outside capacity for a genuinely useful order.

**Completion.** Existing stock remains cared for; no new batch starts without consent and capacity.

**Next optional routes.** Race a prepared host, seek a new customer, improve the line or display selected stock.

**Meaningful failure.** Unsold animals cost resources. The plan reveals that obligation before extra production rather than disguising it as passive revenue.

**Material objects.** [W15.OBJECT.CARE_PLAN](#w15-object-care-plan) · [W15.OBJECT.RETURN_CARD](#w15-object-return-card)

**Limits.** This is a service and reference design, not an executed scheduler. Exact care prices, mortality and dormant-storage behavior are unresolved until the relevant biology chapter is authored.

<a id="w15-system-grades"></a>
### Progression in useful differences, not five decorative labels

`W15.SYSTEM.GRADES`

**Problem.** A conjunction-based grade can hide meaningful single-axis improvement, while a small allele alphabet may never populate the advertised intermediate bands.

**Design.** Keep meaningful qualification but make actual improvements, operating variants and recognizable new mechanisms independent progression rewards.

**Prior reference.** `W08.QUALIFY.07`, `W14.MAP.SETS`, `W14.SET.LANGUAGE`, `W12.RECIPE.08`

**Research.** [W15.S01](RRR_Wave_15_Research.md#w15-s01) · [W15.S03](RRR_Wave_15_Research.md#w15-s03) · [W15.S09](RRR_Wave_15_Research.md#w15-s09) · [W15.S12](RRR_Wave_15_Research.md#w15-s12)

<a id="w15-grades-01"></a>
**W15.GRADES.01 — Every product presents four separate questions: what function it supplies, how its observed properties compare, which declared operating profile it meets, and what appearance or history makes it desirable.**

Purpose: Neither rare color nor a high headline band answers every question.

<a id="w15-grades-02"></a>
**W15.GRADES.02 — Within-profile improvement is visible even when the qualification band stays unchanged. Show the actual improved axis and the criterion still limiting the next band.**

Purpose: A better offspring can feel and be useful before crossing a catalogue threshold.

<a id="w15-grades-03"></a>
**W15.GRADES.03 — Hobby, Club, Circuit, Premier and Apex remain names for demonstrated protocols where those steps are meaningful. A donor profile is not required to occupy every label until its supported variation and acquisition routes make them useful.**

Purpose: Do not fabricate reachable progression by relabeling identical anatomy.

<a id="w15-grades-04"></a>
**W15.GRADES.04 — New morphological or preparation variants require changed physical geometry, contact, movement or recognizable collection purpose. Their inheritance, prevalence and preparation belong to the source profile.**

Purpose: A forked horn or channelled pad must be more than a recolored percentage buff.

<a id="w15-grades-05"></a>
**W15.GRADES.05 — Geometry and operating choices remain ranges, not globally higher-better scores. Mechanical advantage, reach and braking friction may require different target bands for different projects.**

Purpose: Maintain useful tuning decisions while allowing actual quality improvement within the chosen operating range.

<a id="w15-grades-06"></a>
**W15.GRADES.06 — A superior part can improve several useful properties at equal burden. Keep that discovery unless a real mechanical interaction explains a limit; do not impose an unrelated weakness as a tax for progress.**

Purpose: The player can legitimately seek the faster roach.

<a id="w15-grades-07"></a>
**W15.GRADES.07 — Review component families against native baselines, affordable alternatives, representative courses and full-build mass/supply. An item-only improvement that is always useless after installation needs redesign or honest specialist positioning.**

Purpose: The part has to matter where players use it.

<a id="w15-grades-08"></a>
**W15.GRADES.08 — Performance qualification does not alter genetics, event legality or buyer payments by itself. Buyers can choose a measured profile as an acceptance shorthand but retain a real purpose and price.**

Purpose: The label informs a transaction; it is not a universal rarity economy.

**Worked definition.** A Glassfoot descendant improves peel release while remaining in the current contact qualification band because dusty purchase is still below the next threshold.

**Decision.** Use the faster release now, retain it as a parent, or sell it for an order concerned with clean track performance.

**Completion.** Its measured difference remains visible and can feed the same installed contact model.

**Next optional routes.** Pursue dusty purchase, a different profile or a visual variant rather than repeating an identical generic upgrade.

**Meaningful failure.** A changed grade name without different anatomy or useful measurement is rejected as progression polish.

**Material objects.** [W15.OBJECT.COMPONENT_CARD](#w15-object-component-card) · [W15.OBJECT.CONVERSION_GUIDE](#w15-object-conversion-guide)

**Limits.** No new trait range, mutation rate or band frequency is treated as calibrated. Comparative use and player recognition must be tested; complete product monographs remain the source of inheritance detail.

<a id="w15-system-livelihood"></a>
### Three distinct maker businesses, not one subsidized crafting funnel

`W15.SYSTEM.LIVELIHOOD`

**Problem.** The documented first maker project succeeded in delivery and lost cash; calling its sale passive income would conceal a real incentive problem.

**Design.** Design distinct service, manufactured-kit and breeder-supply businesses around their actual value and costs, with an accessible earning route that does not require breeding.

**Prior reference.** `W12.DEMAND.10`, `W12.REPAIR.07`, `W14.INSTITUTION.FITTER`, `W14.INSTITUTION.CINDER`

**Research.** [W15.S04](RRR_Wave_15_Research.md#w15-s04) · [W15.S09](RRR_Wave_15_Research.md#w15-s09) · [W15.S10](RRR_Wave_15_Research.md#w15-s10)

<a id="w15-livelihood-01"></a>
**W15.LIVELIHOOD.01 — Customer-stock fitting is a first-class commission. The client supplies and retains ownership of a named host and components; the fitter is paid for agreed preparation, arrangement and evidence.**

Purpose: A player can earn through building expertise without purchasing and financing an entire second racer.

<a id="w15-livelihood-02"></a>
**W15.LIVELIHOOD.02 — A manufactured-kit order purchases actual material stock, the living foundation, preparation, coordination and guide. A template match alone is not delivered work.**

Purpose: Repeatable production has a product people can assemble, not an abstract crafting completion.

<a id="w15-livelihood-03"></a>
**W15.LIVELIHOOD.03 — A breeder-supply order purchases live eligible animals and the stated evidence. It is priced and accepted separately from their potential extracted parts.**

Purpose: Fertility, compatible roles and reliable output can have economic value independent of peak race performance.

<a id="w15-livelihood-04"></a>
**W15.LIVELIHOOD.04 — Quote three separate quantities: incremental cost of the next delivery, allocated ongoing business overhead, and still-unrecovered research/capital spending. Purchased inputs, own-stock opportunity value and consumed supplies are labeled distinctly.**

Purpose: A profitable next batch and a profitable lifetime project are not the same claim.

<a id="w15-livelihood-05"></a>
**W15.LIVELIHOOD.05 — A fixed contract price is agreed before irreversible production. A forecast shows what assumptions make it affordable; the customer does not magically reimburse bad breeding outcomes or choose a hidden perfect sale value.**

Purpose: Risk remains meaningful and understandable.

<a id="w15-livelihood-06"></a>
**W15.LIVELIHOOD.06 — Where current terms cannot pay a competent repeat supplier for its necessary costs, redesign the quantity, input sourcing, service scope or buyer budget. Do not hide the loss as experience or relabel consumed working stock as permanent capital.**

Purpose: At least one deliberate ordinary livelihood must actually support upkeep under reasonable play.

<a id="w15-livelihood-07"></a>
**W15.LIVELIHOOD.07 — Kit-maker source requirements match the intended use. A starter commission does not demand exhaustive ancestry certification; a premium breeder claim may require multiple observed broods.**

Purpose: Evidence cost belongs where it buys relevant confidence.

<a id="w15-livelihood-08"></a>
**W15.LIVELIHOOD.08 — Retail and wholesale prices reflect different real duties: selection, care, presentation, warranty or fitting work, and customer demand. Same-counter posted-order spread checks may support transaction safety but do not define every world price.**

Purpose: Fix the economic cause instead of increasing all prices whenever a player finds an opportunity.

<a id="w15-livelihood-09"></a>
**W15.LIVELIHOOD.09 — Legitimate resale from place, timing, information, preparation or service can earn money. Repeating the same transfer without new value, a changed real buyer need or new funding cannot generate an endless self-financed return.**

Purpose: Commerce stays interesting without ledger exploits or blanket anti-trading restrictions.

<a id="w15-livelihood-10"></a>
**W15.LIVELIHOOD.10 — Compare buyer-only, catcher, breeder and fitter routes with matched starting resources, available time and product objective. Include failed attempts, care of leftovers, resale opportunities and retained assets separately.**

Purpose: Different route schedules are not evidence that one profession is superior.

**Worked definition.** An independent fitter receives a client-owned Lowline and two measured compatible drive/contact options.

**Decision.** Agree which construction or matched measurement is wanted, inspect actual supplied condition and quote the work.

**Completion.** The fitted host and actual observation return to the customer; a service receipt pays labor and consumed supplies.

**Next optional routes.** Accept another job, use earnings for personal parts, or develop a complete commercial kit with separately sourced inventory.

**Meaningful failure.** If the supplied component cannot meet the requested outcome, decline or renegotiate before destructive work; no fabricated improvement is awarded.

**Material objects.** [W15.OBJECT.CUSTOMER_STOCK_BRIEF](#w15-object-customer-stock-brief) · [W15.OBJECT.QUOTE_SHEET](#w15-object-quote-sheet)

**Limits.** No new price list, profit rate or customer budget is claimed as balanced. W12’s -38-credit result remains unchanged evidence. A revised definition is not a retroactive refund.

### Ordinary stock and alternate anatomy have named uses

`W15.SYSTEM.SURPLUS`

**Problem.** Ordinary catches can be nominally saleable yet have no available buyer; a research-oriented interface can also classify every non-target specimen as useless.

**Design.** Make quick disposal of genuine surplus useful without turning every living thing into guaranteed cash or erasing valuable anatomical alternatives.

<a id="w15-surplus-01"></a>
**W15.SURPLUS.01 — Stock is evaluated separately for immediate assembly, breeding contribution, funded product demand, collection and remaining material. One missing target does not automatically fail every purpose.**

Purpose: The keep-good/sell-unwanted rhythm becomes informed rather than rarity-only.

<a id="w15-surplus-02"></a>
**W15.SURPLUS.02 — Ordinary buyers publish the forms, stages, intact anatomy and quantities they need for starter stock, replacement components or other stated uses. Their budgets come from actual trade or an explicit authored economic boundary.**

Purpose: Common stock has deliberate demand instead of an unbounded money-conversion slot.

<a id="w15-surplus-03"></a>
**W15.SURPLUS.03 — Alternative preparation outlets name the exact region and useful measurements. A poor complete jumper may provide acceptable landing joints; low drive value does not prove that a scarab’s separate plates are poor.**

Purpose: Brood and field surplus can feed more than the premium goal.

<a id="w15-surplus-04"></a>
**W15.SURPLUS.04 — Damaged stock can be unsuitable for one product and still valuable as a breeder, display object or supported alternate preparation. Only actually remaining usable anatomy can be offered.**

Purpose: Damage is local and consequential, not an all-purpose zero-price tag.

<a id="w15-surplus-05"></a>
**W15.SURPLUS.05 — An unmarketable animal is not assigned an invented universal feed or salvage buyer. Retention, supported placement, release where the authored ecology allows it, or another declared disposition remains necessary.**

Purpose: The economy has limits without making discarded anatomy reappear.

<a id="w15-surplus-06"></a>
**W15.SURPLUS.06 — Quick-sale previews the actual eligible identities and selected buyer quote, excluding protected founders, chosen displays, committed operations, kits and installed parts. The player may deliberately override a relevant protection.**

Purpose: Routine volume is easy while rare or personally important stock remains under control.

<a id="w15-surplus-07"></a>
**W15.SURPLUS.07 — When a buyer is temporarily full, offer only real alternatives: another known purpose or buyer, later scheduled demand, preparation, or retention. A persistent shortage signals a production decision, not an automatic universal price collapse.**

Purpose: Saturation creates strategy without shutting every earning route through one wallet.

<a id="w15-surplus-08"></a>
**W15.SURPLUS.08 — Return summaries attribute money, ongoing home sales, care, captures and knowledge separately. Repeated appraisals do not reroll the animal or generate additional stock.**

Purpose: The player can identify whether a run, a brood or an independent sale paid for the next upgrade.

**Decision.** Reserve the first for a line, sell the ordinary donor against actual stock demand, and evaluate the damaged animal’s reproductive or alternate remaining-anatomy use.

**Completion.** All three receive explicit dispositions; the sale is one real receipt and the retained animals remain obligations.

**Next optional routes.** Use proceeds immediately, continue the line or obtain an assay only where it could change a decision.

**Meaningful failure.** A requested intact complete assembly cannot be fulfilled by relabeling the damaged donor.

**Material objects.** [W15.OBJECT.DISPOSITION_CARD](#w15-object-disposition-card) · [W15.OBJECT.ORDINARY_LOT_CARD](#w15-object-ordinary-lot-card)

**Limits.** Prices and volume must be calibrated jointly with stock generation and care. No ordinary route is guaranteed profit on every outing.

<a id="w15-customers-08"></a>

<a id="w15-system-sport"></a>
### A permanent local hobby inside an earned championship ladder

`W15.SYSTEM.SPORT`

**Problem.** Finite funded prototype editions and long mirrored programmes risk becoming the only meaning of racing, leaving no ordinary return loop after the player finishes a list.

**Design.** Keep local competition and experimentation continuous while funding, special prizes and official advancement remain meaningful.

**Prior reference.** `W11.MONEY.08`, `W11.FORMAT.01`, `W14.SPORT.LADDER`, `W14.INSTITUTION.SEASONS`

<a id="w15-sport-01"></a>
**W15.SPORT.01 — Separate bench trials, casual practice/rival races, funded meets, qualifying events and exhibitions. Each states which outcome it records and which reward or advancement it can actually award.**

Purpose: Every real finish matters within its context without paying an infinite purse.

<a id="w15-sport-02"></a>
**W15.SPORT.02 — Clubs continue practice, informal rivalry, trade and display outside a championship edition. Availability depends on the place and its resources, not exhaustion of a three-entry prototype list.**

Purpose: The world-popular hobby remains after a player completes a particular programme.

<a id="w15-sport-03"></a>
**W15.SPORT.03 — A renewed sponsored event requires funding, participants, usable course and operating support. Its new edition can award a new real prize; replay of an old edition cannot.**

Purpose: Repeat participation is genuine activity, not duplicated settlement.

<a id="w15-sport-04"></a>
**W15.SPORT.04 — The championship path remains Locals, Regionals, Factionals and representation at New World City. Those qualifications control their competitions, not the existence or purchase of genes, tools or donor forms.**

Purpose: Competitive prestige does not force every hobbyist into the same career.

<a id="w15-sport-05"></a>
**W15.SPORT.05 — Open preserves build freedom; Speedsters restrict dedicated weapons; Fireworks requires functional offense and light carapace; sustained Flyers race Flyers. No runtime absence deletes a division from the reference universe.**

Purpose: The original class identities survive later technical choices.

<a id="w15-sport-06"></a>
**W15.SPORT.06 — Every event monograph specifies actual field interaction, roster, starting treatment, allowed between-round changes, progression and intended session burden. Sequential mirrored pairs are one option, not a claim of simultaneous pack physics or a universal beginner format.**

Purpose: Sporting purpose determines the programme; a solver convenience does not dictate the whole world.

<a id="w15-sport-07"></a>
**W15.SPORT.07 — A loss preserves its truthful classification and can still produce observed performance, a personal record or relevant experience. It does not automatically pay a consolation purse or guarantee useful stock.**

Purpose: Feedback supports another attempt without making defeat meaningless.

<a id="w15-sport-08"></a>
**W15.SPORT.08 — Preparation information is accessible before entry. Rivals use declared constructions and bounded knowledge rather than reading the player’s hidden genome for a perfect counter.**

Purpose: Improvement and inspection reward actual understanding.

<a id="w15-sport-09"></a>
**W15.SPORT.09 — A season introduces new opponents, published layouts, stock activity and product interests without erasing the player’s specimens, line work or permanent records. Offline default pause remains.**

Purpose: There is an inviting reason to return without a compulsory reset or real-world attendance streak.

**Worked definition.** A local Open champion has claimed that edition’s donor reward and spent its remaining sponsored entries.

**Decision.** Continue ordinary club racing, prepare for the earned Regional event, host an exhibition or campaign another build.

**Completion.** A new real event records new results; an exhausted prize edition remains historical.

**Next optional routes.** A new funded programme or different local activity provides a fresh challenge when actually available.

**Meaningful failure.** The reference does not allow a player-authored easy course or old replay to mint official qualification.

**Material objects.** [W15.OBJECT.COURSE_BRIEF](#w15-object-course-brief) · [W15.OBJECT.PROGRAMME_CARD](#w15-object-programme-card) · [W15.OBJECT.RECIPIENT_CARD](#w15-object-recipient-card)

<a id="construction-families"></a>
## 5. Eight construction families: immediate use, long projects and customers

These refine existing families rather than add eight disconnected mechanics. Every construction has an immediate intended pleasure, a purposeful source/breeding path, useful intermediate stock and at least one case where its special contribution is not useful. No entire source-game coefficient table is invented.

<a id="w15-family-tin"></a>
### Tin Can Special — finish a personally owned racer

**Existing entries.** `W14.SET.TIN`, `W14.ANATOMY.HOST`

**Source function.** Chosen vehicle/body, complete kit, appearance and local racing.

**Construction.** A suitable Lowline or declared alternative host, the printed first contact or guide upgrade, and explicit retained native functions.

**Immediate payoff.** Complete the actual assembly, choose a name/finish, and enter an ordinary run.

**What to seek and why.** Service-gallery hosts are selected for useful proportions and native characteristics; everyday kits buy consistent appropriate stock rather than requiring rare founders.

**Intermediate progress.** One later contact, drive or handling change can be useful before a full specialist conversion is owned.

**Who wants the output.** Two Nets buys dependable whole hosts and ordinary compatible preparations for affordable complete tins.

**Representative opportunities.** Native-ready first run; Same-host before/after one contact change; Personal finishing or a local rival

**Disconfirming case.** A native-only host may be preferable to a badly fitted upgrade; a beginner kit must not require every organ replaced.

**Visible recognition.** The owner recognizes the same named animal on the bench and starting line; the finish and actual race result are not buried by future tasks.

<a id="w15-family-needle"></a>
### Needle & Thread — pace that can be used

**Existing entries.** `W14.SET.NEEDLE`, `W14.PART.REDLINE`, `W14.PART.STICKTOE`

**Source function.** Motor, tire/contact choice and body geometry.

**Construction.** Narrow host, useful drive musculature, quick-release feet and retained native roles.

**Immediate payoff.** Observe faster useful stepping or a genuine matched-run improvement from the chosen contribution.

**What to seek and why.** Root-furnace cicadas provide cadence/force/efficiency; Glassfoot stock provides purchase/release. Seek the actual bottleneck, not the loudest insect or highest adhesion.

**Intermediate progress.** An efficient ordinary-cadence drive or a faster-releasing ordinary-grip foot can be a useful donor, direct component or complementary breeder.

**Who wants the output.** Sprint-kit makers can buy measured contact pairs or drives independently; a fitting customer may already own the good motor.

**Representative opportunities.** Straight acceleration with adequate supply; A contact-limited build before and after release improvement; A populated bend with real passing width

**Disconfirming case.** A better motor cannot force a stuck foot to release or erase inadequate supply; this does not make all superior motors compulsory sidegrades.

**Visible recognition.** Stride cadence, slipping and release are readable; a comparison identifies the changed component rather than an unexplained overall power tier.

<a id="w15-family-glass"></a>
### Glassline — hold, release, then correct

**Existing entries.** `W14.SET.GLASS`, `W14.PART.STICKTOE`, `W14.PART.GYROPODS`, `W14.PART.COUNTERBRACES`

**Source function.** Contact compound and stabilizing support serve different purposes.

**Construction.** Useful foot material plus sensing connected to available response anatomy; a brace is a separately chosen physical support construction.

**Immediate payoff.** The feet retain useful contact while cycling, and a sensed disturbance produces an observable available correction.

**What to seek and why.** Glazed Overgrowth supplies contact stock; Damp Service Voids supplies paired sensor anatomy. Select a missing contact contribution or useful latency rather than a shared rarity color.

**Intermediate progress.** A moderate-grip/fast-release founder can improve an already gripping line; ordinary pairs can serve beginner contacts or alternate brake strips.

**Who wants the output.** Two Nets supplies contact sets; fitters align paired sensors; appropriate material can enter Apron brake orders instead of pretending all output is premium feet.

**Representative opportunities.** A load where feet can hold and release; A disturbance with an available corrective limb; The same disturbance with that limb committed elsewhere

**Disconfirming case.** An excellent sensor does not supply force or create contact while suspended; a brace must reach a real surface.

**Visible recognition.** The contact, cue and correction are distinct visible events, not three identical balance effects.

<a id="w15-family-heavy"></a>
### Foundry Heavy / Lowstep — working force through a supported body

**Existing entries.** `W14.SET.HEAVY`, `W14.PART.TORQUE`, `W14.PART.LOWSTEP`, `W14.PART.BOXBRACE`, `W14.PART.CLEATS`

**Source function.** Motor force, gear ratio, chassis support and contact material.

**Construction.** Suitable loaded host; Torque Bundle; target-range Lowstep; oriented Boxbrace; actual surface-appropriate feet.

**Immediate payoff.** Improved launch, climbing or recovery under the declared load where the construction has adequate contact.

**What to seek and why.** Cooling compost scarabs for specific force/support; Ballast crickets for suitable leverage, cleats and articulation. A bigger ratio is a different operating choice, not a universal tier.

**Intermediate progress.** A stock animal may supply disjoint hip/toe products or drive/support material when preserved. Unneeded high-force stock may suit another host rather than be discarded.

**Who wants the output.** Cinderhouse can supply useful force and support separately; local loose-surface clubs create ordinary cleat demand; fitters earn for correct load paths.

**Representative opportunities.** Loaded restart; Climb with sufficient ground purchase; Loose-surface recovery after contact

**Disconfirming case.** Long uninterrupted straights can expose limited stride travel. Unsupported plates do not provide useful root bracing or whole-body armor.

**Visible recognition.** The body pushes and recovers visibly; the working structure remains coherent under load rather than simply displaying a larger strength number.

<a id="w15-family-hop"></a>
### Last Laugh / Third Friend — source, breed, build, leap, return

**Existing entries.** `W14.SET.HOP`, `W14.PART.HOPPERS`, `W14.PART.LOCKSTEP`, `W14.PART.FATBODY`, `W14.PART.KEEL`

**Source function.** Useful power delivery, suspension and damping from PCR/Tamiya, integrated with RRR-native active jumping.

**Construction.** Complete launch-capable hind system; adequate work and reserve; working orientation; planned receiving contact; separate damper only where included.

**Immediate payoff.** A properly timed hop clears an actual obstacle or rival and returns the animal to useful running.

**What to seek and why.** Switchyard stock is selected independently for return, synchronization and settling. A complete kit can be bought; a complementary field founder can serve a longer project.

**Intermediate progress.** Plausible synchronization → observed descendant expression → usable combined individual → repeated qualified output. Ordinary assemblies and joint-only preparations remain valid before perfection.

**Who wants the output.** Third Friend tins, selected founder requests, ordinary landing parts and measured fitting work use different outcomes from the same project.

**Representative opportunities.** Clear legal crossing with no rival required; Closing pass with a viable load/release window; Landing under matched stored work and body mass

**Disconfirming case.** A ceiling, inaccessible takeoff or occupied landing can invalidate that opportunity. No auto-pass compensates for poor mechanics, and no single selected gene guarantees the entire action.

**Visible recognition.** Loading, release, air passage, receiving contact and recovery are visible; useful intermediate behavior can be noticed before a full-lap advantage.

<a id="w15-family-apron"></a>
### Apron — useful contact in two different places

**Existing entries.** `W14.SET.APRON`, `W14.PART.FELTBRAKE`, `W14.PART.GRIPSTRIP`, `W14.PART.RAILSHOES`

**Source function.** Physical-hobby contact brakes and wall guides, not additional hidden Gaiden slots.

**Construction.** A pad with real clearance/compliance and a supported rail guide from separate required material.

**Immediate payoff.** Approach contact removes excessive speed; guide contact reduces loss at an actual wall.

**What to seek and why.** Sandfelt stock from Feltworks for material/compression; darklings from Underpipe Aprons for sliding surface/support; Glassfoot pads can be allocated to Gripstrips alternatively.

**Intermediate progress.** A dependable middle operating range can serve many ordinary builds. Better low-loss surface at adequate support is a genuine improvement; larger friction alone is not.

**Who wants the output.** Material specialists supply stock; a fitter sells engagement/alignment; a trackmaker provides a course where multiple setup solutions are possible.

**Representative opportunities.** Same approach with pad engaged and raised; Same rail encounter with supported guide and native shell; Same guide away from the rail

**Disconfirming case.** A long safe landing or another approach can make the pad unnecessary. A noncontact guide has no speed aura. Passive launch does not spend a nonexistent Hopper charge.

**Visible recognition.** The visible point of contact precedes the effect; the result is not inferred from equipment presence.

<a id="w15-family-fire"></a>
### Flashboil — race-winning contact, not an elimination mode

**Existing entries.** `W14.SET.FIRE`, `W14.PART.BOILER`, `W14.PART.CATCHCLAWS`, `W14.PART.RAM`, `W14.PART.NECTAR`

**Source function.** Racing power, supply and physical construction extended by RRR’s original derby identity.

**Construction.** Light protective construction; working offense; supported roots and finite action resources; native or supplied ordinary movement.

**Immediate payoff.** A timed physical interaction opens or protects race position while the racer continues toward a valid finish.

**What to seek and why.** Mantis closing time at working reach, horn-root support/contact geometry, or dependable bombardier valves are distinct breeder goals. A collector may still prefer a different striking form.

**Intermediate progress.** An ordinary dependable arm or valve can serve a local build; a premium research line can pursue range/timing combinations without making ordinary attacks unusable.

**Who wants the output.** Fireworks builders, repair/material suppliers and specialist kit makers purchase specific functional assemblies.

**Representative opportunities.** Reachable aligned contact; A missed early/late commitment; A successful contact whose time cost still matters to the race

**Disconfirming case.** A distant opponent cannot be damaged by completing an animation; dedicated weapons remain excluded in Speedsters; finish order, not kill count, decides the race.

**Visible recognition.** Wind-up, reach, contact, effect on both racers and recovery remain visible. Spectacle does not require obscuring the race with constant effects.

<a id="w15-family-sky"></a>
### Skyhook — the same hobby expressed through flight

**Existing entries.** `W14.SET.SKY`, `W14.PART.QUADWING`, `W14.PART.LONGWING`, `W14.PART.NECTAR`

**Source function.** The kit’s output, delivery, structure, reserve and control roles remain; powered flight is RRR-native, not the passive front-wing slot.

**Construction.** Host; working flight surfaces/roots; drive and wingstroke linkage; adequate supply; control; launch and landing provisions.

**Immediate payoff.** Assemble a genuine aerial racer that traverses a declared gate course against other flyers.

**What to seek and why.** Pond/emergence quality, wing-root behavior, sustained work and appropriate reproductive stock create different sourcing needs from ground kits.

**Intermediate progress.** Ordinary intact wing sets, root preparations and complete starter flight kits permit participation while experts pursue coordinated high-output constructions.

**Who wants the output.** Pond breeders, emergence services, kit makers, carriers and Flyer venues all have distinct useful outputs.

**Representative opportunities.** Sustained legal flight; A controlled turn/gate approach; Safe supported landing

**Disconfirming case.** A raised ground sprite, passive foil or prolonged hop is not powered flight. The full donor life history and aerial implementation remain future work.

**Visible recognition.** The gate, wing-root action and body attitude explain the maneuver; this remains a compendium acceptance requirement, not a claim of a new playable division.

<a id="physical-objects"></a>
## 6. Physical reference objects and service briefs

These are content briefs for the hobby’s tangible objects and their interaction meaning. They are not eighteen compulsory forms, menus or new currencies. Several can share one illustrated page or work surface.

<a id="w15-object-project-card"></a>
### A chosen project, not a compulsory quest log

**Physical object.** A small card clipped to a racer case, donor tank or workshop project. Its front bears the actual creature or intended construction; the back carries optional technical notes.

**Information carried.** Desired result; Present useful stock; Missing contribution or next construction decision; Existing evidence; Chosen next action; Optional spending/space boundary

**Use.** A racer can keep “make this Needleback quicker out of the bend”; a breeder can keep “add synchronization”; a collector can keep an appearance target.

**Decision.** Choose an intention without reserving every vaguely related animal or accepting a timed contract.

**Complete outcome.** Cross out or archive the completed intention while retaining the actual object and result.

**Boundary.** It grants no stock, automatic diagnosis, hidden genetics or mandatory objective chain.

<a id="w15-object-first-tin"></a>
### The first personally completed racer

**Physical object.** An illustrated complete-host tin with a sheltered living compartment, shaped preparation wells, a numbered fitting sheet and room for a name and race number.

**Information carried.** Included host; Actual prepared components; Explicit native roles; Ordinary supported arrangement; Required supplies included or quoted; Optional first conversion

**Use.** A Wrangloid sells a coherent starter project; the owner assembles and finishes it before or during a supported club visit.

**Decision.** Choose silhouette, a recognizable racing intention and personal finish; use the guide or alter it.

**Complete outcome.** A named, physically fitted racer completes a first actual run. Victory is possible, not guaranteed.

**Boundary.** A “starter” label does not require uninteresting native-only racing, a compulsory breeding tutorial or a guaranteed win.

<a id="w15-object-session-card"></a>
### Foundry bench-session card

**Physical object.** A reusable work card placed beside the host cradle, with a clear allowance area and replaceable strips for quoted additional work.

**Information carried.** Host and working components; Booked physical time; Included ordinary fitting/testing allowance; Consumables actually committed; Current change from the baseline; Separately authorized extra work

**Use.** Groups one useful visit into a comprehensible service rather than selling every interface gesture as a separate appointment.

**Decision.** Try ready owned components, adjust a setup and compare within the stated session; approve new preparation or extra time explicitly.

**Complete outcome.** Leave with a committed build and a readable comparison, or restore the prior arrangement using the same retained material.

**Boundary.** The allowance is finite physical service; no infinite live tests, free resource refills or exemption from care outside its terms.

<a id="w15-object-comparison-slip"></a>
### Paired build-comparison slip

**Physical object.** Two adjacent silhouettes of the same racer or specified compared hosts, with the changed anatomy outlined and the finish result printed first.

**Information carried.** Reference version; Candidate version; Changed inputs; Fixed course and conditions; Observed result; Visible event; Uncertainty and untested causes

**Use.** Connects an ordinary swap to something the player can see and understand without demanding a laboratory report.

**Decision.** Keep the improvement, revise the actual cause of the failure, or deliberately return to the earlier build.

**Complete outcome.** The owner can state what changed and what happened; old results remain frozen.

**Boundary.** A different rival, body and surface cannot be presented as an isolated gene comparison. Replaying a slip is not new attendance.

<a id="w15-object-course-brief"></a>
### A course invitation with useful construction information

**Physical object.** A legible sectional course drawing with scale/connector notes in the margin, major surfaces distinguished, and a small space for the host club’s identity.

**Information carried.** Layout version; Distance and required route; Surface and clearance features; Shared versus guided space; Launch/landing features; Event purpose; Several plausible construction responses

**Use.** Lets a novice recognize a fast straight or short landing and lets a specialist inspect dimensions and contact opportunities.

**Decision.** Choose a racer and configuration without receiving a secret list of mandatory branded components.

**Complete outcome.** The posted physical conditions match the course actually raced.

**Boundary.** A structurally valid layout does not promise every build will finish. Illustrative counterplay is not an exhaustive solution list.

<a id="w15-object-field-brief"></a>
### A purpose-led collecting brief

**Physical object.** A field card combining a habitat sketch, one anatomical detail to protect, and the current project’s missing contribution.

**Information carried.** Named source population; Target form and useful stage; Visible signs; What can be observed on site; Tools and transport; Ordinary uses; Supported return handling

**Use.** Connects a component desire to a place and a particular kind of observation, not to a generic rare-drop icon.

**Decision.** Choose detail, quick-route representation or buying instead; within the outing choose an individual and preservation method.

**Complete outcome.** Returns actual stock and reusable knowledge, including an honest result when the desired specimen was not found.

**Boundary.** A project cannot rewrite the source population or certify an unmeasured trait; quick mode cannot duplicate the same available cohort.

<a id="w15-object-disposition-card"></a>
### Return tray with explicit competing uses

**Physical object.** A divided receiving tray whose removable labels read Use, Research, Replacement, Ordinary sale and Display; each real specimen occupies one physical holding location.

**Information carried.** Specimen identity; Visible condition and stage; Known contribution; Protected or reserved use; Alternative preparations; Actual buyer/quote if any; Continuing care

**Use.** Supports fast handling of ordinary volume and deliberate review of a few promising or personally valued animals.

**Decision.** Choose a use based on purpose rather than one global quality number; move a specimen between plans deliberately.

**Complete outcome.** A completed batch leaves ownership, anatomy, care and cash mutually consistent.

**Boundary.** Several possible uses are not several copies. “Sale candidate” is not already sold, and unmeasured does not mean bad.

<a id="w15-object-brood-card"></a>
### Split-brood project and production card

**Physical object.** A cohort label mounted on the actual enclosure, with separate bands for the current stage, the target contribution, and planned mature-stock destinations.

**Information carried.** Parents and line; Target contribution; Observed stage; Real capacity commitment; Research review quota; Replacement target and current reserve; Personal/product/sale intentions; Evidence after observation

**Use.** Keeps a longer breeding project intelligible while existing racers and ordinary output remain useful now.

**Decision.** Continue a research branch, fill a replacement gap, use an intermediate or sell appropriate surplus.

**Complete outcome.** Records an observed milestone without calling every nonpremium animal a failed project.

**Boundary.** Disposition numbers are intentions, not promised sexes, survival, genotype or receipts; the model cannot invent an absent compatible pair.

<a id="w15-object-care-plan"></a>
### Care and maintenance agreement

**Physical object.** A durable enclosure/case label with the responsible caretaker and supplied conditions on its face, with the next real handoff written on a replaceable strip.

**Information carried.** Actual animals or preparations; Required material/stage conditions; Supplies and responsible service; Committed duration; New starts allowed or paused; Space and receiving plan; Named failure/return conditions

**Use.** Lets a hobbyist keep a stable or production line without manually operating every routine.

**Decision.** Select research, production or maintenance; obtain suitable boarding or handling where useful.

**Complete outcome.** The animals remain in real supported care while the owner pursues another activity.

**Boundary.** No universal preservation rule, selective frozen world clock, automatic regrowth or hidden unpaid service is implied.

<a id="w15-object-return-card"></a>
### A clean stopping point

**Physical object.** A bookmark in the player’s project book or a card resting on the racer case; the front shows the completed achievement rather than warning icons.

**Information carried.** Last completed result; Self-chosen next intention; Actual stock involved; Live obligations; Paused or active future plans; Actionable blocker if any

**Use.** Supports ending and resuming a session without a memory test or a housekeeping ritual.

**Decision.** Keep the intention, change it, or do something else when returning.

**Complete outcome.** The player can stop after a satisfying action with the world’s state preserved and default offline time paused.

**Boundary.** No streak, missed-login penalty, fabricated catch or retroactive care bill creates the reason to return.

<a id="w15-object-component-card"></a>
### A readable part card with actual progress

**Physical object.** A prepared-sect tag that leads with the anatomical silhouette and functional name. Measurements sit beneath their purpose; identity and detailed provenance are on the reverse.

**Information carried.** Source and preparation; Useful function; Known measured axes and protocol; Current condition; Fit envelope; Qualification when supported; Compared change on the chosen purpose

**Use.** Shows why a particular part is useful even when it has not crossed the next qualification boundary.

**Decision.** Use, retain, improve, sell or compare the component for a named construction.

**Complete outcome.** A meaningful measured improvement is visible without secretly changing performance to match a new label.

**Boundary.** One score cannot substitute for geometry, rarity, breeding value, condition, appearance and all race purposes.

<a id="w15-object-conversion-guide"></a>
### A set as a sequence of usable changes

**Physical object.** An exploded overlay aligned to the existing host, with each real component shown once and its separate function marked.

**Information carried.** Reference host envelope; Real component requirements; Native functions retained; Order of reversible changes; Complete intermediate builds; Final arrangement; Alternative supported preparations

**Use.** A coordinated set offers an enjoyable complete project but also intelligible intermediate upgrades when its pieces are acquired separately.

**Decision.** Build the supplied set or take one useful conversion while retaining other native anatomy.

**Complete outcome.** Each declared intermediate is a functional animal, and the final set has an identifiable mechanical purpose.

**Boundary.** No invisible same-brand aura; no duplicate tissue for multiple roles; a partial set is not automatically worthless.

<a id="w15-object-customer-stock-brief"></a>
### Customer-owned-stock fitting brief

**Physical object.** A work envelope containing the customer’s host and component identities, a drawing of the desired change and a signed handling boundary.

**Information carried.** Customer goal; Actual supplied stock; Handling authority; Working constraints; Promised service; Agreed fee; Acceptance and return

**Use.** Allows an expert builder to earn with skills and facilities before financing a breeding and retail operation.

**Decision.** Accept achievable fitting work, request suitable missing stock, or decline an unsupported promise.

**Complete outcome.** Performed work, observations and the same assets return to the customer for the agreed fee.

**Boundary.** No guaranteed victory, genome improvement or sale of client-owned material without explicit permission.

<a id="w15-object-quote-sheet"></a>
### A quote that separates the business being sold

**Physical object.** A simple counter sheet with separate columns for discovery/design, repeat product work, client property, and cash due.

**Information carried.** Service/manufacturing/breeder offering; Inputs actually purchased; Care and labor; Research or reference-development costs; Delivered-output specification; Buyer budget and acceptance; Stock retained; Cash and capital exposure

**Use.** Prevents an attractive gross payment from concealing a loss-making repeat operation or falsely billing every research expense as per-unit production.

**Decision.** Choose a fit-only job, complete manufacturing, living-stock supply or no order.

**Complete outcome.** The seller can identify what work earns the margin and what risks remain outside the quote.

**Boundary.** No arbitrary reclassification of the old 150-credit example; no unsupported claim that future scale makes a negative margin profitable.

<a id="w15-object-ordinary-lot-card"></a>
### Useful ordinary supply lot

**Physical object.** A tank or tray label with a modest product sketch and a clear acceptance statement; no premium pedigree crest is required.

**Information carried.** Form/stage; Intact or explicitly altered anatomy; Product or whole-stock use; Buyer and finite quantity; Ordinary acceptance; Quote and holding needs

**Use.** Supplies beginner kits, routine repairs, alternative preparations and affordable projects from appropriate nonpremium stock.

**Decision.** Sell now, prepare for a real order, or retain for a different purpose.

**Complete outcome.** An accepted physical lot changes owner for an actual payment.

**Boundary.** Every unwanted animal need not have a profitable outlet. Damaged stock cannot be disguised as intact standard supply.

<a id="w15-object-recipient-card"></a>
### The product comes back as a recognizable relationship

**Physical object.** A customer’s labelled build photograph or illustration attached to the actual product receipt, with a brief note about its use and permission to display it.

**Information carried.** Delivered host/parts; Customer purpose; Permitted public details; Actual use or display; Specific feedback; Any new requested work; Budget if a new agreement exists

**Use.** A kit maker can see the same stock in somebody else’s construction; a collector can see commissioned work displayed.

**Decision.** Respond with fitting advice, a revision, replacement stock, another agreed product or no further transaction.

**Complete outcome.** The delivered asset has a visible downstream use while the producer’s reference remains intact.

**Boundary.** The card is not automatic five-star praise, a forced sale, a universal reputation multiplier or proof that a genetic line caused a win.

<a id="w15-object-programme-card"></a>
### Persistent club programme beside special editions

**Physical object.** A wall card or handbill separating ordinary open sessions, a published special event, display dates and the championship route.

**Information carried.** Venue and access; Ordinary practice/rivals; Event purpose and format; Known roster; Course version; Funding and fees; Qualification consequence; Available services

**Use.** Makes a world-popular hobby available beyond a few finite prize editions, with practical occasions to use different constructions.

**Decision.** Race casually, enter an actual funded competition, book a workshop, display work or help host.

**Complete outcome.** A declared activity delivers its result; ordinary participation continues when a special purse ends.

**Boundary.** A recurring programme is not an infinite unbacked prize pool or a mandatory real-world appointment.

<a id="w15-object-display-label"></a>
### An honest collection identity

**Physical object.** A case label composed around the creature or original material: the title is prominent, provenance and replacement history are legible, and technical detail does not cover the object.

**Information carried.** Name and object; Natural versus applied appearance; Original/descendant/reproduced status; Retained or replaced components; Owner-approved history; Current condition; Display purpose

**Use.** Supports finishing, restoration, collections and pride in old work without inventing a speed justification.

**Decision.** Present a particular aspect, seek a complementary piece, commission a finish or leave the object private.

**Complete outcome.** A coherent collection or finished piece exists as the outcome, whether or not it is sold.

**Boundary.** A striking pattern is not automatically heritable, historically authentic or a championship-quality component.

<a id="worked-paths"></a>
## 7. Six cross-period reference paths

These are authored decision sequences for downstream design. They do not report a newly executed playtest or promise a particular random outcome. Each has a complete stopping point and an optional continuation.

<a id="w15-path-buy-build"></a>
### Buy, build and race without a second occupation

**Three-period connection.** 0-5: complete personal kit and understandable performance; 5-10: host/parts and actual stable; 10-15: club result, comparison and optional public identity

**Required real starting objects.** An ordinary available complete kit; A local bench/course service; The customer’s actual purchase budget

| Step | Decision / change |
|---|---|
| 1 | Choose a Tin Can Special or an appropriate founder-style kit by appearance, contents and intended racing—not by a forced introductory production class. |
| 2 | Assemble the supplied anatomy, retain the explicit native roles, name and finish the host. The guide has complete build checkpoints rather than requiring the entire catalogue learned first. |
| 3 | Use a booked bench session to inspect one useful setting and take an ordinary run. No donor breeding, product publication or assay certificate is a prerequisite. |
| 4 | Celebrate the actual finish. If the run suggests a desired change, compare one ready part or setup and leave with a committed construction. |
| 5 | Record an optional next intention: a rival challenge, a shop comparison, a field source, a visual finish or nothing yet. |

**Completion.** A personally completed, recognizable racer exists. A real run/result exists; the first run need not be a win. The user can choose a next interest without accepting all deeper systems.

**Meaningful failure.** The build can lose. Clear feedback and an affordable existing-part comparison provide a possible response; no automatic winner buff is granted.

**Clean stopping point.** The fitted host and its chosen care remain recorded; no ongoing brood or commission was silently created.

**Not claimed.** No measured onboarding time, first-run success rate or player-retention gain is asserted.

<a id="w15-path-complement"></a>
### A targeted capture becomes an intermediate build and a breeding project

**Three-period connection.** 0-5: Hopper action and kit purpose; 5-10: actual stage/facility/anatomy allocation; 10-15: spatial source, living-breeder customer and compendium action detail

**Required real starting objects.** An already useful racer; A high-return Hopper founder or access to one; An identified Switchyard source and realistic case capacity

| Step | Decision / change |
|---|---|
| 1 | Choose synchronization as the missing contribution. Read the existing habitat brief; do not alter the population’s genes to suit that target. |
| 2 | Observe a candidate’s paired release under its visible conditions, approach it and protect its useful anatomy. A good field cue remains uncertain evidence. |
| 3 | At home, compare controlled evidence if the decision warrants it. Keep the candidate as a possible parent instead of demanding premium return from that same individual. |
| 4 | Plan research review, ordinary components and a bounded replacement target before starting a supported brood. Offspring and compatible roles are not guaranteed. |
| 5 | When individuals exist and are observed, allocate useful intermediates to a personal assembly or real ordinary buyer. Retain the particular candidates that advance the next target. |
| 6 | Fit an actual descendant’s preparation or continue using the existing racer while selection continues. A later recipient sees the same physical output, not a gene unlocked by progress. |

**Completion.** The catch has a specific plausible purpose. At least one actual observed project milestone can be recorded independently of perfect offspring. Each animal and source region has one current use.

**Meaningful failure.** A wrong role, failed pairing, poor expression or damaged anatomy can matter. The plan permits another founder or buying useful stock; it does not force a convenient brood.

**Clean stopping point.** Stop new starts when the chosen reserve is full or demand is absent. Living stock continues under agreed care; offline time pauses.

**Not claimed.** No multigeneration success probability or profitable-brood guarantee is implied.

<a id="w15-path-fitter"></a>
### Earn through construction before financing every source

**Three-period connection.** 0-5: fitting and causal component roles; 5-10: real stock and useful controlled comparison; 10-15: customer purpose, quote and recipient record

**Required real starting objects.** A customer’s owned host; A customer’s prepared drive and contact set; A fitter’s actual bench/time/tools

| Step | Decision / change |
|---|---|
| 1 | The customer describes the desired improvement and authorizes handling of its particular stock. The fitter checks whether the current construction can reasonably address that goal. |
| 2 | Quote performed fitting and comparison work. Identify material supplied by the client separately from any item the fitter must purchase. |
| 3 | Use a bounded session to adjust the actual release/drive relationship or root arrangement. Preserve the reference until the new commitment is approved. |
| 4 | Test under declared conditions. A negative result can complete the agreed diagnostic service but cannot be sold as an achieved speed improvement. |
| 5 | Return the same animal and stock with the agreed result. Payment follows the actual service contract. |
| 6 | The fitter may spend realized income on a personally owned part, obtain breeding stock, or continue serving clients. |

**Completion.** A low-capital non-breeding livelihood has a defined product. Customer stock returns in its authorized condition/custody. The claimed result matches the work actually done.

**Meaningful failure.** The client may reject an unsupported guarantee; a part may not fit; the fitter can decline or revise before spending irreversible stock.

**Clean stopping point.** The completed service has no automatically renewed deadline or hidden ownership claim on the client’s animal.

**Not claimed.** No new fees or expected hourly income are calibrated here.

<a id="w15-path-maker"></a>
### A signature construction reaches a real recipient

**Three-period connection.** 0-5: named host, component functions, complete project; 5-10: stable, source stock, prepared anatomy; 10-15: measured recipe, alternate-stock kit, recipient and genuine new demand

**Required real starting objects.** An existing personal reference racer; A product specification with explicit purpose; Another actual host and qualifying components; A funded customer request

| Step | Decision / change |
|---|---|
| 1 | Publish the intended construction requirements and allowed mechanisms without transferring any of the reference racer’s anatomy. |
| 2 | Separate prior experimentation from the repeat job. Quote the actual product inputs, preparation, care, work and delivery; do not assume retail-purchased inputs become profitable through a recipe label. |
| 3 | Select another host and different physical parts. A substitute must satisfy its functional measurements and whole-build fit, not merely a higher band. |
| 4 | Pack with the declared care and actual source allocation. Deliver only the promised physical inventory for the agreed payment. |
| 5 | The recipient opens and personally assembles those same contents. A later note identifies actual use: racing, display, or an issue needing fitting assistance. |
| 6 | A repeat request exists only when the customer has another actual purpose and funds. It may request a revised release range, another host fit or a matching visual edition; no automatic popularity dividend is paid. |

**Completion.** The original remains intact. The sold kit contains real alternate stock and a usable guide. The recipient’s actual purpose closes the product loop.

**Meaningful failure.** A technically completed order can still lose money, as W12’s route did. Preserve that result, investigate real cost categories and decline an unsuitable repeat rather than add a subsidy disguised as reputation.

**Clean stopping point.** The maker keeps the product knowledge and any explicitly retained stock; completed orders create no permanent reserve on all future output.

**Not claimed.** No autonomous customer simulation or profitable pricing is claimed as delivered.

<a id="w15-path-trackmaker"></a>
### Course ownership becomes useful public work

**Three-period connection.** 0-5: modular racing reference and distinct handling parts; 5-10: owned Trackworks sections and brake/guide source functions; 10-15: club programme, services and clear recognition

**Required real starting objects.** Owned or explicitly borrowed circuit sections; A usable venue and setup brief; Entrants with their own actual builds

| Step | Decision / change |
|---|---|
| 1 | Design a closed route for the stated audience. Identify real speed, turn, surface, contact and landing opportunities rather than secretly requiring one proprietary set. |
| 2 | Publish the actual layout, key conditions and event purpose. An exhibition, practice session and official qualifier have different consequences. |
| 3 | Make several plausible responses available through existing stock or setup: landing length, approach, ratio, material, alternate line or a suitable different racer. |
| 4 | Host the meeting. A handling component’s effect is credited only during its actual action; ordinary racing still emphasizes pace and finishing. |
| 5 | Settle the stated setup work or funded prizes. Participants can recognize the builder and request another real layout occasion without making every replay payable. |
| 6 | Archive the construction knowledge and retain the actual section ownership. An ordinary club programme continues beyond one sponsored edition. |

**Completion.** The course is a real completed construction. Its special features create legible optional decisions. Hosting and racing rewards have distinct funded purposes.

**Meaningful failure.** A structurally valid course can be unsuitable for a particular racer. The designer revises or advertises the challenge honestly; no validation rule guarantees every entry finishes.

**Clean stopping point.** The setup is complete and the result recorded; the next event is an available choice, not a real-world attendance obligation.

**Not claimed.** No expanded geometry solver, four-roach physics or new official qualification is implemented by this reference.

<a id="w15-path-collector"></a>
### A beautiful specimen is a complete reason to participate

**Three-period connection.** 0-5: personal finish and collectible host; 5-10: distinct live specimen and ordinary commercial stock; 10-15: field recognition, display institutions, provenance and current compendium

**Required real starting objects.** An existing collection or an affordable empty display project; A source or Wrangloid with an appealing actual specimen; Suitable ongoing care or structural-material storage

| Step | Decision / change |
|---|---|
| 1 | Choose a visual or historical goal without assigning it a race-stat target. Identify what is natural, applied or claimed. |
| 2 | Catch, buy or commission the actual object. Select preservation and handling for the anatomy or appearance being valued. |
| 3 | Prepare the case or finish the construction while maintaining the object’s necessary working and care conditions. |
| 4 | Write an honest display label distinguishing original specimen, descendant, applied finish and reproduced edition. |
| 5 | Show it at an available club display, keep it privately or offer it to a relevant funded customer. A visible compliment is not a genetically certified price increase. |
| 6 | Preserve the finished project and choose whether to pursue a complementary piece, a racing conversion, restoration or no further action. |

**Completion.** Acquisition and finishing produce an actual kept object. Display and recognition have a purpose independent of race victory. Its history survives optional later rebuilding.

**Meaningful failure.** An appealing claim may lack evidence or a specimen may not suit the intended handling. The collector can decline or choose a different purpose without receiving a false stat consolation.

**Clean stopping point.** The project is complete and its care plan is known. No compulsory daily display or bonus-maintenance sequence is attached.

**Not claimed.** Inherited appearance prevalence, collector pricing and human attachment remain untested.

<a id="economic-review"></a>
## 8. Economic polish must retain the inconvenient evidence

### The first maker route was a cash loss

The historical Wave 12 browser route began its post-meet manufacturing stage with 174 prototype credits, made 150 credits of new payments, and received 112 credits on delivery. It ended with 136 credits. The post-meet cash change was **−38**.

The exact reference is [Wave 12, Actual browser evidence](reference/RRR_Wave_12_Foundations.md). This milestone does not rerun or alter it. The summarized payments do not establish how much was research, retail acquisition, lasting equipment, care, or repeated-unit cost. Those categories require the underlying line items; simply calling the loss “investment” does not resolve the design.

### Three offerings need three cost structures


| Offering | Seller must provide | Relevant margin | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer-owned-stock fitting | Performed preparation/fitting/testing within the agreed authority. | Agreed service receipt minus actual labor, service access, supplies and associated care. | Promising a change the customer’s stock cannot support. |
| Complete-kit manufacture | A real host, actual components, preparation, supported handling and a usable guide. | Accepted product receipts minus actual new inputs, repeat work, care/overhead and delivery; research separately reported. | Expensive retail inputs or production conditions exceed the buyer’s useful value. |
| Breeding supply | Eligible living stock or actual prepared output plus any required observed evidence. | Receipts from accepted deliveries minus actual breeding/rearing/handling costs; retained productive stock remains inventory. | Uncertain reproduction, unmet specification, accumulated care or absent funded demand. |

The repeated-unit design must demonstrate an achievable earning-a-keep route under declared conditions; it does not require every novice attempt or every brood to profit. When recurring acceptable output costs more than buyers will pay, improve the actual productive method, product purpose, order scope or customer segment—or reject the offering. Do not solve it with a hidden prestige multiplier.

A research programme can intentionally spend money. A hobbyist can willingly buy a costly personal build. Neither fact justifies describing an unprofitable repeat business as passive income.

### A growing reserve is not automatic progress

The historical Wave 06 repeated-production sample reports these states:

| World turn | Cash | Owned living stock |
|---|---|---|
| 25 | 204 | 5 |
| 49 | 200 | 7 |
| 73 | 191 | 16 |

These are observations from one prior seed and policy, not a causal experiment. They justify reviewing care, demand and replacement accumulation together; they do not prove which factor caused the cash change. See [Wave 06, repeated production](reference/RRR_Wave_06_Foundations.md).

The new rule is qualitative and specific: **maintain a chosen replacement target; do not silently append permanent keepers forever.** If a better candidate appears, the player chooses whether it replaces a reserve member, starts a different branch, becomes a personal animal, is sold, or is otherwise handled. The old animal does not vanish.

### Conditional risk, not multiplied optimistic averages

Encounter, observation, capture, preservation, maturity, reproductive compatibility, inherited contribution, survival and product acceptance are conditional events. Their dependence matters. A poor rearing environment can affect maturity, condition and delivery together.

For downstream analysis, keep the relevant conditional chain rather than multiplying unrelated published averages. The compendium supplies no new probability values. A legitimate alternative—buying a measured part, acquiring a compatible breeding unit, using another preparation or taking customer-owned-stock work—can reduce a selected risk without guaranteeing every outcome.

The primary economic reference remains the actual object: what is owned, what it can do, what was spent, who wants it, what they can fund, and what has really been delivered.

### Six later comparisons

<a id="w15-study-bench"></a>
#### Does the bench-session model improve understood experimentation without concealing physical costs?

**Comparison.** Per-small-action appointments versus a bounded useful bench session.

**Keep controlled.** Same starting host, stock, total physical service opportunity, information and achievable builds; record differences in actual spending rather than secretly subsidize one variant.

**Observe.** Completed personally fitted builds; Correct explanation of one changed mechanism; Number of avoidable confirmation/administrative actions; Whether participants willingly try a second variation; Perceived control and burden

**Reject or revise when.** If users cannot distinguish real new work from free draft manipulation, or the session merely hides higher costs, revise it.

**Status.** Future human study; not performed.

<a id="w15-study-field"></a>
#### Can players identify a useful candidate without an oracle or an obligatory assay for every catch?

**Comparison.** All useful traits hidden until assay versus contextual visible cues plus targeted precise tests.

**Keep controlled.** Same source population, individual truths, capture conditions, prices and hidden variables; change only the public evidence layer.

**Observe.** Purposeful keep/use/sell explanations; False certainty about genes; Willingness to revisit a familiar source; Unnecessary paid tests; Ability to recognize a complement rather than strongest individual

**Reject or revise when.** If visible cues become exact-genome or price oracles, or no novice can make an informed decision, revise the information hierarchy.

**Status.** Future human and content review; not performed.

<a id="w15-study-brood"></a>
#### Does useful intermediate output support strategy without removing meaningful risk?

**Comparison.** Single premium pass/fail goal versus split-brood objectives with bounded replacements and the same actual offspring.

**Keep controlled.** Same parents, outcome sequence, costs, capacities and buyers; distinguish decisions from differences in favorable random outcomes.

**Observe.** Retained useful complements; Actual ordinary product use; Unintended stock accumulation; Ability to stop new starts; Understanding of failed attempts and unknown descendants

**Reject or revise when.** If the polished plan silently selects hidden perfect genomes or deletes excess animals, it fails regardless of reported convenience.

**Status.** Future controlled study; not performed.

<a id="w15-study-maker"></a>
#### Which livelihood is affordable under matched resources, and what does its buyer actually value?

**Comparison.** Retail-supplied assembly, field-sourced manufacture, established-line production, and customer-owned-stock fitting as distinct offerings.

**Keep controlled.** Matched starting capital and action opportunity; disclose facilities, source assets, research allocation, care, outcome uncertainty, target quality and funded demand. Do not compare an endowed mature business to a new builder without adjustment.

**Observe.** Operating receipts and costs; Cash exposure before payment; Retained productive inventory; Failures and fallback routes; Repeat-unit feasibility; Buyer purpose and willingness to purchase

**Reject or revise when.** A model that loses money per repeated accepted unit cannot be called an earning-a-keep route because its first sale is visually pleasing.

**Status.** Future economic calibration and human study; not performed.

<a id="w15-study-racing"></a>
#### Does each advertised construction repeatedly express a visible useful action without fake results?

**Comparison.** Matched baseline/candidate components and relevant versus irrelevant opportunity contexts.

**Keep controlled.** Hold host, relevant setup, course and competitors fixed for one-component claims; separately examine complete build concepts without calling them isolated tests.

**Observe.** Actual action opportunities and attempts; Visible loading/contact/recovery; Useful position or completion changes; Known counterexamples; Whether observers identify the fitted anatomy; Desire to rerace beyond prize exhaustion

**Reject or revise when.** Do not rescue a rarely working flagship ability with an automatic pass, unseen multiplier, or a course that only that item can finish.

**Status.** Future downstream implementation and human study; not performed.

<a id="w15-study-return"></a>
#### Do players return for self-chosen projects rather than avoidable upkeep or expiration pressure?

**Comparison.** An obligation-led reentry versus a completed result plus one chosen next intention and optional expert state.

**Keep controlled.** Same saved world, care rules, offline pause, available projects and rewards; no streak or artificial scarcity advantage in either version.

**Observe.** What the player intends to do next; Time spent rediscovering the current goal; Perceived autonomy; Projects finished rather than merely accumulated; Reasons for voluntary stopping and returning; Enjoyment distinct from total time spent

**Reject or revise when.** More session minutes do not count as success if they are caused by confusion, repeated administration or fear of losing stock.

**Status.** Future human study; not performed.

### Evidence boundary for “compelling”

The desired outcome is voluntary return for mastery, discovery, competition, ownership, social recognition or a chosen project. More minutes, more clicks, more unresolved obligations or greater anxiety about leaving are not sufficient evidence of that outcome.

Readability and consistency are necessary, not sufficient. The manuscript has no authority to declare a measured retention improvement. The six comparisons state what later evidence would need to distinguish, including unfavorable results.

### MIVIM contribution assessment

| Criterion | Current finding | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Quality | Explicit replacement definitions, retained source facts, linked objects and comparison cases are authored. Documentary completeness and cross-reference checks support reviewability. | This does not certify final game quality; human comprehension and interaction costs require downstream observation. |
| Novel-development adherence | Project-specific integration is concrete: bounded bench visits, complementary-stock loops, target-based replacements, separate maker businesses, recipient recognition and a persistent ordinary sport share the same physical objects. | No worldwide-novelty or prior-art exhaustiveness claim; standard tools support a specific whole. |
| Effectiveness | The written systems answer the identified dependency, selection, cost and continuity problems at specification scope. | Expected experiential improvement remains a hypothesis. No new gameplay, retention or profitability test was run. |
| Creativity | The revised connections preserve living material, hand-built kits, insects, clubs, breeders and distinct economic purposes rather than replacing them with generic XP or rarity loops. | Whether players experience them as distinctive or pleasurable is not established by the author’s judgment alone. |
| Scaling potential | Shared source/product/evidence/care distinctions and repeatable object briefs allow new donors and communities to use a coherent design language. | No runtime scale, autonomous national economy, large-agent retrieval benchmark or real player population has been measured. |

**Hard admission.** Required quality at declared compendium scope AND adherence to purposeful novel integration. Neither is offset by length, speed, counts or mechanical checks.
