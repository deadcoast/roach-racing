# RRR — Wave v0.17

## Research Register: Source Function and Biological Inspiration

External observations are separated from the fictional designs they informed. A scientific description of one species is not a universal rule for every insect, and a performance-part category supplies no undocumented PCR coefficients. No source image, research figure, video or copyrighted article is reproduced. Source links are references, not real-insect collection instructions.

### W17.S05 — Kinematics of Terrestrial Locomotion in Mole Cricket Gryllotalpa orientalis (2011)

**Observed external fact.** The study describes specialized digging forelegs, a distinctive alternating tripod gait and compensating roles of middle legs and body joints in walking and turning.

**RRR design use.** The source insect’s digging limbs do not prove its entire gait or every extracted lever is a superior running system.

**Limit.** Indexed primary-paper abstract; no numerical reanalysis or whole-species generalization. Ballast anatomy and all preparation patterns remain fictional.

**Reading scope.** Publisher abstract, DOI 10.1016/S1672-6529(11)60013-9.

**Checked.** 2026-09-11

**Source:** `https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1672652911600139`

### W17.S06 — Structure, mechanics and material properties of claw cuticle from mole cricket Gryllotalpa orientalis (2019)

**Observed external fact.** The studied claw tips and bases differ in structure and measured mechanical properties; supporting ribs and graded material have different roles.

**RRR design use.** Cleat tips, bases and supporting joints receive separate descriptions and condition checks; hardness alone does not certify a complete cleat.

**Limit.** No composition recipe, material number or improvement percentage is transferred. The abstract does not validate Ballast donor grafting.

**Reading scope.** Primary-paper abstract in PubMed.

**Checked.** 2026-09-11

**Source:** `https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31491009/`

### W17.S07 — Cornell — Speedy tiger beetles use antennae to see while running (2014)

**Observed external fact.** The reported experiments found forward-held antennae important for obstacle negotiation in fast-running tiger beetles.

**RRR design use.** Preserve the distinction between long moving anatomy and the information needed to use it; a limb graft does not import the donor’s sensory behavior.

**Limit.** Researcher-institution report on particular experiments. No universal blindness penalty, mandatory gait pause or new sensor item is imposed on RRR.

**Reading scope.** University report of the study; video not used.

**Checked.** 2026-09-11

**Source:** `https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2014/02/speedy-tiger-beetles-use-antennae-see-while-running`

### W17.S09 — Rossoni & Niven (2020) — Prey speed influences the speed and structure of the raptorial strike of a sit-and-wait predator

**Observed external fact.** The studied mantises varied aspects of their strike, including extension duration and pauses, with prey speed.

**RRR design use.** Separate observation/commitment from arm travel and closure; an observed strike duration is not one immutable tissue speed.

**Limit.** Species-specific primary abstract. Neither the donor’s entire controller nor exact laboratory timings are imported into a roach.

**Reading scope.** Indexed primary-paper abstract; PMC full-text open was blocked. DOI 10.1098/rsbl.2020.0098.

**Checked.** 2026-09-11

**Source:** `https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32396788/`

<a id="w17-s10"></a>

### W17.S10 — McCullough, Tobalske & Emlen (2014) — Structural adaptations to diverse fighting styles in sexually selected weapons

**Observed external fact.** Finite-element comparisons found studied horn shapes performed better under their species-typical fighting loads than atypical loads.

**RRR design use.** Bullhorn profiles identify contact geometry and loading direction rather than a universal length-to-damage scale.

**Limit.** Three real horn forms and modeled loads do not define all beetles or calibrated RRR damage.

**Reading scope.** Primary abstract and explanatory figure captions; no simulation replication. DOI 10.1073/pnas.1409585111.

**Checked.** 2026-09-11

**Source:** `https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25201949/`

<a id="w17-s11"></a>

### W17.S11 — Rhinoceros beetle horn development reveals deep parallels with dung beetles (2018)

**Observed external fact.** The paper describes sex-specific horn expression and multiple developmental contributions to horn shape in the studied beetle.

**RRR design use.** A Bullhorn reproductive contribution need not be expressed as a full horn on every parent; shape and development remain distinct.

**Limit.** Only indexed abstract/result excerpts were read. No gene-editing procedure, exact loci or universal beetle sex rule is adopted.

**Reading scope.** Primary paper indexed text; RRR sex-associated expression is a declared working profile.

**Checked.** 2026-09-11

**Source:** `https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6171792/`

<a id="w17-s12"></a>

### W17.S12 — Elaborate horns in a giant rhinoceros beetle incur negligible aerodynamic costs (2013)

**Observed external fact.** Measured aerodynamic and mass costs of the studied horns were small under the tested flight conditions.

**RRR design use.** Reject automatic huge drag or mass penalties merely because a horn looks extravagant.

**Limit.** Not evidence that every horn is free of cost or every RRR configuration is aerodynamically negligible.

**Reading scope.** Primary abstract and indexed discussion; DOI 10.1098/rspb.2013.0197.

**Checked.** 2026-09-11

**Source:** `https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23486444/`

### W17.S13 — Kundanati et al. (2018) — Multilayer stag beetle elytra perform better under external loading via non-symmetric bending properties

**Observed external fact.** Studied multilayer elytra had direction-dependent bending behavior under the examined configurations.

**RRR design use.** Keep load face, backing and orientation attached to shell preparations; flipping a shell does not preserve every working property.

**Limit.** This is not a measured Gutterback material profile or a friction study.

**Reading scope.** Primary abstract; DOI 10.1098/rsif.2018.0427.

**Checked.** 2026-09-11

**Source:** `https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30045895/`

### W17.S14 — Compound microstructures and wax layer of beetle elytral surfaces and their influence on wetting properties (2012)

**Observed external fact.** The study examines relationships between surface structure, wax and wettability in selected beetle elytra.

**RRR design use.** Treat surface condition and microstructure as real subjects; shine, water behavior and rail friction must not be conflated.

**Limit.** Indexed results on wetting do not directly certify sliding friction. No cleaning chemistry or friction values are imported.

**Reading scope.** Indexed primary-paper abstract/discussion; direct PMC open was blocked.

**Checked.** 2026-09-11

**Source:** `https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3464267/`

<a id="w17-s15"></a>

### W17.S15 — Neil et al. (2020) — Thoracic scales of moths as a stealth coating against bat biosonar

**Observed external fact.** Studied moth thoraces carry hair-like scales whose measured acoustic role was investigated.

**RRR design use.** Ground the distinction between a fibrous body covering and flight membrane, then explicitly author Sandfelt’s different fictional contact adaptation.

**Limit.** Sound absorption is NOT proof of brake friction, cushioning, stealth in RRR or an inherited racing buff.

**Reading scope.** Primary abstract and author-university record; DOI 10.1098/rsif.2019.0692.

**Checked.** 2026-09-11

**Source:** `https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32093539/`

<a id="w17-s16"></a>

### W17.S16 — Biomechanics of a moth scale at ultrasonic frequencies (2018)

**Observed external fact.** The study distinguishes sculptured scales, their attachment and the supporting wing membrane.

**RRR design use.** Separate Ashveil scale appearance, membrane, vein-supported shape and fitted aerodynamic function.

**Limit.** The acoustic experiments do not show that all scale loss disables flight, that every scale carries pigment, or that Ashveil vanes make useful downforce.

**Reading scope.** Indexed primary-paper introduction/abstract and discussion; no numerical analysis or acoustic feature added.

**Checked.** 2026-09-11

**Source:** `https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6275474/`

## Research-derived distinctions used in this volume

| Finding | What the compendium changes | What it does not infer |
|---|---|---|
| A complete strike can change with the target. | Separate cue/decision, extension, closure and release in Catchclaw assessment. | A fixed genetic strike-duration value from one wild observation. |
| Horn structure is suited to particular loads. | Describe contact direction, root support and load path. | A universal larger-horn power tier or automatic cost penalty. |
| Mole-cricket tips and supporting regions differ. | Inspect the contact end and the base receiving its force. | A hard tip supplies its own support or an entire faster gait. |
| Wing-cover structure and surface condition differ. | Inspect the working face, backing and permitted load direction. | Gloss or water repellence certifies low rail friction. |
| Moth scale studies examine specific acoustic mechanisms. | Use structural texture as inspiration, then author and assess the fictional brake or vane independently. | Acoustic absorption proves brake friction or flight performance. |
