## 1. Reading authority and the scope of this volume

This is a reference-book expansion, not a new executable release. Earlier experiments remain useful evidence and historical artifacts. The chapters below define objects, relationships, observable behavior and creative intent before downstream teams choose simulation methods. New biology and institutions are authored working proposals; only explicit user premises are author canon. Numerical prototype outcomes are not silently promoted into natural laws.

<a id="w14-auth-01"></a>
### What is fixed, what is designed, what is experimental
`W14.AUTH.01`

**Author foundation.** The sport is fast, competitive rad-roach circuit racing in a nuclear wasteland. The host can assimilate insect parts. The founding horn, mantis and hopper rivalry precedes the mature hobby by thirty years. Anyone can catch and breed. Wrangloids are hobbyists who make their expertise a livelihood. Open, Flyers, Fireworks and Speedsters retain the author’s class distinctions.

**Working world.** Named donors, preparations, organizations, care practices and the anatomical explanations in this volume are current design proposals. They can be revised through an explicit amendment without deleting the useful history.

**Experimental record.** A three-locus genome, six-animal patch, particular wallet, three event editions, two-roach solver or nine-by-six field describes a prior experiment. None limits the universe’s actual variety, population, wealth or sporting formats.

**Unknown.** An unresolved size, lifespan, preservation time or calendar interval is not zero, infinite, forbidden or secretly selected. This volume supplies qualitative constraints where a useful definition is possible without invented calibration.

**Earlier reference:** `C01`, `C02`, `C03`, `C04`, `C05`, `C06`, `C07`, `C08`, `C09`, `C10`, `C11`, `C12`, `C13`, `C14`, `C15`, `C16`, `C17`, `C18`, `C19`

<a id="w14-auth-02"></a>
### Scope changes do not erase prior work
`W14.AUTH.02`

**Current priority.** Develop the compendium’s living anatomy, component families, action vocabulary, selection objectives, commercial forms and shared institutions. The previous motion-code task is retained in the downstream implementation backlog, not marked complete.

**Preserved.** Existing names, author canon, world records, reference evidence, trial histories and all previous wave data remain in the cumulative archive.

**New reading order.** Read this volume for the present qualitative interpretation. Consult prior wave experiments for examples and their stated limits. An explicit W14 amendment takes precedence only for the named interpretation; it does not recalculate a saved racer or make an earlier simulation disappear.

**Completion claim.** This volume completes the entries it contains at reference-definition scope. It is not a claim that every continent, faction, donor or game system has been fully authored.

### Sets and tiers are separate cataloguing systems
`W14.MAP.SETS`

**Functional family.** What a component does: drive, contact, reserve, support, guidance or another declared function.

**Preparation pattern.** How anatomy has been arranged for use. One donor can supply alternate patterns; one pattern can be supplied by different suitable individuals.

**Performance qualification.** A named protocol and its required properties. Hobby, Club, Circuit, Premier and Apex remain readable proposed bands, never genes or universal quality certificates.

**Availability.** Standing stock, a scheduled brood release, a revived historic line, a one-off custom build or a commemorative tin. Scarcity can matter to a collector independently of race performance.

**Coordinated set.** A selection of complementary physical components plus fitting knowledge. Matching names does not award an invisible aura.

**Distinct value.** An improved component may genuinely outperform another at equal burden. An alternative ratio or reach may instead suit a different construction. Neither distinction should be erased to force all products into one ladder.

## 3. The living racer: common anatomical language

The host remains an animal whose working anatomy has been deliberately rebuilt. The following terms define what the builder is manipulating; they are not additional mandatory equipment slots.

<a id="w14-anatomy-host"></a>
### Host
`W14.ANATOMY.HOST`

**Definition.** The persistent living roach that supplies the native foundation and receives grafts. Its identity continues through changes in equipment, finish, ownership and race career.

**Distinction.** A build is a configuration of this animal; a saved build is not another animal.

<a id="w14-anatomy-bodyplan"></a>
### Body plan
`W14.ANATOMY.BODYPLAN`

**Definition.** The native distribution of length, width, mass, articulation and root-bearing surfaces. Lowline, Needleback and Broadback are established working examples, not an exhaustive species list.

**Distinction.** A narrow animal does not shrink bought limbs. Body-plan inheritance is a biology question, not a reason to copy the three-plan prototype globally.

<a id="w14-anatomy-seam"></a>
### Molt seam
`W14.ANATOMY.SEAM`

**Definition.** The proposed name for the rad roach’s mutable junction tissue. It incorporates donor anatomy and forms continuity with the host. It is an expression of the author’s assimilation premise.

**Distinction.** The name does not require ordinary adult insects to molt, imply general limb regrowth, or restrict all grafting to a juvenile window.

<a id="w14-anatomy-root"></a>
### Graft root
`W14.ANATOMY.ROOT`

**Definition.** The interface through which a sect attaches and becomes supported, supplied and controlled. A root includes the tissues needed by its own function; different preparations expose different shapes.

**Distinction.** The root is not an arbitrary colored socket that rejects another insect family.

<a id="w14-anatomy-anchor"></a>
### Load anchor
`W14.ANATOMY.ANCHOR`

**Definition.** The portion of a construction that passes force from the working sect into adequate supporting anatomy. A horn root, wing hinge and leg root impose different directions of load.

**Distinction.** A surface attachment that looks connected does not establish a useful load path.

<a id="w14-anatomy-fascicle"></a>
### Drive bundle
`W14.ANATOMY.FASCICLE`

**Definition.** Contractile tissue plus the interfaces needed to apply its work. Mechanical output, control and supply must all reach the body part being driven.

**Distinction.** Visible size, buzzing and species reputation do not individually establish useful output.

<a id="w14-anatomy-link"></a>
### Gait linkage
`W14.ANATOMY.LINK`

**Definition.** Levers and coordinating junctions that define stroke travel, working force and timing. The native roach already has such structures.

**Distinction.** The part that changes travel is not necessarily the part that synchronizes release.

<a id="w14-anatomy-contact"></a>
### Contact surface
`W14.ANATOMY.CONTACT`

**Definition.** The actual anatomy touching a floor, wall, opponent or support. Material, area, orientation, loading and release all belong to its purpose.

**Distinction.** Foot traction, rail sliding, gripping jaws and braking material are not a single universal grip score.

<a id="w14-anatomy-supply"></a>
### Supply pathway
`W14.ANATOMY.SUPPLY`

**Definition.** The host and graft interfaces through which stored energy and necessary physiological support reach working tissues. The definition is functional; it does not assert a particular real-insect circulatory mechanism.

**Distinction.** Separate organs cannot each spend the entire same reserve independently.

<a id="w14-anatomy-return"></a>
### Return path
`W14.ANATOMY.RETURN`

**Definition.** The route by which a displaced limb, plate or moving mass returns to its useful position. It includes elastic, muscular or damped elements where those exist.

**Distinction.** A spring can return energy; it does not automatically remove rebound or aim the returning limb.

<a id="w14-anatomy-command"></a>
### Motor and sensory junction
`W14.ANATOMY.COMMAND`

**Definition.** The connection between host control, local sensory information and a working graft. The shared host remains the actor; an installed arm does not bring an independent donor personality.

**Distinction.** A sensor supplies information, not strength or omniscient knowledge of the course.

<a id="w14-anatomy-collar"></a>
### Prepared exchange collar
`W14.ANATOMY.COLLAR`

**Definition.** A deliberately maintained release boundary around a graft root that supports reversible workshop rebuilding. It trades preparation and care for predictable swaps.

**Distinction.** The collar helps reuse; it does not make assimilation possible in the first place, is not proprietary species permission, and does not guarantee every raw extraction is harmless.

<a id="w14-anatomy-joins"></a>
### A functional installation has five kinds of continuity
`W14.ANATOMY.JOINS`

**Attachment.** The graft remains seated through its required movements.

**Support.** Forces pass into the host rather than through a decorative or unsupported connection.

**Supply.** The working anatomy receives the resources it requires and has unobstructed necessary exchange surfaces.

**Control.** The host can activate, relax and interpret the graft to the extent required by the construction.

**Clearance.** The working and returning anatomy has physical room to move.

**Example.** A second drive bundle is meaningful when its supply and output can join a supported transmission. Simply attaching another muscle does not duplicate the entire leg system; conversely, a one-drive trial interface is not a universal law against multi-drive racers.

**Common experience.** A coordinated beginner kit supplies an understandable arrangement that solves these requirements. Experts can redesign them. Every novice is not required to perform five repetitive diagnostic rituals before a first race.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Graft root](#w14-anatomy-root) · [Supply pathway](#w14-anatomy-supply) · [Motor](#w14-map-drive)

<a id="w14-anatomy-regions"></a>
### The body has regions, not an immutable equipment grid
`W14.ANATOMY.REGIONS`

**Brow and head.** Good visibility and forward contact opportunities; horn or jaw load still needs a supported root. Split vanes may coexist around a central horn.

**Thorax.** Working muscle, major load transfer and limb roots compete through actual anatomy. This is where bracing and controlled flexibility matter most.

**Flanks.** Sensory stalks, articulated braces and rail guides need their own exposure, travel and support. Their working width may differ from folded width.

**Belly.** Reservoir position, compliant landings, suspended masses and contact brakes interact through clearance and distribution.

**Abdomen.** Storage and specialized glands affect distribution, supply and clearance. A rear weapon’s contents are not also available as the drive’s full fuel reserve.

**Dorsal envelope.** Protective plates, vanes and powered wings need different opening and working spaces. Split armor can coexist with a flight root where its actual arrangement allows it.

**Related entries / evidence:** [A functional installation has five kinds of continuity](#w14-anatomy-joins)

<a id="w14-anatomy-race_readings"></a>
### What the public performance readings mean
`W14.ANATOMY.RACE_READINGS`

**Speed and acceleration.** Pace and how quickly it is reached are outcomes of the working construction. They must remain desirable, intelligible comparisons; neither is a duplicate gene added on top of every contributing part.

**Grip and cornering.** Grip describes contact behavior under specified conditions. Cornering describes the whole racer negotiating a route while maintaining useful motion. These values can correlate without being interchangeable.

**Balance.** The construction’s ability to maintain or restore usable posture through sensing, support and action. It is not a universal reduction to all forms of damage.

**Hold-down.** An aerodynamic load tied to motion and surface arrangement. Its authored RRR usefulness must remain tied to air interaction, not a standing-still buff.

**Mass and reserve.** Actual carried material and available energy. Fill, hardware-like fixtures, living tissue and protection have distinguishable contributions.

**Observation discipline.** A comparison names its load, surface, configuration and relevant condition. A measured lap is a result, not proof that one selected gene caused every improvement.

## 4. Sect reference catalogue: anatomy with a reason to exist

Each entry states its physical subject, useful selection, fitting contribution, visible behavior and commercial use. Familiar names are retained. Recovered earlier concepts are identified as reference designs rather than falsely counted as new implemented products. A product’s complete anatomical allocation governs whether two preparations can come from one donor; the same tissue cannot be sold twice.

<a id="w14-part-redline"></a>
### Cicada Redline
`W14.PART.REDLINE`

**Reference role.** DRIVE

**Donor and source.** Furnace cicada; rooted pipe galleries, the Rooted signal pole and Root furnace. Adults supply working tissue; continued production needs the root-fed juvenile stage, not only an adult display cage.

**Prepared anatomy.** A ribbed thoracic muscle bundle with supported ends and preserved supply/control interfaces. Its visible pulse becomes purposeful limb or wing-drive work after integration.

**Selection and breeding.** Compare cadence under a declared load, useful force and conversion efficiency. Retain an efficient ordinary-cadence founder to complement a fast but hungry line. Noise and agitated movement are screening clues, not measurements.

**Builder contribution.** Route work to a suitable linkage and choose leverage independently. Supply must support the desired duty, with space for the bundle to work without pinching surrounding anatomy.

**Visible racing consequence.** Faster repeated working strokes when the rest of the build can use them. Foot-release bottlenecks or insufficient supply can prevent that output becoming pace.

**Hobby and trade.** Useful in sprint kits and flight-drive preparations. Ordinary output supports club upgrades; selected economical tissue serves longer heats. A complete root nursery supports a recurring supplier.

**Boundary.** It does not set leg reach or manufacture energy. The fictional Redline is not a claim that a real cicada’s sound-producing anatomy is a ready-made racing motor.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Motor](#w14-map-drive)

**Earlier reference:** `redline`, `W02.DONOR.CICADA`

<a id="w14-part-torque"></a>
### Scarab Torque Bundle
`W14.PART.TORQUE`

**Reference role.** DRIVE

**Donor and source.** Kilnback scarab; protected furnace compost and Cooling compost. Retain healthy adults and complete grub/pupal development for repeat production.

**Prepared anatomy.** Dense muscle columns with broad working attachments. The prepared block needs supporting anatomy capable of receiving its repeated load.

**Selection and breeding.** Select useful force per material mass and sustained force under the intended operating demand. Large shell size is not sufficient. Frame traits from the same line are a separate product objective.

**Builder contribution.** Place the bundle near a practical output path and choose the gait ratio. A loaded body may need structural reinforcement; an empty decorative plate does not supply it.

**Visible racing consequence.** Forceful starts, climbing work and recovery after traffic when contact can transmit the force. Higher-force tissue need not have the highest unloaded cadence.

**Hobby and trade.** Loaded Open constructions, rough-course kits and drive commissions. Disjoint internal support plates can support a separate Boxbrace preparation where the harvest plan preserves them.

**Boundary.** A forceful drive does not automatically make a charge effective: approach, contact and horn-root support remain necessary.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Motor](#w14-map-drive)

**Earlier reference:** `torque`, `W02.DONOR.SCARAB`

<a id="w14-part-lowstep"></a>
### Mole-Cricket Lowstep
`W14.PART.LOWSTEP`

**Reference role.** GAIT

**Donor and source.** Ballast mole cricket; Ballast trench, Ballast Banks and suitable loose root beds. Hip structure, toes and sleeve tissue have different allocation purposes.

**Prepared anatomy.** Short hip levers and their working junctions, prepared to exchange stroke distance for force at the driven limbs.

**Selection and breeding.** Select the mechanical-advantage range required by the intended construction and consistent joint geometry. Do not select maximum ratio as a universal optimum. A modest ratio with good articulation may fit the product better.

**Builder contribution.** Choose the working ratio through preparation and fine alignment. Match travel with the installed muscle and running spars rather than adding a second stride bonus.

**Visible racing consequence.** Shorter forceful strokes, useful starts and load handling. Extended clean straights may expose its limited travel per drive cycle.

**Hobby and trade.** Loaded kit production; hip assemblies sold separately from cleats. A breeder can supply a repeated geometry range rather than an alleged top-tier gene.

**Boundary.** Ratio is an operating choice. It does not create more work than the input supplies.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Gears](#w14-map-gait)

**Earlier reference:** `lowstep`, `W12.REPAIR.01`

<a id="w14-part-longthrow"></a>
### Tiger-Beetle Longthrow
`W14.PART.LONGTHROW`

**Reference role.** GAIT

**Donor and source.** Trackrunner tiger beetle; hardpan between sleepers and sunlit slab edges. This donor and assembly are retained from the earlier compendium, beyond the limited playable source roster.

**Prepared anatomy.** Long, light hip-and-limb lever structures with separately identified distal contact tips.

**Selection and breeding.** Select reach without unnecessary moving mass, and working articulation that preserves the intended stroke. Very long but heavy extremities can be a poor high-cadence combination.

**Builder contribution.** Match drive force, stroke travel and stance. A complete limb assembly includes its geometry; adding a separate limb-reach term must not count that length twice.

**Visible racing consequence.** Greater travel per useful stroke on suitable ground, with different launch and load behavior from Lowstep.

**Hobby and trade.** Sprint builders and replacement-limb makers seek selected reach bands. Non-premium individuals can still provide useful tips or another size of assembly.

**Boundary.** This is not a promise that long legs always win or that a juvenile automatically grows into a higher product tier.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Gears](#w14-map-gait)

**Earlier reference:** `Tiger-Beetle Longthrow`, `W02.DONOR.TIGER`

<a id="w14-part-lockstep"></a>
### Planthopper Lockstep
`W14.PART.LOCKSTEP`

**Reference role.** GAIT

**Donor and source.** Clockleaf planthopper; protected seepage reeds. Late juveniles are the working source in this fictional profile; adult founders provide the next cohorts. Retained earlier compendium concept.

## 5. Race action lexicon: what anatomy visibly does

These entries specify causes, commitments, interactions and readable outcomes. They do not prescribe a solver, animation frame count or numerical success roll. A real-time implementation, an abstract simulation and an illustrated handbook must describe the same action.

<a id="w14-action-run"></a>
### Run, accelerate and hold pace
`W14.ACTION.RUN`

**Required anatomy and situation.** Useful feet, working limbs, drive, coordination and supply; native anatomy already provides a complete running system.

**Causal sequence.** A contact plants, available work loads it, the body advances, that contact unloads and releases, and another useful support continues the gait. Maintaining pace is repeated successful delivery, not repeated purchase of a speed bonus.

**Builder or breeder influence.** Drive cadence and force, foot release, stride geometry, moving mass and supply create different improvement opportunities. An efficient new drive can sustain the same output longer; a quicker foot can remove a gait bottleneck.

**Interference and unsuccessful outcomes.** Loose footing, obstructed travel, limited supply or a heavily loaded body can limit useful output. The result must not imply the animal lacks a motor when its feet are actually stuck.

**Recognition.** Step frequency, stroke reach, slipping and recovery belong to the visible construction. A fast racer should visibly travel fast; the presentation must not diminish speed or winning into a lesson about diagnostics.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Cicada Redline](#w14-part-redline) · [Scarab Torque Bundle](#w14-part-torque) · [Sticktoe Contact Set](#w14-part-sticktoe) · [Mole-Cricket Lowstep](#w14-part-lowstep)

<a id="w14-action-corner"></a>
### Choose a line and turn
`W14.ACTION.CORNER`

**Required anatomy and situation.** Contact and articulated anatomy capable of redirecting the body within the available racing space.

**Causal sequence.** The animal changes its support and working strokes, redirects momentum and continues into the next part of the course. Wall contact may assist, obstruct or dissipate motion according to the actual geometry.

**Builder or breeder influence.** Stance, body proportions, contact material, native articulation and supported correction matter. A high-cadence motor does not alone specify a turning radius.

**Interference and unsuccessful outcomes.** Traffic can occupy a desired line; an exposed graft can clip a wall; inadequate contact can cause outward loss. A fixed penalty applied at every bend cannot stand in for all these distinct meanings.

**Recognition.** The body and feet orient into the turn. A held wide brace or guide remains in the actual width of the racer rather than vanishing to permit a pass.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Beetle Railshoes](#w14-part-railshoes) · [Mantis Counterbraces](#w14-part-counterbraces) · [Body plan](#w14-anatomy-bodyplan)

<a id="w14-action-correct"></a>
### Detect and correct a developing lean
`W14.ACTION.CORRECT`

**Required anatomy and situation.** An available cue, an appropriate controller or reflex, and an actual corrective contact or actuator. A passive stop instead acts through contact without an active sensing requirement.

**Causal sequence.** Disturbance occurs; sensing or mechanical contact registers it; available anatomy responds; the body returns toward useful support or continues into failure.

**Builder or breeder influence.** Sensor delay changes when correction can begin. Brace range changes what surface can be reached. Body and root support determine whether the response can carry the load.

**Interference and unsuccessful outcomes.** A sensor cannot correct while its only response limb is committed to gripping an opponent. A brace cannot push against empty air. These outcomes follow the construction, not a universal immunity state.

**Recognition.** Distinguish a small anticipatory foot correction, an extended support, and a hard Guardknuckle touching a wall. They should not all play the same generic balance sparkle.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Fly Gyropods](#w14-part-gyropods) · [Mantis Counterbraces](#w14-part-counterbraces) · [Beetle Guardknuckles](#w14-part-guardknuckles)

<a id="w14-action-brake"></a>
### Engage an approach brake
`W14.ACTION.BRAKE`

**Required anatomy and situation.** A prepared contact surface, adequate support and a matching surface within reach. Native limb deceleration is a different available action.

**Causal sequence.** The pad reaches the approach, compresses within its travel, dissipates motion while contact persists, then disengages or continues to drag if set too low.

**Builder or breeder influence.** Material and support are donor/preparation properties; height, area and approach setup are builder choices. Both determine the working contact.

**Interference and unsuccessful outcomes.** A raised pad misses the surface. A worn or fouled pad behaves differently. Excess contact can lose unnecessary pace. None of these directly mutates the donor line.

**Recognition.** The visible pad touches before slowing is credited. A passively airborne racer is not secretly braking against the ground below it.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Moth-Felt Brake](#w14-part-feltbrake) · [Sticktoe Gripstrip](#w14-part-gripstrip)

<a id="w14-action-ram"></a>
### Commit a charge
`W14.ACTION.RAM`

**Required anatomy and situation.** An aligned reachable rival, useful closing motion, supported horn root and adequate contact to deliver the approach.

**Causal sequence.** Align; brace the working structure; approach; horn or opponent makes contact; load transfers into both constructions; separate or remain engaged; recover useful running.

**Builder or breeder influence.** Horn geometry determines contact opportunities. Drive and traction supply the approach. Root and frame support determine how much of it becomes a useful interaction.

**Interference and unsuccessful outcomes.** A rival can move out of reach, brace, intercept, jump or absorb the contact locally. A long horn can miss useful purchase. No specific module grants universal victory over every other.

**Recognition.** Contact is visible at an actual point and the attacker also responds. A missed charge does not damage a remote rival because the animation finished.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Bullhorn Ram](#w14-part-ram) · [Scarab Boxbrace](#w14-part-boxbrace) · [Mole-Cricket Cleats](#w14-part-cleats)

<a id="w14-action-grasp"></a>
### Intercept, hold and release
`W14.ACTION.GRASP`

**Required anatomy and situation.** Available grasping arms, a reachable object, enough closing time and a support path for the resulting load.

**Causal sequence.** Present the grasp envelope; close around actual anatomy; hold or redirect within available leverage; release; recover the folded or running arrangement.

**Builder or breeder influence.** Closure speed, reach, gripping shape and release control define the opportunity. Extra dedicated arms need not remove the native forefeet; replacing forelegs may do so. The exact build decides the locomotion cost.

**Interference and unsuccessful outcomes.** An early close catches nothing; a late close misses; holding can slow both racers. An opponent can break alignment or force a poor load path. A rendered grip is not automatic immobilization.

**Recognition.** The arms occupy space throughout opening, contact and return. A folded arm, a prepared brace and an open trap have distinct silhouettes.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Mantis Catchclaws](#w14-part-catchclaws) · [Commit a charge](#w14-action-ram)

<a id="w14-action-hop"></a>
### Load and execute an active hop
`W14.ACTION.HOP`

**Required anatomy and situation.** A complete launch-capable assembly, a support surface, available work, useful clearance, and a planned recontact opportunity. The roach does not need a rival to use it.

**Causal sequence.** Adopt a loading posture; store work; retain or relax the preload; release through synchronized hind action; leave the support; follow the resulting trajectory; prepare the landing; recover running.

**Builder or breeder influence.** Return determines usable stored energy; synchronization affects unwanted rotation; fitting determines launch orientation; settling affects recovery. Lower preload can be useful for a nearby clearance even with an exceptional high-capacity assembly.

**Interference and unsuccessful outcomes.** A target may move before release; a ceiling may block the trajectory; a landing may become crowded. The animal can abandon a commitment before release under its behavioral policy, with elapsed work and time retained. After takeoff, control depends on actual available anatomy.

**Recognition.** Loaded joints shorten or bow before release. Airborne legs reposition for landing. A high-quality motion interpretation shows commitment and outcome without requiring gigantic jumps every time.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Grasshopper Hoppers](#w14-part-hoppers) · [Planthopper Lockstep](#w14-part-lockstep) · [Receive impact and resume running](#w14-action-land)

<a id="w14-action-ramp"></a>
### Leave a ramp without active jumping
`W14.ACTION.RAMP`

**Required anatomy and situation.** Ground speed and a course shape that redirects or removes support. A native roach can become airborne without a Hopper module.

**Causal sequence.** The body approaches; course contact changes velocity and support; the animal leaves the lip; it travels through air and seeks valid recontact.

**Builder or breeder influence.** Approach speed, brake contact, posture, body mass distribution and passive aero can influence the trajectory. Active jumping is only additional work if the build actually performs it.

**Interference and unsuccessful outcomes.** Loss of useful route, excessive landing demand or a collision can prevent valid completion. A specific prototype retiring every airborne bend is not the world definition of all airborne movement.

**Recognition.** The launch originates at the course. No unexplained hind-spring loading or second energy pulse should be shown when it did not occur.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Engage an approach brake](#w14-action-brake) · [Receive impact and resume running](#w14-action-land) · [Ashveil Split-Vanes](#w14-part-splitvanes)

<a id="w14-action-land"></a>
### Receive impact and resume running
`W14.ACTION.LAND`

**Required anatomy and situation.** A reachable surface, a usable body attitude, actual receiving contacts and support for the load.

**Causal sequence.** Place receiving limbs; contact; compress available travel; dissipate or redirect energy; return toward useful stance; resume the next work cycle.

**Builder or breeder influence.** Landing joints manage travel and load; a Keel reduces continuing oscillation; feet establish purchase. Better sensing can prepare contact earlier but does not replace these structures.

**Interference and unsuccessful outcomes.** A spring can rebound; a hard stop can transmit a harsh impulse; feet can skid; a poor attitude can contact the shell instead of the intended legs. The eventual injury model determines damage, not an assumed universal health tax.

**Recognition.** Compression precedes recovery. Rapid settling is a shorter visible interruption, not an instantaneous teleport back to full running.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Locust Springknees](#w14-part-springknees) · [Larval Keel](#w14-part-keel) · [Sticktoe Contact Set](#w14-part-sticktoe)

<a id="w14-action-slide"></a>
### Guide along a rail
`W14.ACTION.SLIDE`

**Required anatomy and situation.** A wall within reach of a supported guide or native shell and a direction of motion along that wall.

**Causal sequence.** Contact loads the exposed surface; motion slides along it; friction and support determine the loss; separation ends that interaction.

**Builder or breeder influence.** Guide material changes sliding loss. Working width and height determine when it meets the wall. Available support determines how much contact the guide can manage.

**Interference and unsuccessful outcomes.** A soft unsupported root can let the shell take the remaining load. A guide cannot improve a noncontact section merely because it remains equipped.

**Recognition.** The rail-side surface is actually present at the contact. Guiding is not indistinguishable from an invisible magnet pulling the racer through bends.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Beetle Railshoes](#w14-part-railshoes) · [Beetle Guardknuckles](#w14-part-guardknuckles)

<a id="w14-action-flight"></a>
### Sustain powered flight
`W14.ACTION.FLIGHT`

**Required anatomy and situation.** An integrated flight system: working wings, drive, wing-root leverage, support, supply and control. Passive vanes and an active hop are insufficient by themselves.

**Causal sequence.** Generate working wing motion; produce the needed aerodynamic force; adjust it through root and surface control; traverse the aerial course; transition to supported landing or continued flight.

**Builder or breeder influence.** Wing work, root response, drive efficiency, supply and body distribution determine capabilities. Ground-running contact becomes important again at launch or landing, not as a free airborne traction stat.

**Interference and unsuccessful outcomes.** Surfaces can strike gates, lose useful orientation or exceed available output. Disturbance is addressed through actual control authority. Flight competitors obey aerial route completion, not direct travel to the finish.

**Recognition.** Wing roots and the body’s attitude express the maneuver. A hovering gate approach and sustained forward travel need different motion, not a ground sprite raised a few pixels.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Dragonfly Quadwing](#w14-part-quadwing) · [Locust Longwing](#w14-part-longwing) · [Fly Gyropods](#w14-part-gyropods)

<a id="w14-action-right"></a>
### Recover from a toppled posture
`W14.ACTION.RIGHT`

**Required anatomy and situation.** Usable native legs or a fitted recovery assembly with the necessary support, room and stored or supplied work.

**Causal sequence.** Find support; load the available recovery mechanism; alter body attitude; re-establish feet; resume the race only after a valid recovery.

**Builder or breeder influence.** Body shape, useful latch geometry, sufficient force and clearance determine whether the method can work.

**Interference and unsuccessful outcomes.** A trapped or suspended roach cannot use a surface it does not reach. Structural damage may remove the available method. No arbitrary recovery cooldown replaces the physical sequence.

**Recognition.** An extended leg recovery and a Kickback snap are visibly different. Neither erases distance already lost.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Click-Beetle Kickback](#w14-part-kickback) · [Body plan](#w14-anatomy-bodyplan)

<a id="w14-action-control"></a>
### One animal coordinates its construction
`W14.ACTION.CONTROL`

**Native competence.** The host already runs, senses obstacles and responds to ordinary support changes. A beginner does not have to buy a brain module to make a complete kit move.

**Builder configuration.** Placement, preload, stance, outlet orientation and other physical settings alter what the body can do. They are neither learned skills nor inherited genes.

**Handling and conditioning.** Familiarity with release, noise, cases and newly fitted movement can affect preparedness. Conditioning should address a named behavior or response rather than add an invisible permanent motor tier.

**Race plan.** A lanista can favor clean passing, selective contact, early preparation or another supported tendency. This expresses the desired use of anatomy; it cannot cause impossible knowledge or physically unavailable actions.

**Live intervention.** Whether and how the player can issue in-race cues remains a separate interaction decision. The compendium does not require direct steering, a fixed token pool or an input on every corner.

**Course knowledge.** A body can react only to actual cues or prior familiarization. Rules officials determine checkpoint validity; this does not mean the insect reads a hidden checkpoint list or knows an opponent’s genome.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Motor and sensory junction](#w14-anatomy-command) · [Run, accelerate and hold pace](#w14-action-run) · [Load and execute an active hop](#w14-action-hop)

## 6. Breeding objectives, morphological variation and productive habitats

A breeder pursues an outcome in offspring, not a larger generic quality score. The material, shape, development and present condition of an animal must remain distinguishable from the work performed by a fitter.

<a id="w14-breed-causes"></a>
### Five causes that must not be conflated
`W14.BREED.CAUSES`

**Inherited contribution.** The potential for anatomy, material and other declared traits. It is the subject of selective breeding.

**Development.** Whether an individual forms usable tissue under its actual growth conditions. A cramped emergence can damage a promising wing without changing the underlying inheritance.

**Present condition.** Fill, fatigue, fouling, maturity and injury where those apply. They change current usefulness; a full crop does not prove higher inherited capacity.

**Preparation.** The arrangement, preservation and shaping that turn actual source anatomy into a usable component. It can reveal, preserve or redirect value without inventing a new germline.

**Fitting.** The host-specific assembly and settings. A carefully aligned parent does not produce offspring already aligned to a different customer’s roach.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Graft root](#w14-anatomy-root) · [Honeybee Nectar Crop](#w14-part-nectar)

<a id="w14-breed-projects"></a>
### Different breeding projects deserve different stock
`W14.BREED.PROJECTS`

**Complement project.** Add synchronization to a high-return Hopper line. Retain the missing contribution even in an individually modest jumper.

**Direct improvement project.** Improve drive efficiency while retaining acceptable cadence and force. Genuine better output at the same burden remains desirable.

**Production project.** Increase the proportion of usable standard components through dependable reproduction and development. Eggs alone are not finished products.

**Fit project.** Produce useful anatomy within a repeated host or kit envelope. Similar adult size is insufficient if the working roots or motion differ.

**Collection project.** Develop recognizable natural banding or form. Appearance can be the primary objective without claiming a racing advantage.

**Hereditary boundary.** The current working branch keeps donor and host breeding distinct. Assimilating a horn does not automatically place a copy of that paid horn in every offspring, and graft compatibility does not establish reproductive compatibility.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Grasshopper Hoppers](#w14-part-hoppers) · [Cicada Redline](#w14-part-redline) · [Five causes that must not be conflated](#w14-breed-causes)

<a id="w14-breed-source"></a>
### A habitat is a source, not a trait-granting machine
`W14.BREED.SOURCE`

**Population.** People can return for new cohorts, unrelated founders and a different useful stage. A population’s ecology and history can influence available variation without tailoring itself to the player’s current target.

**Field evidence.** A track, molt, movement or contact mark is evidence under its actual conditions. Detailed observation and paid expertise can improve understanding; repeated looking does not force a favorable genetic outcome.

**Quick and detailed routes.** A brief collection service and a personally explored patch may represent the same source or different nearby populations. The compendium requires the relationship to be named; it does not canonize the prototype’s duplicate-source separation.

**Desired anatomy.** An acquisition method is selected for the tissue it must preserve: matched limbs, intact wing margins, contact pads, sensory stalks or whole reproductive stock.

**Return choice.** The catch can become a racer, a breeder, a donor, a collectible, a sale or a released animal. A species discovery does not give ownership of its future bodies.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Different breeding projects deserve different stock](#w14-breed-projects)

**Earlier reference:** `W13.ATLAS.08`, `W13.ECOSYSTEM.03`

<a id="w14-breed-nursery"></a>
### Facilities express life history and production intent
`W14.BREED.NURSERY`

**Dry-bank rearing.** Provides appropriate refuge, laying places, limb-growth space and adult holding for the supported terrestrial profile. The same box does not automatically serve predators or aquatic stages.

**Deep-substrate rearing.** Provides sufficient substrate, resources and access for burrowing juveniles, with adult holding handled according to the actual form.

**Root-to-adult cabinet.** Connects a juvenile feeding system to emergence and adult reproduction. An adult-only cicada cage is a holding facility, not a complete productive line.

**Vertical molt case.** Provides hanging and separation space for growing appendages or stage-specific coordinating anatomy. Quantity without working clearance can reduce intact output.

**Predator partitions.** Separate incompatible animals and permit controlled reproductive contact. Their value is preserved stock and viable output, not a free bonus to inherited aggression.

**Larval/pupal drawer.** Permits allocation between harvest-stage tissue and retained adults. Harvesting every useful larva removes the intended reproductive replacements.

**Pond/emergence system.** Links aquatic growth, emergence support, wing expansion and adult holding. Intact wing yield requires more than a water tank.

**Colony nursery.** Separates productive members, reproductive structure and provisioning. It requires a dedicated donor model rather than copying a two-worker pairing rule.

**Common rule.** A facility changes conditions, care burden, output reliability or feasible life stages. It does not select the best allele or add inherited speed because it is expensive.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Cicada Redline](#w14-part-redline) · [Planthopper Lockstep](#w14-part-lockstep) · [Waxmoth Fatbody](#w14-part-fatbody) · [Dragonfly Quadwing](#w14-part-quadwing) · [Honeybee Nectar Crop](#w14-part-nectar)

<a id="w14-breed-evidence"></a>
### Line identity is not a guarantee
`W14.BREED.EVIDENCE`

**Line.** An intentionally maintained ancestry and selection project. Its name can develop recognition through repeatable observed output and history.

**Brood.** A particular reproductive cohort. Several measurements from one animal do not create several descendants; several siblings do not create several independent broods.

**Observed claim.** State what was measured, under what conditions and among which recorded descendants. Unmeasured animals remain unknown, not automatic failures or successes.

**Changing objective.** A new selection objective may found a branch or a revised line. A catalogued revision number is not automatically a count of generations.

**Ordinary use.** Casual buying and ordinary surplus sales do not require research-grade pedigree paperwork. Evidence becomes valuable where a customer’s particular promise needs it.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Sets and tiers are separate cataloguing systems](#w14-map-sets)

**Earlier reference:** `LINE_SALTSTEP`, `W12.BREEDER.09`

## 7. Grafting, swapping and the life of a component

Swappable living parts are central to RRR. The proposed common workshop practice below protects that identity without converting the host into an indestructible machine or imposing an unexplained injury roll on every routine rebuild. This chapter advances D03/D04 qualitatively; exact times, loss rates, regrowth and lifetime policy remain separate decisions.

<a id="w14-graft-practice"></a>
### Assimilation is innate; preparation is expertise
`W14.GRAFT.PRACTICE`

**Raw grafting.** The defining mutation can incorporate suitable insect anatomy without a licensed commercial kit. This preserves the founding horn-and-pincers improvisation.

**Prepared grafting.** A fitter selects the source, exposes a useful root, supplies support and arranges a repeatable interface. The preparation improves usability and predictability; it does not confer permission to assimilate.

**Working readiness.** Attachment, supply and useful motion may become ready at different points. A root that has seated is not proof that every high-load action is ready. The visual and care state should make the distinction legible.

**Accessibility.** A beginner tin and a competent local Wrangloid provide a dependable route to an ordinary build. Requiring rare surgery skills for every legal kit contradicts the mass-market hobby.

**Freedom.** Expert custom roots and improvised assemblies can exceed commercial patterns. Their difficulties come from actual support, clearance and supply, not a species rejection list.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Molt seam](#w14-anatomy-seam) · [A functional installation has five kinds of continuity](#w14-anatomy-joins) · [Tamiya — FM-A Balanced Starter Pack, 18710](#w14-s06)

<a id="w14-graft-swap"></a>
### A maintained exchange boundary supports repeated rebuilding
`W14.GRAFT.SWAP`

**Common workshop profile.** Prepared sects with intact exchange boundaries can be detached, supported and refitted through the declared ordinary service procedure. Rebuilding is a normal hobby activity, not inherently a gamble that destroys a prized collection.

**Raw constructions.** A fully merged improvised structure may lack a prepared release boundary. Its removal requires an appropriate handling decision rather than the assumption that every graft detaches identically.

**Native material.** Where native anatomy was displaced and retained, it is actual retained material. Where it was consumed, regrowth or a replacement must have its own explanation; a menu choice cannot conjure it.

**Condition.** Damaged roots, inadequate preservation or obstructed motion can make a particular swap more demanding. Those causes are named. No universal per-swap damage tax is adopted here.

**Commercial effect.** Prepared components support second-hand trade, borrowing, repair services and multi-build collections. A seller distinguishes the source history and present interface condition from the component’s original pedigree.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Prepared exchange collar](#w14-anatomy-collar)

**Earlier reference:** `D03`, `D04`, `W05.BENCH.06`

<a id="w14-graft-states"></a>
### Four material states with different meanings
`W14.GRAFT.STATES`

**Working on a host.** Integrated anatomy uses that host’s support and supply. Its current setup belongs to the fitted construction.

**Buffered living sect.** Removed tissue is held in an appropriate support and maintenance environment. It is not undergoing ordinary whole-donor development as if still attached to the original animal.

**Prepared structural material.** Shell-derived rigid or elastic material may retain physical utility under different conditions from an active organ. The living root and the structural part are not always the same material.

**Spent or damaged material.** A part can lose a working function while retaining another use: appearance, study, restoration reference or a salvage preparation explicitly supported by the remaining anatomy.

**No universal shelf rule.** Neither infinite shelf life nor rapid spoilage for every component is established. A preservation class names what must remain intact and how readiness is restored; exact durations belong to later material profiles.

**Related entries / evidence:** [A maintained exchange boundary supports repeated rebuilding](#w14-graft-swap)

<a id="w14-graft-stage"></a>
### Donor stage and graft maintenance are separate histories
`W14.GRAFT.STAGE`

**Before preparation.** The donor’s stage determines which anatomy exists and what future role the animal can serve. A waxmoth adult is not a larval fatbody order.

**After preparation.** The removed component has its own condition and maintenance history. Its parent animal aging in another enclosure does not transform that detached component automatically.

**Integrated growth.** How a host maintains or remodels a graft must be specified by the graft’s profile. Assimilation is not permission for unlimited renewal of rare material or automatic improvement with age.

**Juvenile preparations.** A stage-specific Lockstep remains useful only if the preparation preserves the required working structure. Its availability before extraction and its viability afterward are different design questions.

**Supply implication.** Breeders can sell target-stage stock, prepared inventory or adults for reproduction. These are not interchangeable products with the same transport requirements.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Planthopper Lockstep](#w14-part-lockstep) · [Waxmoth Fatbody](#w14-part-fatbody) · [Four material states with different meanings](#w14-graft-states)

<a id="w14-graft-care"></a>
### Keep consequence without making every hobby a care chore
`W14.GRAFT.CARE`

**Purposeful care.** Maintains an animal, a graft, a productive brood or a valuable condition. Each care practice names its affected object.

**Routine support.** Approved care presets, hired boarding, common feed and standard preservation let casual hobbyists own attractive racers without repeated compulsory clicks.

**Expert work.** Specific conditioning, stage management, preparation and selection offer additional depth where they change a useful outcome.

**Consequence.** Failing a real requirement can damage stock, interrupt production or lose a race according to the later agreed condition model. It does not automatically destroy unrelated facilities and collections.

**Unresolved policy.** Global aging, permanent death, broad injury and regenerative limits remain explicitly open. Defining repeatable workshop swaps does not settle all of them.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Facilities express life history and production intent](#w14-breed-nursery) · [A maintained exchange boundary supports repeated rebuilding](#w14-graft-swap)

**Earlier reference:** `D07`

## 8. Set architecture, product families and complete projects

A coordinated set is desirable because it solves a construction problem, gives it a recognizable identity and supplies the actual parts. The source kit/upgrade distinction supports complete host tins and conversion sets without assuming a universal set bonus. [W14.S05](#w14-s05) [W14.S06](#w14-s06)

<a id="w14-set-tin"></a>

<a id="w14-set-language"></a>
### One catalogue, several kinds of desirability
`W14.SET.LANGUAGE`

**Pattern name.** Identifies a preparation or coordinated construction. It should be recognizable in anatomy and action.

**Breeder line.** Identifies selected ancestry and its recorded objectives. It is not automatically owned by every shop selling its offspring.

**Printed edition.** Identifies the contents, appearance and promises offered by a particular producer at that time.

**Performance band.** Summarizes a declared measurement profile. It is not a price, a legal class or a historical authenticity claim.

**Collector variant.** May be desirable for natural markings, packaging, provenance, restoration or a famous builder’s finish. A limited tin can contain ordinary excellent components rather than inaccessible superior genes.

**Historic recreation.** Names what is original, retained, replaced or reproduced. A descendant host, an original winning animal, a revived donor line and a modern kit copied from an old sheet are different collectibles.

## 9. The hobby’s institutions and material culture

Institutions exist because they perform useful work for people with different interests. Their roles do not become exclusive player classes or biological permissions. Existing place and organization identities are retained.

<a id="w14-institution-two_nets"></a>
### Two Nets Live Stock
`W14.INSTITUTION.TWO_NETS`

**Role.** An accessible Wrangloid counter centered on whole living insects, with prepared anatomy and coordinated kits alongside them.

**Practices and knowledge.** Tank stock cards distinguish source, stage, visible condition, intended uses and the evidence the shop actually holds. The counter can demonstrate a component without pretending to know every wild animal’s ancestry.

**Livelihood.** Buys ordinary stock and selected output, keeps useful breeders, commissions preparations and sells complete projects. Care, space and specialist knowledge make its work worth paying for.

**Boundary.** Not a loot dispenser, genetics oracle or mandatory licensing office. Anyone can catch, breed or prepare outside the shop.

**Characteristic objects.** Live-stock tanks, an intake bench, separated preparation cradles, illustrated tins and a small demonstration run make its different activities physically legible.

**Earlier reference:** `SHOP_TWO_NETS`

<a id="w14-institution-cinder"></a>
### Cinderhouse Broodworks
`W14.INSTITUTION.CINDER`

**Role.** A cooperative breeder and supplier whose repeatable output supports independent Wrangloids.

**Practices and knowledge.** Maintains separate research and supply objectives: a premium force/support line, ordinary kit stock, retained replacements and known useful preparation windows.

**Livelihood.** Earns through suitable cohorts, selected founders and dependable dimensions or material properties. Finished kits can combine its stock with another supplier’s rather than require brand purity.

**Boundary.** Not the owner of every retailer carrying its stock and not a universal authority that decides which insects may reproduce.

**Characteristic objects.** Cohort racks, retained founder cases, comparative sample sets and shipping supports distinguish a broodworks from a decorative pet shop.

**Earlier reference:** `ORG_CINDER`

### Foundry Club
`W14.INSTITUTION.FOUNDRY`

**Role.** A recurring venue for assembly, practice, racing, demonstrations and social comparison.

**Practices and knowledge.** Provides different services: free social advice, supported bench use, booked testing, named events and display space. Not every conversation is a contract or quest.

**Livelihood.** Hosting, fitting and facility services can support workers and organizers. New entrants can borrow access to knowledge or facilities without becoming breeders first.

**Boundary.** Only a declared qualifying event advances a championship. A display award does not secretly alter racing stats.

**Characteristic objects.** Section shelves, repairable benches, timing desk, racer cases, trophies and a display cabinet connect everyday practice to public competition.

### The Saltglass Counter
`W14.INSTITUTION.SALT`

**Practices and knowledge.** Keeps an asking-price claim distinct from evidence. A striking specimen can be valuable to a collector despite ordinary racing material.

**Livelihood.** Commissions targeted searches, arranges consignments and finds appropriate buyers. A market for beautiful or historic stock does not require a universal fixed rarity price.

**Boundary.** Not every inhabitant is wealthy, every offering is rare, or every trade is a championship upgrade.

**Livelihood.** Earn through scheduled capacity, careful handling and reliable arrival. A desired part can cost more in a remote settlement because reaching it requires actual work.

**Boundary.** A departure receipt is not arrival. Carriers do not freeze every larva at its most profitable stage or make all towns instantly share information.

`W14.INSTITUTION.PRESS`

**Livelihood.** Helps people discover products, venues and desirable projects. Attention can lead to inquiries, but payments require actual agreements and delivered work.

**Boundary.** Not a world-wide omniscient feed. Distant people learn through publication and distribution, not because a player opened a results panel.

`W14.INSTITUTION.FITTER`

**Role.** A builder who makes useful anatomy work well on a particular host.

**Practices and knowledge.** Explains the requested result, supports the load path, maintains exchange boundaries, arranges physical fit and distinguishes restoration from inherited improvement.

**Boundary.** Expertise is valuable, but no profession unlock is required to attempt the work yourself.

<a id="w14-institution-common"></a>
### The common bench language is a practice, not a monopoly
`W14.INSTITUTION.COMMON`

**Purpose.** Hobbyists can compare preparations and reproduce useful kits because they share vocabulary for source anatomy, measured behavior, fitting and condition.

**What it standardizes.** A published pattern tells the recipient what is included, which body region it uses, what must be supplied and which settings remain adjustable.

**What it does not control.** It does not own the mutation, ban raw grafting or require every independent invention to become a branded product before entering Open.

**Local variation.** Communities can use regional names and different construction traditions while recording enough common information for visitors to understand the actual object.

**Commercial value.** Reliable descriptions reduce unsuitable purchases and let an exceptional independent breeder sell beyond their immediate acquaintances.

<a id="w14-institution-seasons"></a>
### The hobby has a cycle without becoming an appointment game
`W14.INSTITUTION.SEASONS`

**Local continuity.** Practice, informal rivalries, retail and breeding continue outside the headline championship. A world-popular hobby cannot consist only of the player’s finite event list.

**Public rhythm.** Stock releases, new course plans, qualification periods, display meets, traveling visitors and retrospective issues give the community reasons to return.

**Biological timing.** Different donors produce useful stages on their own life cycles. Sporting demand can make a stage timely without causing the genes or insects to appear out of nothing.

**Access.** Later race qualification controls entry to that competition, not the right to catch a donor or the existence of a superior trait.

**Player time.** The default offline pause remains a participation principle. Specific schedules should create planning choices within the world, not punish the player for leaving the application.

## 10. The sporting world and the course as an object

Courses, equipment policies, qualification and competitive prestige define different things. The compendium describes the sport that the world can support; the limitations of an earlier two-roach experimental solver are not a limit on what the sport is.

<a id="w14-sport-axes"></a>
### An event is described on independent axes
`W14.SPORT.AXES`

**Locomotion.** Ground racing and sustained-flight racing use suitable routes and different performance demands. A winged silhouette is not enough to classify a flyer; actual locomotion is what matters.

**Equipment.** Open permits unrestricted competitive constructions within the advertised course and basic validity conditions. Speedsters excludes dedicated weapons. Fireworks requires meaningful functioning offense and light protection. Each equipment policy can be stated independently of competitive standing.

**Competition.** Local, Regional, Factional and world-level participation concern qualification and representation. They do not determine whether an insect can exist, be captured or reproduce.

**Format.** A time trial, a single race, heats and finals, or a multi-race championship need their own announced rules. The earlier mirrored-pair programme is one experimental format, not the only possible event in a world-popular hobby.

**Result.** The race requires valid course completion. Finish order determines a race victory. A championship can combine the results of its declared races; it does not silently become a damage contest.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Tamiya — Official Mini 4WD Race Regulations](#w14-s10) · [Sustain powered flight](#w14-action-flight)

**Earlier reference:** `C10`, `C11`, `C12`, `C13`, `C14`, `W11.FORMAT.01`

<a id="w14-sport-boundaries"></a>
### Equipment freedom and useful sporting boundaries
`W14.SPORT.BOUNDARIES`

**Open.** A course must specify where the racers start, where they must go and what counts as finishing. Those requirements do not justify a universal parts budget or a hidden ban on inventive anatomy.

**Speedsters.** Ordinary biting, collision and body contact remain part of racing. Dedicated attacking anatomy is judged by what the fitted preparation can do, not the shop label or a promise to use it peacefully.

**Fireworks.** Minimum attack refers to an available, functioning offensive construction. Light protection refers to its real protective capacity and coverage; a heavily armored frame cannot escape the rule simply by being named a brace.

**Hoppers and flyers.** A ballistic jump spends a launch and returns to a landing. Sustained flight repeatedly supplies aerial propulsion or support. The exact border, including glide-assisted and mixed-locomotion builds, belongs in the event’s published capability definition, not an arbitrary insect-name list.

**No forced austerity.** A newcomer may choose an affordable horn or hopper project in an appropriate event. Learning the hobby does not require an artificially weaponless opening culture.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Bullhorn Ram](#w14-part-ram) · [Grasshopper Hoppers](#w14-part-hoppers) · [Scarab Laminate](#w14-part-laminate) · [Dragonfly Quadwing](#w14-part-quadwing)

<a id="w14-sport-ladder"></a>
### The road to New World City
`W14.SPORT.LADDER`

**Established route.** Locals → Regionals → Factionals → faction representation in New World City → the What’s Left of the World Derby.

**Local purpose.** Build a recognizable competitive record among people who can see the roach and its construction. Some local events qualify competitors; ordinary club races can exist without doing so.

**Regional purpose.** Meet a broader pool of constructors, sources and course traditions. More varied competition should expose real strengths and weaknesses, not apply an invisible regional multiplier to every opponent.

**Factional purpose.** Select representatives through the faction’s explicitly defined sporting process. Institutions may fund travel, prepare entrants, lend stock or commission builds; representation is not a species-ownership monopoly.

**World purpose.** The mature hobby converges on its most visible championship. The final’s prestige can sustain manufacturers, collectors, sponsors and publications without making World City the only place where the hobby matters.

**Unwritten details.** Named factions, territories, selection quotas, championship calendar and travel requirements need dedicated entries. They are not quietly supplied by a prototype’s two unlocked event cards.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Foundry Club](#w14-institution-foundry) · [Cinderhouse Broodworks](#w14-institution-cinder) · [The Case Runners](#w14-institution-cases)

**Earlier reference:** `C14`, `D11`

<a id="w14-sport-course"></a>
### A modular course has four separate identities
`W14.SPORT.COURSE`

**Pattern.** A repeatable section design has connectors, a traveled surface, boundary geometry and any necessary supports. Its visual scrap material does not replace those properties.

**Physical piece.** A club or hobbyist owns a particular section. It can be maintained, repaired, painted, lent and returned. One physical piece cannot occupy two simultaneous layouts.

**Layout.** An arrangement of pieces forms a route or set of routes. Connections, dimensions, elevation, passing space, mandatory gates and finish distance describe the construction.

**Event configuration.** An event selects that layout version, permitted routes, lap requirements, equipment conditions and procedures. Later edits cannot change the course remembered by an old result.

**Historical plan.** A drawing or saved plan preserves knowledge. Recreating it requires suitable actual sections and supports; owning its diagram is not owning the course.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Trackmaker and event crew](#w14-institution-trackmaker)

**Earlier reference:** `W09.TRACK.01`, `W09.TRACK.10`

<a id="w14-sport-contact"></a>
### Course surfaces ask different things of the same body
`W14.SPORT.CONTACT`

**Smooth channel.** A clear straight can reward pace and acceleration. Rail contact still depends on the line and neighbors; a guide gives no friction advantage while it touches nothing.

**Loose insert.** Ash or fragmented material changes foot purchase and possible fouling. The same geometry can therefore support a different contact choice without an arbitrary region-wide stat penalty.

**Raised approach.** A ventral brake engages only if actual geometry brings it into contact. A rise is one possible trigger; the universe is not limited to the final thirty percent of one prototype piece.

**Landing.** Trajectory, available space, arrival posture, compliant travel, damping and return to useful contact all matter. Some constructions need a longer landing; others can carry a more controlled approach.

**Shared section.** An open pack channel permits passing and contact. Solid lane walls still block physical interactions. Lane-guided and shared-space tracks can both belong to the hobby.

**Aerial section.** A Flyer course needs a defined three-dimensional route and completion sequence. Ordered hoops are a valid construction vocabulary; simply lifting a ground ribbon off the floor does not supply aerial decisions.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Run, accelerate and hold pace](#w14-action-run) · [Engage an approach brake](#w14-action-brake) · [Receive impact and resume running](#w14-action-land) · [Guide along a rail](#w14-action-slide) · [Sustain powered flight](#w14-action-flight)

<a id="w14-sport-procedures"></a>
### Procedures protect the race without replacing it
`W14.SPORT.PROCEDURES`

**Before entry.** Publish the meaningful geometry, equipment policy, participation requirements, prize terms and consequences of retirement. Inspection assesses the actual construction.

**At the start.** All entrants follow the same declared release procedure. A physical or timed event format must address starting-position advantages appropriately rather than assume symmetry.

**Between races.** Any permitted rebuilding and inspection interval belongs to the format. A frozen completed heat never follows a later change in anatomy.

**After the finish.** Present the winner, placing, close finishes, records and earned qualifications before optional diagnosis. No finish, a withdrawal and a legitimate loss are different outcomes.

**Care and consequence.** Race damage can materially affect the animal or its components. The compendium must distinguish repairable impairment, retirement, preparation damage and permanent loss before an implementation selects their probabilities.

**Limits of imported rules.** Tamiya supplies evidence that inspection and classification are meaningful parts of the hobby. Its brand-only or damage restrictions are not transplanted into RRR’s Open class.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Tamiya — Official Mini 4WD Race Regulations](#w14-s10) · [Keep consequence without making every hobby a care chore](#w14-graft-care)

## 11. Material and visual object briefs

These are construction and presentation requirements for artists and content authors. They specify what an object communicates and how it participates in the hobby, without prescribing an engine, UI framework or final asset resolution.

<a id="w14-object-grammar"></a>
### Recognizable anatomy before a stat panel
`W14.OBJECT.GRAMMAR`

**Host continuity.** Keep the native head, thorax and abdomen recognizable enough to identify the named animal through rebuilding. A grafted racer should read as one incorporated organism, not a pile of unrelated item thumbnails.

**Foreign anatomy.** Preserve the donor’s characteristic material or shape where it helps recognition: a horn’s curve, folded grasping arm, ribbed drive bundle, segmented reserve or pleated wing surface.

**Junctions.** A root should look incorporated and supported. Prepared collars may remain visible as useful construction details; avoid dressing every part as a bolted metal appliance when the premise is biological assimilation.

**Material roles.** Supporting shell is rigid where it carries load; flexible seams articulate; contact pads deform where they touch; membranes fold and tension; reserves appear filled or depleted through their own form. These distinctions carry information.

**Finish.** Applied colors and markings coexist with natural variants and breeder signatures. They do not need an invented performance effect. Essential motion, vents, contact surfaces and ownership identifiers must remain readable.

**Scale.** The working direction remains bench-built and hand-carried. Absolute dimensions await the scale chapter; final artists should not alternate casually between tiny insects and rideable monsters.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Host](#w14-anatomy-host) · [Graft root](#w14-anatomy-root) · [Stable banded shell](#w14-morph-banding)

<a id="w14-object-tankcard"></a>
### Wrangloid tank card
`W14.OBJECT.TANKCARD`

**Appearance and construction.** A small replaceable card beside a specific enclosure, with a clear stock name, stage, source or line and an illustrated distinguishing feature. Different individuals can carry attached smaller marks.

**Information and function.** Identifies what the customer is looking at, whether it is offered as a breeder, donor, display specimen or ordinary stock, and which measurements are known.

**Hobby use.** A hand-amended condition note or a demonstrated property can materially change the purchase decision. A breeding animal and a harvested part need different cards.

**Misreading to prevent.** The card cannot expose unknown genetics or make every specimen in the tank identically excellent. Rare, measured and suitable must remain different labels.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Two Nets Live Stock](#w14-institution-two_nets)

<a id="w14-object-tray"></a>
### Prepared sect tray
`W14.OBJECT.TRAY`

**Appearance and construction.** A shallow, cleanable tray with shaped root supports, separate paired wells where required, a specimen tag and space for the actual fitting instructions.

**Information and function.** Keeps delicate joints aligned and makes the assembled unit intelligible: which tissues are supplied together, where its root belongs and what native anatomy it replaces.

**Hobby use.** A paired leg assembly occupies one coordinated support. A brake strip prepared from the same foot material appears in a different support and carries a different purpose.

**Misreading to prevent.** The tray does not regenerate removed tissue or preserve every material indefinitely. Its support and medium must match the prepared object.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Four material states with different meanings](#w14-graft-states)

<a id="w14-object-tin"></a>
### Complete living-kit tin
`W14.OBJECT.TIN`

**Appearance and construction.** An illustrated lid showing the promised construction; protected host compartment; labeled sect wells; finish sheet; physical separator between the living host and loose preparations.

**Information and function.** Declares one real host, the actual preparations, native functions retained, and a build guide. A conversion tin prominently states that no host is included.

**Hobby use.** The recipient opens a project, identifies the parts, places them and gives the resulting roach its own identity. A signature edition can reproduce a recognizable design without cloning the original winner.

**Misreading to prevent.** A painted lid is not evidence that the contents satisfy the recipe. Packaging cannot stop living care, create duplicate components or install the build automatically.

**Related entries / evidence:** [One catalogue, several kinds of desirability](#w14-set-language)

<a id="w14-object-guide"></a>
### Exploded assembly sheet
`W14.OBJECT.GUIDE`

**Appearance and construction.** A clear host outline with separately drawn prepared assemblies, their roots, orientation, working clearance and retained native structures. Pair-matched pieces share a visible grouping.

**Information and function.** Translates a complex construction into an understandable first build. Alternate preparations show their actual arrangement instead of silently occupying an identical generic slot.

**Hobby use.** Expert annotations can document preload, reserve position, contact choice and allowed substitutions. A restored historic sheet can have collection value separate from current performance.

**Misreading to prevent.** The sheet must distinguish fit instructions from donor quality. Following an angle cannot give an ordinary part a nonexistent inherited trait.

**Related entries / evidence:** [A functional installation has five kinds of continuity](#w14-anatomy-joins)

<a id="w14-object-nursery"></a>
### Stage-aware brood rack
`W14.OBJECT.NURSERY`

**Appearance and construction.** An enclosure system with visible laying or egg surfaces, appropriate young-stage space, accessible cohort marks and clearly separate retained reproductive stock.

**Information and function.** Communicates the species-specific production process. Roots, soil columns, climbing stems, flight space and pupation sleeves are purposeful fittings, not interchangeable decorations.

**Hobby use.** The occupied chamber, current stage and intended retained adults can be understood at a glance. Detailed pedigree and measurements stay attached to that cohort rather than becoming a second disconnected inventory.

**Misreading to prevent.** The illustration may show observed living young, not reveal unhatched survival outcomes. Extra glass does not create fertile founders or improve genes.

<a id="w14-object-case"></a>
### Wildlands collection case
`W14.OBJECT.CASE`

**Appearance and construction.** Individual supports for legs, wings or whole animals; breathable or otherwise appropriate compartment details; useful source tags; tool storage that does not crush the catch.

**Information and function.** Makes the intended collection strategy visible. A careful specimen case prioritizes useful anatomy; a bulk carrier prioritizes volume under its declared handling conditions.

**Hobby use.** An insect’s identity and source tag continue from the field to intake. A preserved perch or sleeve can reveal why that capture method was chosen.

**Misreading to prevent.** Capacity depends on actual occupants and the declared handling profile, not rarity colors. A case is not a universal life-stage freezer.

**Related entries / evidence:** [A habitat is a source, not a trait-granting machine](#w14-breed-source)

<a id="w14-object-section"></a>
### A repaired modular course section
`W14.OBJECT.SECTION`

**Appearance and construction.** A shaped racing channel or other declared route component, reliable connector ends, a distinct working surface, support points, an owner mark and visible repairs that do not obscure its geometry.

**Information and function.** Makes modular ownership and different surface behavior legible. Clean enamel, loose inserts, raised approaches and abrasive rails should not all be indistinguishable scrap.

**Hobby use.** It can travel in a section case, join a club pool, become part of a new layout and reappear in a historic recreation. Repair work can preserve utility and sentimental value.

**Misreading to prevent.** A backdrop crossing is not automatically a connected overpass. Cosmetic rust does not secretly create an unannounced obstacle.

**Related entries / evidence:** [A modular course has four separate identities](#w14-sport-course)

<a id="w14-object-passport"></a>
### Racer passport and result strip
`W14.OBJECT.PASSPORT`

**Appearance and construction.** A durable identity record with a recognizable host image, chosen name, owner or team mark, configuration references and detachable or replaceable race-result entries.

**Information and function.** Separates the animal from its current construction and records meaningful achievements. A trophy refers to the entered configuration without permanently fixing the creature in that shape.

**Hobby use.** It supports displays, restoration projects, public build features and provenance-led trade. Ownership changes can be recorded without erasing earlier results.

**Misreading to prevent.** Paperwork does not grant missing animal ownership, expose a private genome or make an unverified seller claim true.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Host](#w14-anatomy-host)

<a id="w14-object-motion"></a>
### Movement is part of an asset’s identity
`W14.OBJECT.MOTION`

**What should be recognizable.** A working muscle tensions, a linked pair coordinates, a foot plants and peels, a horn loads its root, a grasping arm closes, a spring compresses, and a damper moves relative to the host.

**What must agree.** The visible action corresponds to the fitted preparation and current circumstances. A host without Hoppers cannot display their launch; a brake above the surface cannot create a contact skid.

**No compulsory noise.** Camera shake, motion blur, screen-wide flashes and constant decorative particles are not substitutes for readable anatomy. Race position and an important action must remain followable.

**Failure recognition.** A late clasp, insufficient takeoff clearance, snagged contact or prolonged settling should look different. That difference gives the breeder or builder a useful next question.

**Unresolved art decisions.** Final animation frame budgets, viewpoint, pixel scale and rendering style require their own art-direction record; the compendium specifies the information and character to preserve, not a guessed engine pipeline.

## 12. Integrated reference chains

Each chain identifies a desire, a biological target, a construction and a customer. These are reusable design cases, not scripted quests, mandatory recipes or guaranteed outcomes.

<a id="w14-chain-contact"></a>
### A genuinely better contact set
`W14.CHAIN.CONTACT`

**Desire.** The racer has useful drive but its current feet remain planted too long between steps. Alternatively, a maker needs contact sets for a quick-stepping kit.

**Reference function.** The tire function is contact. Distinguish sufficient purchase from rapid release, and inspect both under the appropriate surface and load.

**Source and selection.** Glazed Overgrowth supplies Glassfoot stock. Observe clean attachment and release; retain a quick-releasing animal as a complement to an already high-purchase line even if it is not the best immediate donor.

**Breeding and production.** Rear the appropriate stages in a vertical arrangement. Record the useful inherited contributions separately from damage, recent humidity and preparation. Suitable offspring supply front/middle contact sets or alternatively brake material.

**Fitting and race or display.** Fit real pads to the selected limb pairs. A faster release is visible in renewed stepping; it does not give another free drive organ or cancel unsupported turning loads.

**World and livelihood.** Ordinary offspring can supply standard kits. A selected line can serve rapid-release component orders or breeding-stock requests. Collector markings remain separately desirable.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Sticktoe Contact Set](#w14-part-sticktoe) · [Different breeding projects deserve different stock](#w14-breed-projects) · [Glassline](#w14-set-glass)

<a id="w14-chain-hop"></a>
### The founding jumper, developed into a line
`W14.CHAIN.HOP`

**Desire.** The owner wants a fast, spectacular Hopper racer; the current high-return stock launches strongly but inconsistently.

**Reference function.** The compound assembly contains propulsion-support geometry and landing roles; active jumping is RRR’s own extension of the build-and-race foundation.

**Source and selection.** Switchyard Reclamation supplies paired hind limbs and unrelated founders. Seek close release timing and intact joints; the single farthest observed escape leap is not a complete measurement.

**Breeding and production.** Cross for the missing contribution, retain useful partial outcomes and preserve reproductive adults. Decide whether a particular offspring should provide a full Hopper, a joint preparation or future breeding stock.

**Fitting and race or display.** Preload, alignment, approach and landing support remain builder choices. A favorable line creates better possibilities; the creature still needs a physical launch and landing.

**World and livelihood.** The same lineage can supply personal upgrades, ordinary Hopper tins, selected pairs and appearance-led editions. A famous victory advertises one recorded build, not guaranteed victories for its descendants.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Grasshopper Hoppers](#w14-part-hoppers) · [Locust Springknees](#w14-part-springknees) · [Load and execute an active hop](#w14-action-hop) · [Last Laugh / Third Friend family](#w14-set-hop)

<a id="w14-chain-apron"></a>
### A course generates two different material requests
`W14.CHAIN.APRON`

**Desire.** A club introduces a short raised approach and an abrasive outside rail. Some racers arrive too fast; others lose work while riding the wall.

**Reference function.** Contact braking removes approach motion. A guide changes supported rail contact. They are not interchangeable stability items.

**Source and selection.** Dry Feltworks supplies resilient ventral material; Underpipe Aprons supplies low-loss supporting shell. Preserve the specific surface or paired shell regions, not merely the whole animal’s cosmetic condition.

**Breeding and production.** Material breeders pursue an appropriate friction/retention/travel combination for the pad, or low sliding loss plus support for the guide. One target is not inferred from the other.

**Fitting and race or display.** The fitter sets clearance and guide position. The brake must touch, the guide must touch its rail, and neither works everywhere because it belongs to a purchased set.

**World and livelihood.** A trackmaker earns for construction; breeders and fitters supply the resulting material needs; the club can borrow sections and publish the changed layout. A new track does not automatically force every entrant to buy the same equipment.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Moth-Felt Brake](#w14-part-feltbrake) · [Beetle Railshoes](#w14-part-railshoes) · [Apron / approach set](#w14-set-apron) · [Trackmaker and event crew](#w14-institution-trackmaker)

<a id="w14-chain-load"></a>
### Useful force without confusing support and armor
`W14.CHAIN.LOAD`

**Desire.** An Open builder wants a horn-driven racer capable of accelerating with substantial mounted anatomy.

**Reference function.** Muscle output, delivery geometry, supporting frame and protective shell have different functions.

**Source and selection.** Cooling Works offers scarab stock, Bullhorn sources and nearby useful power donors. Select force under load, good structural support per mass and a horn root suited to its contact geometry.

**Breeding and production.** A scarab line can specialize in drive or supporting plates. Disjoint harvested regions may supply several products; the resulting donor state and vitality consequences remain explicit.

**Fitting and race or display.** The construction aligns charge forces through adequate support. A different gear ratio can help the intended approach while lowering free running speed. Adding armor is an additional decision, not a free property of a broad frame.

**World and livelihood.** Loaded Open kits, light Fireworks derivatives and standalone structural stock have different customers. A protective shell may make a build illegal in one division while being desirable in another.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Scarab Torque Bundle](#w14-part-torque) · [Mole-Cricket Lowstep](#w14-part-lowstep) · [Scarab Boxbrace](#w14-part-boxbrace) · [Bullhorn Ram](#w14-part-ram) · [Scarab Laminate](#w14-part-laminate)

<a id="w14-chain-flyer"></a>
### A Flyer industry needs more than wings
`W14.CHAIN.FLYER`

**Desire.** A lanista wants to enter the aerial sport; a supplier wants to produce complete flight assemblies rather than fragile display surfaces.

**Reference function.** Body, output, leverage, reserve and control remain recognizable construction roles, but powered flight requires a genuinely different movement system.

**Source and selection.** Cooling-pond margins can supply the proposed Glasswing line. Intact adult surfaces and supported wing roots matter to immediate components; viable reproductive stock and the aquatic stages matter to continuing production.

**Breeding and production.** The producer supplies aquatic rearing, emergence, space to expand wings, adult care and appropriate reproduction. A tank that merely keeps adults on display does not complete the cycle.

**Fitting and race or display.** The host needs supported roots, flight drive, wing-root transmission, adequate delivery and information usable by actual correction movements. Aerial paths and gates define the race; a browfoil is not a flight kit.

**World and livelihood.** Suppliers can sell adult wing/root assemblies, breeding stock, emergence services, complete Skyhook projects and replacement components. The compendium defines this industry even before an engine implements flight.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Dragonfly Quadwing](#w14-part-quadwing) · [Locust Longwing](#w14-part-longwing) · [Honeybee Nectar Crop](#w14-part-nectar) · [Fly Gyropods](#w14-part-gyropods) · [Sustain powered flight](#w14-action-flight) · [Skyhook / Flyer construction](#w14-set-sky)

<a id="w14-chain-display"></a>
### A collection piece with an honest history
`W14.CHAIN.DISPLAY`

**Desire.** A collector wants a historic style or a distinctive natural form, not necessarily the fastest currently available build.

**Reference function.** Body choice, color, decals and the physical hobby’s product editions supply an appearance and collecting reference. RRR adds living lineages and graft provenance.

**Source and selection.** A distinctive shell marking, carefully selected Ashveil form or preserved host lineage may supply the desired identity. Its racing measurements remain independent.

**Breeding and production.** Breed supported appearance traits only through a declared heredity profile; record the difference between an original specimen, a descendant, a restored object and a new replica.

**Fitting and race or display.** A finisher composes natural coloration, graft junctions, marks and silhouette while preserving working surfaces. A replica follows a recognizable construction without claiming to be the original animal.

**World and livelihood.** A collector, restorer, finisher, publisher or maker can each supply a useful part of the project. A provenance premium requires a buyer who values the supported history; the history is not a performance multiplier.

**Related entries / evidence:** [Stable banded shell](#w14-morph-banding) · [One catalogue, several kinds of desirability](#w14-set-language) · [The Saltglass Counter](#w14-institution-salt) · [Shell finisher and display builder](#w14-institution-finisher) · [Tamiya — Product classification definitions](#w14-s12)

## 13. Editorial amendments and remaining design commitments

The amendments below change how earlier material should be interpreted in the compendium. They do not rewrite historical test inputs, erase useful experiments, or claim that downstream software already implements the new qualitative definitions.

<a id="w14-amend-a01"></a>
### Prototype scope is not the size of the world
`W14.AMEND.A01`

**Earlier ambiguity.** Six specimens in a patch, a fixed board size, paired heats and three quantitative loci were used by bounded experiments.

**Current compendium interpretation.** World variety, species reproductive models, race formats and habitat populations are authored independently of those technical bounds. Preserve the older values as named experimental profiles only.

**Purpose.** Prevents subsequent authors from mistaking a small demonstration for a universal biological or sporting limit.

**Earlier reference:** `W13.ATLAS.03`, `W13.APPROACH.01`, `W11.FORMAT.01`, `W07.BREED.02`

<a id="w14-amend-a02"></a>
### The host and the frame are different objects
`W14.AMEND.A02`

**Earlier ambiguity.** Early car-to-roach mappings could make body selection, chassis-like native form and frame preparation overlap.

**Current compendium interpretation.** The host is the persistent animal; its body plan supplies native geometry. A frame preparation alters load-bearing support. Protective carapace is a third function.

**Purpose.** Gives three legitimate construction and breeding decisions without charging the same anatomy three times.

**Earlier reference:** `W07.REFERENCE.06`, `W08.HOST.06`

<a id="w14-amend-a03"></a>
### Synchronization is not a variable gear ratio
`W14.AMEND.A03`

**Earlier ambiguity.** Hip leverage and paired timing appeared under a shared gear-equivalent category.

**Current compendium interpretation.** Keep their functions separate. Lowstep and Longthrow change force/travel delivery; Lockstep coordinates timing. A combined assembly can contain both, but timing does not create force or change ratio by name.

**Purpose.** Allows purposeful donor selection and a correct construction explanation before any equation is chosen.

**Earlier reference:** `W02.MAP.TRANSFER`, `W07.PART.02`

<a id="w14-amend-a04"></a>
### Stabilization needs a causal distinction
`W14.AMEND.A04`

**Earlier ambiguity.** Sensing, physical bracing, wall contact and sliding guidance all help some aspect of handling.

**Current compendium interpretation.** Gyropods detect and communicate; Counterbraces make support contacts; Guardknuckles catch a lean; Railshoes carry sliding contact. Each needs its own circumstance and actual physical effect.

**Purpose.** Prevents generic stability stacking and gives four different reasons to seek stock.

**Earlier reference:** `W07.PART.07`, `W09.RAIL.01`

<a id="w14-amend-a05"></a>
### Contact material is not one grip number
`W14.AMEND.A05`

**Earlier ambiguity.** An adhesive surface can be described as universally better because it holds harder.

**Current compendium interpretation.** Distinguish purchase, release, area, orientation, load, contamination and friction against the named surface. The same tissue can serve running feet or a brake through different preparation.

**Purpose.** Preserves the purpose of contact compounds while making the insect translation mechanically distinct.

**Earlier reference:** `W07.PART.03`, `W09.BRAKE.02`

<a id="w14-amend-a06"></a>
### Incoming impact, rebound and recovery are separate
`W14.AMEND.A06`

**Earlier ambiguity.** Landing joints, added tissue mass and balance response risk being treated as interchangeable time reductions.

**Current compendium interpretation.** Compliance accommodates impact; a properly configured damper dissipates relative motion; sensing and actuators restore useful posture. None can silently substitute for the missing anatomy of another.

**Purpose.** Connects visible action, selective traits and component preparation instead of awarding an unexplained handling buff.

**Earlier reference:** `W07.PART.08`, `W13.CONTINUITY.08`

<a id="w14-amend-a07"></a>
### Prepared exchange does not own assimilation
`W14.AMEND.A07`

**Earlier ambiguity.** The historical collar fixture supports safe reversible demonstrations but could be read as a universal required proprietary socket.

**Current compendium interpretation.** Assimilation remains innate and broadly compatible. Prepared exchange boundaries support repeatable hobby rebuilding. Raw incorporation, vital extraction, damage and material preservation retain their own consequences.

**Purpose.** Preserves both the founding slap-on invention and the mature kit industry without making either nonsensical.

**Earlier reference:** `W05.BENCH.06`, `W07.ANATOMY.09`, `D03`, `D04`

<a id="w14-amend-a08"></a>
### Named products do not define a hidden taxonomy
`W14.AMEND.A08`

**Earlier ambiguity.** Historical trade names such as Locust Springknees can appear beside a Switchyard Hopper source and imply a new unrecorded species.

**Current compendium interpretation.** Record donor form and preparation pattern separately. Keep a useful established product name, but identify the actual anatomy supplying it. A future locust donor requires its own monograph.

**Purpose.** Avoids accidental extra species, duplicated tissue and conflicting source routes.

**Earlier reference:** `W02.DONOR.HOPPER`, `W07.BREED.01`

<a id="w14-amend-a09"></a>
### Availability, improvement and collector value remain independent
`W14.AMEND.A09`

**Earlier ambiguity.** Grade labels, rare finds, prototype thresholds and larger geometry could be mistaken for one universal progression ladder.

**Current compendium interpretation.** Use named component-purpose measurements for performance, explicit supply history for availability, and supported appearance/provenance for collection. Genuine multi-property upgrades remain possible; operating alternatives need not be ranked universally.

**Purpose.** Protects both satisfying upgrades and nuanced construction choices.

**Earlier reference:** `W08.QUALIFY.03`, `W12.RECIPE.08`

<a id="w14-amend-a10"></a>
### Qualitative birth-to-part continuity precedes a complete simulation
`W14.AMEND.A10`

**Earlier ambiguity.** A useful juvenile stage, growing donor and harvested sect can be conflated into one changing record.

**Current compendium interpretation.** The living donor progresses through its declared stages. Prepared anatomy then has its own identity and condition. Retained adult breeders, juvenile components and post-harvest material cannot be substituted silently.

**Purpose.** Makes life-stage collecting, production and storage understandable without imposing a universal timer.

**Earlier reference:** `D06`, `W07.BREED.05`, `W13.CLOCK.07`

<a id="w14-amend-a11"></a>
### Unimplemented is not absent from the universe
`W14.AMEND.A11`

**Earlier ambiguity.** Flight, broader morphology and later championships repeatedly appeared only as runtime prerequisites.

**Current compendium interpretation.** Author their required anatomy, course, institutions and consequences in the compendium when useful. Mark their definitions as proposals and their software status separately. Do not wait for executable flight before defining the Flyer hobby.

**Purpose.** Keeps universe creation ahead of implementation without claiming that a described feature is playable.

**Earlier reference:** `W13.REFERENCE.04`, `W11.REWARD.02`

<a id="w14-amend-a12"></a>
### Old observations survive an editorial interpretation change
`W14.AMEND.A12`

**Earlier ambiguity.** Previous waves contain working anatomy and example coefficients beside implementation evidence.

**Current compendium interpretation.** Preserve old source files and records. Add named interpretation amendments and new reference entries. No saved genome, trial result, owner or paid transaction is recalculated by this compendium wave.

**Purpose.** Enables a coherent current reading without falsifying the project’s history.

**Earlier reference:** `W13.CONTINUITY.01`, `W12.CONTINUITY.04`

<a id="w14-open-decisions"></a>
### Decisions that need their own authored chapter
`W14.OPEN.DECISIONS`

**Biosphere and history.** The survival of humans and other donor insects alongside the roaches’ exceptional nuclear-winter survival needs a consistent account. Sealed vaults, refuges or later recovery must not become canon merely because an early assistant proposed them.

**Scale.** Choose a stable physical scale for hosts, sources, handling, housing and circuits. Relative working roles are defined here; exact dimensions are not invented.

**Loss and renewal.** Determine the experience of damage, ordinary recovery, aging, irreversible injury, donor reproduction and rare material renewal. Preserve meaningful consequences without silently imposing either universal invulnerability or mandatory loss of beloved racers.

**Heredity.** Expand each donor’s actual reproductive and variation model, including whether the proposed morphology families can appear, how they are inherited and what observations support a selection decision.

**Wider sport.** Name faction institutions, define their representation process, establish championship geography, and provide full aerial-course and sport-specific action references.

**Care and commerce.** Finish the product-specific handling, storage, exchange, shipping and restoration monographs. Price, elapsed time and probability calibration follow those meaningful distinctions rather than replace them.

**Earlier reference:** `D01`, `D02`, `D03`, `D04`, `D05`, `D06`, `D07`, `D08`, `D09`, `D10`, `D11`, `D12`
