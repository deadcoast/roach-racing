# RAD ROACH RACING

## WASTELANDER LANISTA — WAVE v0.17

### Ground Stock & the Receiving Bench · Donor Atlas, Volume II

**Edition:** 0.17.0 · **Date:** 11 September 2026 · **Phase:** world-bible and compendium architecture.

A source insect becomes valuable through its actual anatomy, inherited possibilities, preparation, useful fit and the person who needs it. A delivered component is not finished work until its current condition and intended use agree. This volume connects seven ground sources to twelve existing preparations and to the work that keeps those products circulating through the hobby.

**Authority and scope.** The explicit author premise governs. W15’s participation and progression revisions remain in force; W14 supplies the common anatomy and W16 the first donor/material volume. W17 refines only the named ground-source, preparation, receiving and repeat-work questions. New organisms, populations, inherited tendencies, material procedures, customer terms and prices are authored working fiction. Research supports the stated reference function or narrow biological observation—not real graft viability, balanced prices, universal heredity or measured player engagement. No game application is changed.

**The approachable route stays complete:** buy a ready kit, assemble it and race. A catcher may sell, keep or prepare a useful animal without breeding it. A breeder may supply ordinary components without campaigning a champion. The detailed supplier knowledge below exists for the world and interested specialists; it is not required reading before a first race.

### Ballast mole cricket

`W17.DONOR.MOLE` · **PCR gearing and foot-contact functions; physical-hobby joint/bearing extension**

**Earlier entries.** [W14.PART.LOWSTEP](reference/W16/reference/W15/reference/RRR_Wave_14_Foundations.md#w14-part-lowstep) · [W14.PART.CLEATS](reference/W16/reference/W15/reference/RRR_Wave_14_Foundations.md#w14-part-cleats) · [W14.PART.SLEEVES](reference/W16/reference/W15/reference/RRR_Wave_14_Foundations.md#w14-part-sleeves) · [W16.PREP.LOWSTEP](reference/W16/RRR_Wave_16_Material_Register.md#w16-prep-lowstep) · [W15.BROOD.01](reference/W16/reference/W15/RRR_Wave_15_Foundations.md#w15-brood-01)

**Source anchors and limits.** [W17.S02](RRR_Wave_17_Research.md#w17-s02) · [W17.S05](RRR_Wave_17_Research.md#w17-s05) · [W17.S06](RRR_Wave_17_Research.md#w17-s06)

**Recognition.** A compact burrowing body with broad digging forefeet, shorter working reaches and more mobile middle running legs. Root sleeves are small parts of actual articulations, not an extra organ drawn on the abdomen. Soil staining identifies present exposure, not a line or quality tier.

**Local ecology.** Ballast troughs retain pockets of root matter, detritus and small prey beneath the coarse surface. The fictional Ballast form uses those connected pockets for feeding and laying, crossing open grit to reach another refuge. It does not eat clean steel or obtain useful force from radiation. A hard-looking capture ground can hide a moist juvenile chamber; reproducing only its dry surface is a poor nursery plan.

#### Population and source knowledge

<a id="w17-donor-mole-pop-01"></a>

##### Trench-face stock

**Where.** The established Ballast trench and exposed sleeper margins.

**Why seek this population.** Useful toe geometry for coarse loose inserts; intact tips and bases are the immediate target.

**Observable clue.** Stable purchase while crossing a known loose patch and visible clearing of material from the toes.

**What the clue does not establish.** The insect may be responding to different grain size or moisture; habitat origin does not certify a friction number.

<a id="w17-donor-mole-pop-02"></a>

##### Root-mat stock

**Where.** The root pockets behind Ballast Banks, not a second species.

**Why seek this population.** Repeated hip travel and protected junctions; a useful counterpoint to forceful but grit-sensitive stock.

**Observable clue.** Smooth backwards withdrawal and turning in a known opening.

**What the clue does not establish.** Whole-animal retreat also uses sensing and several legs; it does not measure one isolated sleeve.

#### Life stages and decisions

| Stage | Role | Available evidence | Useful choice |
|---|---|---|---|
| <a id="w17-donor-mole-stage-01"></a>Egg chamber | A maternal reproductive event occupying a real substrate pocket. | Mother and chamber can be known; paternal contribution may remain uncertain. | Preserve a chamber only with capacity for its potential output, not its hoped-for low survival. |
| <a id="w17-donor-mole-stage-02"></a>Burrowing nymph | Growing limbs and interfaces are formed through successive molts. | Stage, current movement and usable refuge access. | Maintain loose working substrate and growth space; do not sell nymph legs as adult-ratio assemblies. |
| <a id="w17-donor-mole-stage-03"></a>Fresh adult | Working cuticle and junction behavior settle after the final molt. | Readiness under ordinary movement, not size alone. | Condition before comparing geometry under load. |
| <a id="w17-donor-mole-stage-04"></a>Working/reproductive adult | Immediate donor, founder, ordinary live sale or display stock. | Measured or observed working contributions and actual anatomical condition. | Choose the animal’s use before extracting a region needed by another product. |

#### Reproductive unit and inheritance

The authored Ballast profile is sexual and lays eggs in an identifiable soil chamber. A known unmated female and recorded male provide stronger parentage evidence than a previously mated wild female. Prior mating history is carried rather than overwritten when another companion is introduced. Relatedness, fertility and product quality are separate records. The profile does not adopt a universal egg count, maturation time or three-binary-locus genome. Segregated founder chambers can preserve maternity without requiring a casual racer to manage individual eggs.

#### Four selectable contributions

<a id="w17-trait-mole-lever"></a>

##### Working leverage range

**Inherited contribution.** Several proportion and joint-position contributions alter the force/travel relationship. A useful range is selected rather than maximum length or ratio.

**Field screen.** Observe loaded starts and the actual short stroke; record body size and burden.

**Controlled observation.** Measure input/output travel through the prepared linkage across its usable range with resistance reported.

**Confounder to exclude.** A stronger muscle can hide a poor ratio; a jammed joint can appear forceful because it barely moves.

**Racing or livelihood use.** Lowstep supply for loaded starts, repeated acceleration and differently sized hosts.

<a id="w17-trait-mole-toe"></a>

##### Tooth tip and supporting base

**Inherited contribution.** Contact tip geometry, base toughness and attachment interact; neither a sharp tip nor a hard surface alone certifies a complete cleat.

**Field screen.** Inspect intact tips and how the base behaves in coarse material.

**Controlled observation.** Compare penetration/purchase, release and retained geometry on the stated substrate.

**Confounder to exclude.** Testing fine ash and coarse ballast as if identical can reverse an apparent ranking.

**Racing or livelihood use.** Cleats for ash-course makers, replacement pairs and operating-specific contact sets.

<a id="w17-trait-mole-sleeve"></a>

##### Low-loss articulated lining

**Inherited contribution.** Lining structure and permitted travel can contribute to repeatable low resistance without binding.

**Field screen.** Observe repeated travel after ordinary grit exposure.

**Controlled observation.** Compare motion resistance and available travel before/after the named fouling challenge.

**Confounder to exclude.** Cleaning restores condition; it is not inherited improvement. A tight seal can obstruct useful movement.

**Racing or livelihood use.** Joint-sleeve specialists and maintenance services for dirty-course racers.

<a id="w17-trait-mole-fit"></a>

##### Consistent working dimensions

**Inherited contribution.** Family variation in root spacing and segment proportions can improve yield within a declared kit envelope.

**Field screen.** Compare mature animals with a nonloading outline gauge.

**Controlled observation.** Record root spacing and full working travel; keep successful fitting separate from the source dimensions.

**Confounder to exclude.** Underdeveloped individuals or a heavily reshaped preparation are not evidence of a naturally consistent line.

**Racing or livelihood use.** Dependable ordinary Lowstep stock for several printed sizes, rather than a one-size universal part.

#### Nursery and productive stages

Use a deep-substrate module with separable laying refuges, grow-out pockets and adult handling space. A transparent edge or removable observation sleeve should permit checking occupancy without repeatedly collapsing chambers. Adult holding is not evidence of a complete reproductive process. Research retains identified families; production targets a measured preparation envelope; maintenance stops new laying commitments while continuing care.

#### Capture and return

Choose a receiving sleeve or supported container that protects the loaded forefeet and allows the animal to withdraw rather than wrenching it from a crevice. The ordinary route can secure common adults; a targeted route favors intact hip and contact anatomy. A successful catch with a broken toe can retain other physical or reproductive uses, assessed individually.

#### Anatomical allocation

Lowstep is the specified hip-lever package and its required junctions. Cleats come from identified contact ends. Standalone Ballast Sleeves are only additional products from other unallocated linings; the sleeves already retained in a complete Lowstep cannot also be sold loose. A detached source remains absent from the donor. Supported remaining bodies require care and assessed reproductive capability, not an assumption of guaranteed fertility.

#### A purposeful selection project

A maker has an appropriate Lowstep ratio but excessive loss after gritty heats. Seek repeatable low-resistance linings in a comparable geometry, not still more mechanical advantage. Retain a smooth-jointed ordinary-force parent, compare descendants under matched contamination, supply ordinary cleats from other suitable offspring, and keep the replacement reserve bounded. A ready measured assembly remains the buying alternative.

#### Construction opportunities

**Enabling situation.** A loaded racer repeatedly restarts on loose ground: appropriate leverage and well-releasing cleats can deliver useful drive.

**Non-enabling situation.** An unbroken smooth straight does not award a gearing bonus simply because Lowstep is installed.

**Another legitimate response.** A different drive, reduced unnecessary load, different toe preparation or revised stance may solve the same problem; no exclusive Ballast equipment lock.

#### Ordinary stock, customers and services

Two Nets buys intact ordinary adults for general preparation, while a fitting customer may want only a specific sleeve or replacement contact pair. A maker of Foundry Heavy values a repeated geometry range. Whole breeders with useful lining contribution remain a separate offer from extracted assemblies. The trade card names the supplied regions; an apparent three-product bargain must not count one junction twice.

#### Appearance and material identity

Retain scoop-like forefeet, modest running midlegs and visible joint folds. New outline variant: broad-heel toes spread their contact over a different shape; this is a preparation or documented developmental form, not automatically a rare genome. Sleeve cases display a motion gap so they are not mistaken for a rigid clamp.

#### Unresolved numerical or broader policy questions

Numerical leverage ranges, heredity of lining structure, adult longevity, reproductive consequences after specific extractions and material renewal rates remain separately authored/calibrated. No new universal burial, regeneration or per-swap injury rule.

### Trackrunner tiger beetle

`W17.DONOR.TIGER` · **PCR gearing/delivery and physical-hobby limb geometry; not a second motor**

**Earlier entries.** [W14.PART.LONGTHROW](reference/W16/reference/W15/reference/RRR_Wave_14_Foundations.md#w14-part-longthrow) · [W16.PREP.LONGTHROW](reference/W16/RRR_Wave_16_Material_Register.md#w16-prep-longthrow) · [W15.GRADES.05](reference/W16/reference/W15/RRR_Wave_15_Foundations.md#w15-grades-05)

**Source anchors and limits.** [W17.S02](RRR_Wave_17_Research.md#w17-s02) · [W17.S07](RRR_Wave_17_Research.md#w17-s07) · [W17.S08](RRR_Wave_17_Research.md#w17-s08)

**Recognition.** A compact head with prominent eyes, open running stance, long articulated legs and a light-looking distal silhouette. Forward antennae remain native sensory anatomy. A prepared Longthrow contains measured levers and limbs; it is not the donor’s entire pursuit behavior enclosed in a packet.

**Local ecology.** Trackrunners hunt small arthropods along sunlit hardpan and retreat to adjacent shade or broken edges. Juveniles occupy predatory burrows in suitable soil rather than sprinting around like miniature adults. An exposed paved apron can therefore be a good adult collecting surface and a useless complete nursery unless its adjacent juvenile substrate is also provided.

#### Population and source knowledge

<a id="w17-donor-tiger-pop-01"></a>

##### Sun-slab runners

**Where.** Open slabs between the existing Switchyard sleepers.

**Why seek this population.** Long useful adult reach with low distal burden for clear-straight builds.

**Observable clue.** Several unobstructed strides between stops, preserving apparent body size and surface conditions.

**What the clue does not establish.** A single chase burst depends on attention, prey and current condition; it is not a limb-only speed assay.

<a id="w17-donor-tiger-pop-02"></a>

##### Crack-edge runners

**Where.** Broken apron margins beside shaded refuge strips.

**Why seek this population.** Working articulation and compact recovery of long limbs in narrower spaces.

**Observable clue.** Turn and return paths around a known edge without snagging the feet.

**What the clue does not establish.** Better whole-body obstacle negotiation may depend on antennae and posture, not a superior Longthrow.

#### Life stages and decisions

| Stage | Role | Available evidence | Useful choice |
|---|---|---|---|
| <a id="w17-donor-tiger-stage-01"></a>Individually placed eggs | Each egg belongs to an actual mother and source substrate. | Laying positions and maternal record where observed. | Record a managed laying patch without pretending every visible hole contains a viable egg. |
| <a id="w17-donor-tiger-stage-02"></a>Predatory burrow larva | A relatively stationary feeder requiring a usable entrance and space for growth. | Burrow occupancy and observed feeding, not hidden future adult ratios. | Keep supported burrow units rather than stripping larvae into an adult sprint enclosure. |
| <a id="w17-donor-tiger-stage-03"></a>Pupal chamber | Transformation into the adult locomotor form. | Chamber condition and stage where observation is appropriate. | Avoid treating a sealed chamber as dead stock solely because it does not move. |
| <a id="w17-donor-tiger-stage-04"></a>Conditioning/working adult | Mature running geometry becomes available for controlled comparison or reproduction. | Extended limb shape, articulation and current surface readiness. | Choose a direct limb donor, a founder or an ordinary live sale. |

#### Reproductive unit and inheritance

The fictional Trackrunner retains sexual reproduction and individual laying in a suitable juvenile substrate. A known mother can produce a recordable family even when prior mating leaves paternity uncertain. Adult founders and their burrow-grown young have different facility needs. Both sexes may supply useful limbs under this profile; a sex label does not itself set reach or grade. No larval limb is presumed to contain the mature adult Longthrow geometry, and no real tiger-beetle lifespan or protected-species geography is copied into the wasteland.

#### Four selectable contributions

<a id="w17-trait-tiger-reach"></a>

##### Useful stroke reach

**Inherited contribution.** Several limb proportions and joint positions determine travel; longer is useful within an operating envelope, not universally superior.

**Field screen.** Observe a full stride and return on a known unobstructed surface.

**Controlled observation.** Compare useful output travel with input work, stance and range declared.

**Confounder to exclude.** A different drive, body posture or frightened burst can make the same limb seem longer-performing.

**Racing or livelihood use.** Longthrow variants for sprint and open-passing constructions.

<a id="w17-trait-tiger-inertia"></a>

##### Distal mass distribution

**Inherited contribution.** Material placement along the moving limb matters in addition to total assembly mass.

**Field screen.** Compare similarly sized mature limbs visually, without inferring a precise inertia value.

**Controlled observation.** Record assembly mass and its distribution along the working motion; verify repeated cycling under the intended drive.

**Confounder to exclude.** A long hollow-looking silhouette can conceal dense distal structures.

**Racing or livelihood use.** High-cadence constructions seeking real reduction of moving burden.

<a id="w17-trait-tiger-articulation"></a>

##### Fold and return clearance

**Inherited contribution.** Joint travel and segment shape determine whether long anatomy can recover without striking the host or neighbors.

**Field screen.** Watch the folded return as well as the forward extension.

**Controlled observation.** Check the complete movement envelope on a declared host outline.

**Confounder to exclude.** A fitter’s altered root angle can fix a collision without changing heredity.

**Racing or livelihood use.** Compact Longthrow editions and custom fitting jobs.

<a id="w17-trait-tiger-surface"></a>

##### Distal contact compatibility

**Inherited contribution.** Tip-support geometry determines which separately sourced contact preparation can be attached usefully.

**Field screen.** Inspect actual tips and support rather than assuming every long leg accepts every pad.

**Controlled observation.** Check root area, orientation and loaded tip articulation with the proposed contact preparation.

**Confounder to exclude.** An included native tip must not be counted again as a separately purchased full contact set.

**Racing or livelihood use.** Mixed-source sprint kits and replacement-limb suppliers.

#### Nursery and productive stages

A burrow-liner rack provides distinct juvenile feeding entrances and pupation room; an adult run case provides movement, shelter and controlled reproductive handling. These can be adjacent modules, not a demand for a giant private facility. The breeder can rent juvenile grow-out while keeping selected adults. Burrow liners carry actual occupants when moved; copying a labelled sleeve does not copy the animal.

#### Capture and return

Approach from a position that preserves the running limbs and does not pin the animal against the case wall. A broad receiving space can be safer for long extremities than a tight cup chosen only by body mass. Disturbance, obstacle familiarity and sensory access belong in the field note. The roach receiving its legs does not inherit an obligatory stop-and-look routine.

#### Anatomical allocation

The complete Longthrow identifies hip/limb segments, included interfaces and any supplied native contact ends. Independent pads require a separate source or an explicitly unallocated tip region. The same length is not counted as both a full Longthrow and a second full spar upgrade. A complete package cannot simultaneously leave a complete same-side running limb on the donor.

#### A purposeful selection project

A builder’s fast drive cannot use a heavy long limb comfortably. Select useful reach with less distal mass, preserving adequate support. The missing-contribution parent may be shorter but light and well articulated. Suitable intermediate stock serves smaller-host Longthrow editions; it is not automatically scrap because it lacks the project’s greatest span.

#### Construction opportunities

**Enabling situation.** A clear sprint with enough drive and a usable return path converts the selected stroke travel into pace.

**Non-enabling situation.** A narrow crowded turn that obstructs the return path can defeat a very long assembly.

**Another legitimate response.** Different ratio, stronger drive, a shorter low-inertia preparation or a wider legal line can help. Speed is not repaired by adding an invisible limb-tier multiplier.

#### Ordinary stock, customers and services

Sprint makers buy operating sizes, not a globally ranked longest leg. Fitting specialists can sell adaptation and mixed-source contact work. Whole adults and occupied juvenile burrow units are different products with different receiving requirements. A collector may value metallic appearance and clean proportions without asserting ancestry or performance.

#### Appearance and material identity

Show long articulated limbs in both working and folded silhouettes. Reference plates must include the return path; a posed extended leg is not a complete design. Directional light can change natural apparent color, which the field card should not mislabel as two different genetic morphs.

#### Unresolved numerical or broader policy questions

Exact relationship of reach, strength and moving mass, reproductive timing, visual-pattern inheritance and native sensor behavior require later quantitative profiles. The sensory research does not authorize automatic temporary blindness on a grafted roach.

### Saltglass mantis

`W17.DONOR.MANTIS` · **RRR-native grasping offense; alternatively prepared physical support under the stabilizer function**

**Earlier entries.** [W14.PART.CATCHCLAWS](reference/W16/reference/W15/reference/RRR_Wave_14_Foundations.md#w14-part-catchclaws) · [W14.PART.COUNTERBRACES](reference/W16/reference/W15/reference/RRR_Wave_14_Foundations.md#w14-part-counterbraces) · [W14.ACTION.GRASP](reference/W16/reference/W15/reference/RRR_Wave_14_Foundations.md#w14-action-grasp) · [W16.PREP.COUNTERBRACES](reference/W16/RRR_Wave_16_Material_Register.md#w16-prep-counterbraces) · [W15.RACING.05](reference/W16/reference/W15/RRR_Wave_15_Foundations.md#w15-racing-05)

**Source anchors and limits.** [W17.S09](RRR_Wave_17_Research.md#w17-s09)

**Recognition.** An elongated forebody and visibly folded raptorial arms with opposing working surfaces. A moving target, an extended arm, a closed grasp and a planted support posture are separate visual states. The head’s attention is not painted onto the detached limbs as a second personality.

**Local ecology.** Perches among scrub, vine supports and broken window frames provide prey-facing positions and sheltered laying surfaces. Prey comes from the living local insect community, not an endless free feeding prop. A dense prey patch may attract several mantises but does not guarantee they can share an enclosure without conflict.

#### Population and source knowledge

<a id="w17-donor-mantis-pop-01"></a>

##### Hanging-frame stalkers

**Where.** Existing Hanging frames and upright Saltglass perches.

**Why seek this population.** Useful reach, folding clearance and controlled extension from elevated supports.

**Observable clue.** Watch the full extension and recovery relative to target distance.

**What the clue does not establish.** A slow observed attempt may be deliberate timing rather than slow contractile tissue.

<a id="w17-donor-mantis-pop-02"></a>

##### Lintel-gap stalkers

**Where.** Sheltered horizontal frame joints near the same scrub system.

**Why seek this population.** Responsive closure inside shorter moving encounters and compact folded geometry.

**Observable clue.** Note close-range grasp and clean release without forcing repeated attacks.

**What the clue does not establish.** Hunger, prey motion and previous disturbance change the attempt; the locality does not supply a fixed reactive stat.

#### Life stages and decisions

| Stage | Role | Available evidence | Useful choice |
|---|---|---|---|
| <a id="w17-donor-mantis-stage-01"></a>Ootheca / egg case | A real reproductive structure with maternal history and uncertain viable contents. | Case condition, known mother and any actual mating record. | Reserve plausible young-stage support; do not split an illustration into arbitrary guaranteed egg packets. |
| <a id="w17-donor-mantis-stage-02"></a>Young nymphs | Growing predators that need suitable food, space and separation. | Observed hatchlings and completed molts. | Partition actual young and record losses honestly rather than inferring adult pair count from the case. |
| <a id="w17-donor-mantis-stage-03"></a>Late nymph and molt | Adult arm dimensions and surfaces are still developing. | Known stage and room for unimpeded molt. | A cramped or torn molt is a developmental problem, not proof of poor inherited reach. |
| <a id="w17-donor-mantis-stage-04"></a>Adult | Useful arms, supported reproductive contact or a display individual. | Working condition, measured arm response and known history. | Choose breeder or preparation only after considering the needed contribution. |

#### Reproductive unit and inheritance

The working Saltglass profile is sexual and deposits egg cases. A female’s reproductive history can predate capture; a newly introduced male is not automatically credited with existing stored contributions. Managed introductions and predator separation reduce relevant risk without guaranteeing mating. Cannibalism is a situational interaction with actual animals, not a mandatory animation that consumes a male each time breeding begins. Founders may be valuable across multiple attempts while fit and eligible. Failure of a case, poor young survival and failure to express a target are distinct outcomes.

#### Four selectable contributions

<a id="w17-trait-mantis-reach"></a>

##### Reach and grip envelope

**Inherited contribution.** Segment proportions and opposing surface geometry alter which shapes can be grasped.

**Field screen.** Record target distance and the opened arm silhouette.

**Controlled observation.** Compare working reach and closure envelope against stated test forms.

**Confounder to exclude.** Long span without useful opposing contact may catch nothing; staged posture can inflate apparent reach.

**Racing or livelihood use.** Catchclaw interception projects and size-specific grip preparations.

<a id="w17-trait-mantis-close"></a>

##### Working closure response

**Inherited contribution.** Contractile properties, joint resistance and linkage geometry contribute to response once activated.

**Field screen.** Observe several comparable attempts with target motion noted.

**Controlled observation.** Separate response to the command, extension, closure and release under named load.

**Confounder to exclude.** Target-speed-dependent decision timing is not a fixed inherited arm delay.

**Racing or livelihood use.** Fast-closing stock for brief contact windows, without granting a ranged stun.

<a id="w17-trait-mantis-release"></a>

##### Controlled hold and release

**Inherited contribution.** Working surface geometry and return anatomy support gripping and disengagement.

**Field screen.** Observe separation after contact, not only the successful catch.

**Controlled observation.** Measure release under permitted loads and inspect folded clearance.

**Confounder to exclude.** A stuck or damaged joint may look like a powerful hold but be a poor racer.

**Racing or livelihood use.** Selective contact builds that resume running instead of holding both racers indefinitely.

<a id="w17-trait-mantis-support"></a>

##### Load-bearing extension

**Earlier entries.** [W14.PART.SPLITVANES](reference/W16/reference/W15/reference/RRR_Wave_14_Foundations.md#w14-part-splitvanes) · [W14.MORPH.LOUVER](reference/W16/reference/W15/reference/RRR_Wave_14_Foundations.md#w14-morph-louver) · [W16.PREP.SPLITVANES](reference/W16/RRR_Wave_16_Material_Register.md#w16-prep-splitvanes) · [W15.IDENTITY.01](reference/W16/reference/W15/RRR_Wave_15_Foundations.md#w15-identity-01)

**Source anchors and limits.** [W17.S02](RRR_Wave_17_Research.md#w17-s02) · [W17.S16](RRR_Wave_17_Research.md#w17-s16)

**Recognition.** A veined, patterned moth whose paired surfaces remain identifiable after preparation. The membrane carries the surface, veins support its shape, scales contribute their actual surface or visual features, and a prepared root supports the vane. Those are different objects, not one quantity called wing quality.

**Local ecology.** Larvae feed on defined vine growth and associated organic surfaces in Textile Ruins; the ruin offers shelter and host plants rather than unlimited edible fabric. Adults in this working form feed on accessible plant fluids. Window edges expose moving air while deeper awnings offer shelter. The same district can support patterned display stock and useful working surfaces without natural color conferring downforce.

#### Population and source knowledge

<a id="w17-donor-ashveil-pop-01"></a>

##### Windowvine population

**Where.** Light-exposed vines beside the existing Textile Ruins and Felt loft.

**Why seek this population.** Useful veined support under a stated air load and intact outer margins.

**Observable clue.** Observe resting shape and ordinary surface movement in a known breeze.

**What the clue does not establish.** Wind strength, posture and current moisture alter the observed bend; the location does not award a stiffness gene.

<a id="w17-donor-ashveil-pop-02"></a>

##### Awning-shade population

**Where.** Sheltered growth behind the same ruined frames.

**Why seek this population.** Intact membrane, repeatable paired form and distinctive natural markings.

**Observable clue.** Compare conditioned adults under stable light and inspect actual edges.

**What the clue does not establish.** A more pristine surface may reflect shelter rather than a heritable working improvement.

#### Reproductive unit and inheritance

The authored Ashveil cycle is sexual with complete metamorphosis. Several inherited contributions may influence vein arrangement, surface form and pattern, while nutrition, emergence space and handling affect expression. Applied paint, a repaired membrane and a changed mounting angle do not enter the germline. A pictured natural band can be a useful collection objective, but its inheritance requires observed family evidence. Prior mating is recorded as known or unknown. No exact Mendelian pattern, mutation rate or fixed color price is declared by this volume.

#### Four selectable contributions

<a id="w17-trait-ashveil-shape"></a>

##### Loaded surface shape

**Inherited contribution.** Vein layout and membrane properties influence the working shape under the intended air load.

**Field screen.** Observe comparable conditioned surfaces under a known gentle flow.

**Controlled observation.** Assess loaded deflection and shape at the stated mounting and motion range.

**Confounder to exclude.** An unloaded flat outline cannot certify the shape the vane takes while racing.

**Racing or livelihood use.** Passive front-aero preparations with predictable behavior.

<a id="w17-trait-ashveil-burden"></a>

##### Surface burden and resistance

**Inherited contribution.** Area, material distribution and form contribute actual mass and aerodynamic resistance.

**Field screen.** Compare similar mature surfaces without guessing an exact drag value.

**Controlled observation.** Record mass and a supported operating comparison with angle and load stated.

**Confounder to exclude.** A larger surface may help or hinder; a body-mounted angle can change the outcome without any inherited change.

**Racing or livelihood use.** Light useful aero and alternative operating sizes, not maximum-span trophies.

<a id="w17-trait-ashveil-margin"></a>

##### Margin and flex boundary integrity

**Inherited contribution.** Structural tendencies can support useful flex; developmental defects and current tears must be separated.

**Field screen.** Inspect veins, margins and paired shape without overhandling.

**Controlled observation.** Map the permitted flex line and check that support remains continuous.

**Confounder to exclude.** A tear is not automatically a valuable split-louver morph. A hinged pattern still needs actuation if commanded movement is promised.

**Racing or livelihood use.** Reliable Split-Vanes and separately specified experimental louver preparations.

<a id="w17-trait-ashveil-pattern"></a>

##### Natural visual pattern

**Inherited contribution.** Proposed multicomponent coloration and scale pattern with stage/condition-dependent expression.

**Field screen.** Compare stable lighting, actual sides and current wear.

**Controlled observation.** Document appearance separately from working-load measurements and applied finishing.

**Confounder to exclude.** Lighting, rubbed scales and paint cannot be labelled a newly inherited rare variant.

**Racing or livelihood use.** Living collections, signature editions and finish commissions with no hidden speed effect.

#### Nursery and productive stages

Provide the larval resource, protected pupation, unimpeded emergence and separate adult holding. An expansion cabinet supports intact output; it does not improve genes. Research can retain patterned founders while ordinary offspring supply measured aero. Complete adult stock can be purchased; a ground racer does not need to build a moth nursery to change a brow surface.

#### Capture and return

Protect membrane and roots with actual clearance; do not pack adults or prepared vanes under a compressing lid. Capture method and later packing are separate risks. A surface can retain an appealing pattern after losing a working vein, or keep useful load behavior despite a cosmetic rubbed patch; classify the actual defect rather than applying one all-or-nothing wing score.

#### Anatomical allocation

Split-Vanes allocate named membrane/vein areas with their supporting interface. A pair preserves both source records even if formed from two compatible individuals. A commanded opening louver adds declared hinge, actuator, return and control rather than receiving a free ability because a crack resembles a split. Passive aero is not sustained flight. A preparation consumes its actual source surfaces; a painted replica or repaired patch is recorded separately.

#### A purposeful selection project

A maker wants the same recognizable pale banding while improving supported shape at ordinary mass. Keep appearance and performance observations separate. An ordinary aero donor with the desired pattern may serve a display edition or contribute to a future family; a plain but excellent vane can serve the racing edition. Neither is universally the better animal.

#### Construction opportunities

**Enabling situation.** Sufficient motion creates air load on a correctly exposed supported surface, changing attitude or contact load as intended.

**Non-enabling situation.** At rest or with the vane shielded, the intended aerodynamic effect is absent. No wing-shaped surface grants powered flight.

**Another legitimate response.** Different angle, surface area, mass distribution, contact or driving setup can address the race. A smaller intact preparation may outperform a damaged large one within its own promise.

#### Ordinary stock, customers and services

Two Nets supplies ready working vanes; the Saltglass Counter can commission living natural forms and documented restorations. Makers print whether an edition is performance-led, appearance-led or both. A working repair is judged against the order’s stated behavior; original-material provenance remains a separate claim even when performance is restored.

#### Appearance and material identity

Each illustration distinguishes membrane, major veins, patterned scale field and actual mounting root. A repair seam stays legible in detailed records. The split-louver study should be drawn in open and closed states only when a physical mechanism has been defined; decorative notches alone do not depict an active system.

#### Unresolved numerical or broader policy questions

Actual airflow usefulness depends on the future scale and motion model. Genetic pattern distributions, repair limits, general wing lifespan, and the complete Longwing/Quadwing aerial atlas remain separate authoring. Acoustic scale research is not an aerodynamic coefficient.

## 4. Common operating definitions

<a id="w17-rulegroup-bio"></a>

### Source diversity and useful evidence

<a id="w17-bio-01"></a>

**W17.BIO.01 — Whole behavior is not isolated tissue.** A field movement is recorded with its body, sensory situation, load and condition. A prepared limb does not inherit the donor’s controller or entire gait.

**Purpose.** Keep observation informative without creating an exact genetic or component oracle.

<a id="w17-bio-02"></a>

**W17.BIO.02 — Stage-specific anatomy.** A juvenile product, adult reproductive role and detached preparation are different assets and timelines. Every proposed donor defines which actually exists.

**Purpose.** Longthrow cannot come from a larval burrow merely by naming the larva a fast beetle.

<a id="w17-bio-03"></a>

**W17.BIO.03 — Complement versus finished product.** Founder selection evaluates a missing contribution separately from the complete customer specification.

**Purpose.** Useful intermediate animals have purposeful breeding and product outlets.

<a id="w17-bio-04"></a>

**W17.BIO.04 — Parentage scope.** Recorded maternity, current companion, observed mating and verified contributing parent remain separate evidence.

**Purpose.** Known uncertainty is not erased by moving stock between tanks.

<a id="w17-bio-05"></a>

**W17.BIO.05 — Sex-associated expression.** Where a working profile has sex-associated morphology, both parents’ possible contribution is evaluated without requiring both to display the full product.

**Purpose.** A Bullhorn female can be relevant to a horn line without being a hidden finished ram.

<a id="w17-bio-06"></a>

**W17.BIO.06 — Development and maintenance.** Conditioning, feeding history, cleaning, rooting and alignment can change current utility but do not edit inheritance.

**Purpose.** Better husbandry and fitting can have value without becoming gene-upgrade actions.

<a id="w17-bio-07"></a>

**W17.BIO.07 — Population structure.** Local populations have stated resources, stages and observable tendencies; no patch is guaranteed to issue the trait named in the player’s current target.

**Purpose.** Known habitats remain useful without converting ecology into a tailored loot dispenser.

<a id="w17-bio-08"></a>

**W17.BIO.08 — One source, several visits.** Quick and detailed collecting use a declared common or distinct source relationship. Visits cannot create duplicate copies of the same cohort.

**Purpose.** Carry the Wave15 source-identity correction into new monographs.

<a id="w17-bio-09"></a>

**W17.BIO.09 — Predator management.** Predatory interaction follows actual animals and conditions; a breeding command neither guarantees cannibalism nor immunity from it.

**Purpose.** Keep risk consequential without an automatic parent-sacrifice tax.

<a id="w17-bio-10"></a>

**W17.BIO.10 — Ordinary completion.** A known prepared part, whole suitable donor or paid grow-out service is a legitimate route to each covered construction.

**Purpose.** A monograph defines the supplier’s complete work without forcing every racer to perform it.

<a id="w17-rulegroup-assembly"></a>

### Physical preparation and the correct control source

<a id="w17-assembly-01"></a>

**W17.ASSEMBLY.01 — Role is not anatomy.** One component can serve several functions, but its source mass and regions are counted once.

**Purpose.** A compound linkage is not three freely duplicated items.

<a id="w17-assembly-02"></a>

**W17.ASSEMBLY.02 — Included interfaces.** Every complete preparation names which sleeve, lining, actuator, root and support it includes.

**Purpose.** A subcomponent is separately saleable only when it is physically unallocated.

<a id="w17-assembly-03"></a>

**W17.ASSEMBLY.03 — Powered Counterbraces.** Counterbraces declare passive contact, host-driven deployment or included active tissue. Powered versions inherit the active-root material requirements in addition to structural and elastic needs.

**Purpose.** Removing attacking grasp does not explain or supply deployment work.

<a id="w17-assembly-04"></a>

**W17.ASSEMBLY.04 — Same donor is not a calibration.** Paired source identity and paired working agreement are separate observations.

**Purpose.** One injury or imperfect preparation can make a same-donor pair uneven.

<a id="w17-assembly-05"></a>

**W17.ASSEMBLY.05 — Mixed-source pairs.** Different-source components may be combined where actual fit and working tests support the use; provenance records all original sources and the new matching operation.

**Purpose.** Rematching creates a legitimate service without false common ancestry.

<a id="w17-assembly-06"></a>

**W17.ASSEMBLY.06 — Control remains on the host.** Grasp initiation, sensing and recovery need a host or an explicitly supplied local interface. Imported anatomy does not carry a full donor personality.

**Purpose.** Strike timing and sensory behavior cannot be represented by one mandatory donor reflex.

<a id="w17-assembly-07"></a>

**W17.ASSEMBLY.07 — Converting capability.** A re-preparation changes real geometry/control/capability. A shipping lock or peaceful intent cannot relabel a functional attack as a permanent non-attacking support.

**Purpose.** Sporting classes inspect the built capability.

<a id="w17-assembly-08"></a>

**W17.ASSEMBLY.08 — Direction and backing.** Surface, backing and root directions remain attached to the specification. Reversing or reshaping them requires a relevant new check.

**Purpose.** Material quality is not independent of how the material is actually loaded.

<a id="w17-assembly-09"></a>

**W17.ASSEMBLY.09 — Restoration versus duplication.** Replacing an interface uses another real source. Retaining an original core does not recreate any region already consumed.

**Purpose.** Repair supports collection while preserving material accounting.

<a id="w17-assembly-10"></a>

**W17.ASSEMBLY.10 — Actual performance.** Better material can be genuinely better at equal burden. Qualities unsupported by observations, appearance, name or acquisition route do not produce hidden race bonuses.

**Purpose.** Preserve desirable upgrades without compulsory arbitrary penalties.

<a id="w17-rulegroup-transfer"></a>

### Dispatch, arrival and supported recovery

<a id="w17-transfer-01"></a>

**W17.TRANSFER.01 — Quote the received object.** An order specifies the arriving identity or admissible source, stage, complete contents, use, evidence and ordinary first-use allowance.

**Purpose.** Dispatching something alive does not prove delivery of the promised usable product.

<a id="w17-transfer-02"></a>

**W17.TRANSFER.02 — Current truth plus claims.** Retain dispatch observations, transit conditions and arrival observations separately. A former certificate cannot overwrite present damage, and damage cannot rewrite genes.

**Purpose.** Old evidence stays useful without licensing false current readiness.

<a id="w17-transfer-03"></a>

**W17.TRANSFER.03 — Three clocks.** Whole-donor development, post-preparation condition and appointments/care are separate clocks. Only actual supported preparation changes which clock applies to that material.

**Purpose.** A late larva cannot be made current by resetting a shipping timer.

<a id="w17-transfer-04"></a>

**W17.TRANSFER.04 — Reserve receiving support.** Before dispatch, sender, carrier and receiver verify space, needed support and the actual handover window.

**Purpose.** A perfectly carried insect still needs somewhere appropriate to arrive.

<a id="w17-transfer-05"></a>

**W17.TRANSFER.05 — Mixed cargo.** Each item’s needs remain active in a shared case; incompatible physical supports receive separate compartments or shipments.

**Purpose.** A hard horn must not crush a vane, and a predator is not harmless because both items share an order.

<a id="w17-transfer-06"></a>

**W17.TRANSFER.06 — Condition-first acceptance.** Accepted ready, accepted with stated included service, held for a decision, or rejected-for-this-use are distinct dispositions.

**Purpose.** Unready does not mean permanently worthless; accepted-with-service is not an undisclosed downgrade.

<a id="w17-transfer-07"></a>

**W17.TRANSFER.07 — Consent for irreversible remedies.** New preparation, material replacement, conversion, changed price or alternate product requires the relevant owner’s authority.

**Purpose.** A delayed brood or torn wing cannot be converted into a different sale without consent.

<a id="w17-transfer-08"></a>

**W17.TRANSFER.08 — Custody survives a dispute.** The manifest names who has possession, who owns the animal and who provides immediate care while a decision is pending.

**Purpose.** A refused delivery does not delete living stock or abandon care.

<a id="w17-transfer-09"></a>

**W17.TRANSFER.09 — No automatic blame.** A failed arrival condition triggers evidence review and the preagreed remedy, not an automatic random penalty on the carrier or buyer.

**Purpose.** Source defects, packing failure, transit loss and receiver delay are different causes.

<a id="w17-transfer-10"></a>

**W17.TRANSFER.10 — Service preserves meaning.** Included alignment, inspection and ordinary care restore or confirm the declared preparation; specialist repair and changed material are separately priced.

**Purpose.** A casual kit buyer receives a complete first-use path, not an urgent surprise laboratory bill.

<a id="w17-transfer-11"></a>

**W17.TRANSFER.11 — Payment follows terms.** Order reservation is not revenue. Payment, any agreed deposit liability, accepted share, refund and additional authorized service remain separately recorded.

**Purpose.** A shipment notice cannot pay a completed kit twice.

<a id="w17-transfer-12"></a>

**W17.TRANSFER.12 — Safe stopping.** Offline default pause applies to all world clocks. During in-world travel the declared care and stages progress together; no selectively frozen life avoids an obligation.

**Purpose.** Use timing for in-world planning without real-world attendance pressure.

<a id="w17-rulegroup-trade"></a>

### Useful output and continued operations

<a id="w17-trade-01"></a>

**W17.TRADE.01 — Acceptance is purposeful.** Customers request useful performance, anatomy, appearance or reproductive evidence, not a grade with no intended use.

**Purpose.** Every part and stock outlet has a reason to exist.

<a id="w17-trade-02"></a>

**W17.TRADE.02 — Failed target is local.** A component that fails one brief may fit another narrower or different brief, but an actual known buyer and usable anatomy are required.

**Purpose.** No guaranteed salvage conversion slot and no one-failure universal zero value.

<a id="w17-trade-03"></a>

**W17.TRADE.03 — Document feedback.** A receiving report identifies supply failure, transport condition, fit or later racing circumstances separately.

**Purpose.** A customer losing a race does not by itself prove the promised part was defective.

<a id="w17-trade-04"></a>

**W17.TRADE.04 — Continue actual relationships.** Repeat orders depend on new need, usable delivered output and a funded request; trust may simplify repeated checks without making bad anatomy acceptable.

**Purpose.** Reputation acts through relationships rather than a price aura.

<a id="w17-trade-05"></a>

**W17.TRADE.05 — Period continuity.** Cash, occupied stock, unsold kits, retained breeders and obligations carry between budget periods unchanged except for listed real transactions and development.

**Purpose.** An attractive first-period margin cannot conceal the next period’s housing bill.

<a id="w17-trade-06"></a>

**W17.TRADE.06 — Pay labor once.** Operator remuneration is an explicit workshop-to-household payment; the same household expenditure is not charged again as workshop upkeep.

**Purpose.** Earning a keep must include compensation, not call unpaid work profit.

<a id="w17-trade-07"></a>

**W17.TRADE.07 — Capital is not income.** New equipment, initial breeders, replacement reserves and retained inventory are assets with stated acquisition or supplied status.

**Purpose.** A new generation does not immediately repay the research account.

<a id="w17-trade-08"></a>

**W17.TRADE.08 — Stop starts deliberately.** A full replacement reserve or unfunded surplus can lead to maintenance, another real outlet or a revised project. Existing lives and care persist.

**Purpose.** Scaling is controlled production, not endlessly adding protected offspring.

### Ready-to-bench counter handover

**Purpose.** A casual buyer obtains a current prepared component, ordinary supported case and a normal fitting/first-check allowance.

**Work.** Inspect the quoted item → confirm receiving host/bench support → transfer → use included alignment/check → either accept the fit or identify a specific mismatch.

**Responsibility.** The shop owns current stock until the agreed handover, describes included contents and disclosed condition, and provides quoted initial support. The buyer supplies the declared host or books one of the existing supported benches.

**Limits.** No universal free remounting forever and no warranty of winning. This is ordinary first-use access, not a mandatory service purchase on every hobby action.

### Booked shared carrier case

**Purpose.** A regional hobbyist receives material through Case Runners with a known appointment and suitable receiving support.

**Work.** Seller prepares and documents → receiver reserves support → carrier accepts specific custody → material travels under its profile → receiver checks identity and condition → agreed payment/remedy settles.

**Responsibility.** Sender, carrier and receiver have distinct duties. An intact seal can evidence handling history but does not prove working anatomy. Transporting stock does not give the carrier breeding or preparation rights.

**Limits.** Regional routes and currencies remain illustrative local services. No actual travel map or biological time limit is claimed complete.

### Special preparation and receiving commission

**Purpose.** An unusual raw graft, rematched pair, preserved historic core or stage-sensitive living source needs a defined additional process.

**Work.** Name the valuable original material → agree risks and tests → reserve real replacement/support stock → perform authorized work → record changes → accept for the new stated use.

**Responsibility.** The fitter cannot replace a rare core and still label it wholly original. Customer-owned stock remains customer-owned unless explicitly transferred.

**Limits.** No real surgery, chemicals or clinical preservation recipe. Exact duration and risk parameters remain material-specific authoring/calibration.

### A practical receiving example

**Situation.** A delivered pair has one intact responsive arm and another whose supported closure fails the quoted range.

**Decision.** Keep dispatch and receiving measurements distinct. The source records say whether the arms originally shared a donor; neither common origin nor that label restores paired performance.

**Completed resolution.** A separately sourced replacement can be fitted and rematched with owner consent. The completed pair carries both donor records and a new pair observation. If no suitable replacement exists, service or return remains the actual outcome.

A buyer seeking a complete ordinary kit can book a ready-to-bench handover and avoid managing the specialist process personally. A commercial breeder or fitter can own that work as a livelihood. A damaged or changed object never becomes an automatic demand that the same player establish a new breeding line.

**Appearance and construction.** A stock name and simple stage silhouette lead the card; a replaceable line identifies ready now, growing, or retained for reproduction. Source and a narrow observation sit below.

**Appearance and construction.** The entire horn profile is supported at its base in a shaped sleeve. Small directional marks show the tested push/lift orientation and the original core/interface boundary.

**Use.** A builder sees what the contact shape can enter and where force is expected to travel.

**Information to preserve.** A huge ornamental tip does not obscure a fractured root or become a universal damage symbol.

**Appearance and construction.** A transparent outline carries extended and folded limb boundaries, root positions and the actual included contact ends.
