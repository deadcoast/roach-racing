# RAD ROACH RACING
## WASTELANDER LANISTA — WAVE v0.16
### The Donor & Living-Material Atlas · Volume I

**Edition:** 0.16.0 · **Date:** 11 September 2026 · **Phase:** researched world-bible and compendium architecture.

A source insect is desirable because of what its actual body can supply, what its descendants may inherit, how it can be kept and prepared, and who has a reason to use it. This volume connects those questions to the living racer. It does not replace the build-and-race hobby with compulsory husbandry.

**Status:** all RRR organisms, population histories, graft practices and prices here are working fictional definitions. Source references support narrowly identified real mechanisms or source-hobby functions. They do not establish real transplant viability, game balance or player retention. No application is changed.

**Authority:** explicit author premises govern; W15 supplies current participation and progression principles; W14 supplies the existing anatomy and sect catalogue; W16 refines the named donor, material and budget questions. Earlier experiments remain historical evidence at their original scope. Unknown absolute scale, global post-winter chronology and broad aging/death remain open author decisions.

**The completed first-kit route remains intact.** Buy a prepared kit and build it. Catch a specimen and use, sell or keep it. Breed only when that is the chosen project. The complete supplier lifecycle can be handled by an actual Wrangloid, another breeder or an appropriate service.

### Rad-roach host — native foundations


`W16.DONOR.HOST` · **PCR body / living chassis foundation; native parts remain a complete racer**

Prior reference: [W14.ANATOMY.HOST](reference/W15/reference/RRR_Wave_14_Foundations.md) · [W14.ANATOMY.BODYPLAN](reference/W15/reference/RRR_Wave_14_Foundations.md) · [W15.SYSTEM.ENTRY](reference/W15/RRR_Wave_15_Foundations.md)

Biological/source-hobby anchors and limits: [W16.S01](RRR_Wave_16_Research.md#w16-s01) · [W16.S03](RRR_Wave_16_Research.md#w16-s03) · [W16.S13](RRR_Wave_16_Research.md#w16-s13)

**Recognition.** A continuous head–thorax–abdomen silhouette, six native running legs and recognizable segment seams remain visible under later grafts. Lowline, Needleback and Broadback describe established construction tendencies, not three closed species or rarity castes. A host passport depicts the particular individual rather than a stock product silhouette.


**Local ecology.** Sheltered service cavities supply moisture, cover and mixed detrital food. Roaches move between nearby refuges when those resources change. A warm pipe is a refuge, not a source of radiation-powered food; accessible plants, fungi and detritus must support the local source population without settling the whole post-winter biosphere.


#### Population and source knowledge


**Service-culvert residents.** Repeated use of low crevices makes low-profile animals common in observed collections; this is a proposed local frequency, not a capture bonus.


**What can be observed.** Shed cases, feeding traces and passage through a known opening.


**What it does not prove.** A cramped juvenile may look small because of development; narrow appearance does not prove desirable adult stride.


**Workshop-return population.** Unclaimed or deliberately released local stock may carry mixed ancestry from hobby lines; any release history needs evidence.


**What can be observed.** Unusual proportions or a recognizable mark warrant checking.


**What it does not prove.** A mark alone does not prove championship ancestry, ownership or superior genes.


#### Life stages and their different uses


| Stage | Role | Observable evidence | Useful decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Egg case | Embryos belong to a particular reproductive event. | Case condition and known mother can be recorded; viable count is not visible by naming it. | Acquire a whole future cohort only with enough supported capacity; unknown paternal history remains unknown. |
| Growing nymph | Native proportions and joints form through growth. | Stage, current body size and completed molts; no adult motor rating assigned. | Grow for host use or retain for a line; do not fit oversized fixed roots as if a juvenile were an adult. |
| Newly adult / conditioning | The final native form settles into working use. | Surface readiness, articulation and ordinary locomotion. | Assess working readiness separately from reproductive readiness. |
| Working adult | A native racer, breeder or collection animal. | Host measurements, current graft map, condition and reproductive history. | Campaign, breed, sell as whole stock or begin a new personally assembled project; competing commitments use the same animal. |

#### Reproductive unit and inheritance


Current working biology uses sexually compatible adult male/female host stock. A female can retain prior mating contribution; putting an unknown wild female beside a chosen male does not establish paternity. A breeder can instead use known unmated stock, a recorded mating cohort, or honestly label maternal-line-only offspring. Body proportions are correlated developmental outcomes of several inherited contributions, not a requirement that every child copy one entire parental silhouette. Grafts, applied finish and race records are not inherited. Exact loci, maturation durations and adult lifespans remain outside this qualitative profile.

#### Selectable contributions


##### Native working mass


**Inherited contribution.** Several inherited size and tissue-allocation contributions; lower is useful only while the declared support and supply remain adequate.


**Field screen.** Compare animals of similar mature stage and apparent fill.


**Controlled observation.** Weigh the native reference state and identify retained, replaced and filled material.


**Confounder to exclude.** A depleted animal is lighter without being a better host line.


**Construction or business use.** Needle & Thread supply or an efficient native foundation.


##### Stride and articulation


**Inherited contribution.** Limb proportions, joint travel and coordination develop together; no duplicate inherited speed stat.


**Field screen.** Observe unobstructed steps rather than one escape burst.


**Controlled observation.** Compare supported steps at the same burden and surface.


**Confounder to exclude.** Fear, fatigue, fouling or constrained habitat can shorten the observed stride.


**Construction or business use.** Useful body/limb foundation for drive and contact changes.


##### Root-bearing support


**Inherited contribution.** Heritable frame geometry and native material affect supported load.


**Field screen.** Observe flex under ordinary movement; excessive rigidity is not proof of strength.


**Controlled observation.** Record support under named loads with motion allowed.


**Confounder to exclude.** A fitted brace cannot be credited to the host genome.


**Construction or business use.** Foundry Heavy, horn support and later aerial root adaptation.


##### Natural band layout


**Inherited contribution.** A proposed multicomponent pattern tendency; expression also depends on mature stage and cuticle condition.


**Field screen.** Record pattern boundaries before finish is applied.


**Controlled observation.** Compare mature descendants under the same illumination and stage label.


**Confounder to exclude.** Freshly formed pale tissue, staining or a painted line.


**Construction or business use.** Collector foundations and recognizable breeder editions without a race bonus.


#### A purposeful selection project


A maker needing slender native stock retains a suitable mature animal immediately; a breeder asks which native contribution is missing and compares related descendants under the same conditions. Keep a bounded replacement reserve rather than every offspring. A child can become an ordinary Tin Can Special, another personal racer, or honest whole-stock sale before the premium line is complete.

#### Anatomy allocation and donor consequence


Registration never creates a second animal. Native anatomy displaced by a prepared exchange goes into an actual labeled cradle when that preparation retains it. Native structure consumed by custom work is absent; selecting “native” later needs retained or newly supplied anatomy. A living host cannot simultaneously donate the supporting plates it still requires. Host gametes do not copy rare graft cores.

#### Care, preparation and transport


A starter’s maintained collar preparation and ordinary care plan make repeated building accessible. An animal in active reproduction or a committed race is not free for incompatible work. Captured egg cases and unknown adults may be valuable without a pedigree certificate. Housing supports maintenance when breeding stops; personal racers do not expire because a game session ends.

#### Actual customers and ordinary output


| Customer | Output | Reason to buy |
|---|---|---|
| Two Nets | Complete native hosts and coordinated entry tins | Affordable finished first projects. |
| Independent fitter | Customer-owned host adaptation | Earn through usable roots, alignment and support rather than supplying all stock. |
| Saltglass Counter | Natural-pattern hosts with scoped ancestry | Collecting and display value independent of speed. |

**Construction opportunity.** Enabling: a slender host leaves genuine passing space and uses compatible light parts. Non-enabling: an undersupplied drive cannot exploit long stride. Alternative: retain the existing body and improve contact or fit instead of replacing the animal.


**Appearance and provenance.** The passport keeps natural body observations, applied finish, current build and old results distinct. The same champion may be refitted; its descendants are neither the champion nor copies of its winning kit.


**Unresolved rather than assumed.** Absolute scale, continuous bodyform ranges and lifespan calibration. Any global hereditary disease or permanent-loss system needs its own reviewed policy.

### Switchyard hopper


`W16.DONOR.HOPPER` · **Suspension and limb-geometry sources; active jumping is the RRR extension**

Prior reference: [W14.PART.HOPPERS](reference/W15/reference/RRR_Wave_14_Foundations.md) · [W14.PART.SPRINGKNEES](reference/W15/reference/RRR_Wave_14_Foundations.md) · [W15.SYSTEM.BROOD](reference/W15/RRR_Wave_15_Foundations.md)

Biological/source-hobby anchors and limits: [W16.S02](RRR_Wave_16_Research.md#w16-s02) · [W16.S04](RRR_Wave_16_Research.md#w16-s04)

**Recognition.** Long folded hind femora, clear joint saddles and thin forelegs make the donor readable at rest. A working pair compresses before release. The fuller right or left leg is not displayed as a random cosmetic variation when it changes usable paired anatomy.


**Local ecology.** Grass margins, seed-bearing rubble and protected laying soil around the Switchyard provide food and refuge. Populations use both walking and hopping. A long escape leap is a response to circumstances, not proof that every race setup should command maximum launch.


#### Population and source knowledge


**Split-sleeper grass fringe.** Sheltered breaks provide repeated short hops and frequent recontact.


**What can be observed.** Watch paired release and the first stable steps after landing.


**What it does not prove.** Short landing distance can hide a poor high-load response.


**Exposed ballast verge.** Open gaps make longer jumps easier to observe; local size and spring variation overlaps the fringe.


**What can be observed.** Inspect intact joint saddles and takeoff alignment over a known gap.


**What it does not prove.** Wind, slope and launch angle can inflate apparent distance; no automatic high-return genotype.


#### Life stages and their different uses


| Stage | Role | Observable evidence | Useful decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Egg pod | Supported embryos in a laying substrate. | Pod identity and laying history, not a promised hatch count. | Reserve prospective young before starting additional pods. |
| Nymphal growth | Successive hind-limb assemblies form during growth. | Working stage and fresh versus conditioned joint surfaces. | Retain paired clearance during molts; juvenile performance does not certify adult material. |
| Conditioned adult | Primary source for complete Hoppers or landing joints. | Coordinated load, release and recovery at a known size. | Use now, test, breed or sell; adult donor does not need to be the farthest jumper. |
| Supported post-preparation adult | Hind source is missing; other functions remain only where supported. | Anatomy loss and support provision remain visible. | Retain for reproduction only if feeding, posture and reproductive behavior remain feasible; no guaranteed fertility from a living database record. |

#### Reproductive unit and inheritance


Sexual pairing uses mature compatible stock of the same donor form. Work-producing muscles, elastic geometry and coordinating anatomy are distinct inherited contributions that can be correlated. Do not use a single universal dominant “jump gene” or automatically take parental maxima. A progressive-joint morphology is a named candidate branch: its visible shape must also exhibit the desired force/travel behavior. Relatedness and repeated parent identity remain recorded; any inbreeding consequence must follow the authored model, not an unexplained universal fine.

#### Selectable contributions


##### Useful elastic return


**Inherited contribution.** Material and geometry of the loaded spring assembly.


**Field screen.** Observe preload and displacement through a repeated small hop.


**Controlled observation.** Compare output after equal supplied work at the same burden and launch orientation.


**Confounder to exclude.** Different preload, platform compliance or body mass.


**Construction or business use.** Complete Hoppers; stronger available launches or less input for the same required maneuver.


##### Paired release


**Inherited contribution.** Timing and coordination of the working pair, distinct from each limb’s strength.


**Field screen.** A crooked takeoff or repeated clean release is a cue.


**Controlled observation.** Measure mismatch with paired geometry and mounting held comparable.


**Confounder to exclude.** One damaged joint or an asymmetric test support.


**Construction or business use.** A synchrony founder can improve a powerful but yaw-prone line.


##### Receiving travel and recovery


**Inherited contribution.** Joint geometry, return control and tissue behavior after impact.


**Field screen.** Observe compression and the first useful running contact.


**Controlled observation.** Compare the same arrival load, receiving posture and recovery criterion.


**Confounder to exclude.** A soft landing surface can conceal weak joints.


**Construction or business use.** Springknees and ordinary complete Hopper kits.


##### Usable-stage consistency


**Inherited contribution.** A heritable developmental tendency evaluated across comparable broods, not a clock gene already quantified.


**Field screen.** A source cohort has a visibly broad or narrow readiness distribution.


**Controlled observation.** Record when actual members become ready under stable rearing conditions.


**Confounder to exclude.** Unequal feeding or crowded molts.


**Construction or business use.** Suppliers seeking predictable ordinary batches rather than maximum race output.


#### A purposeful selection project


Use the W15 split-brood plan: pair a return-focused line with a plausible synchronization contribution, record intermediate descendants, and retain only the chosen research candidates and target replacements. Saleable complete sets and joint-only preparations provide different intermediate uses. A promising unmapped founder is a reason to investigate, not a guaranteed premium order.

#### Anatomy allocation and donor consequence


The complete hind pair includes limb geometry, elastic loading/release and its landing joints. Springknees consume the corresponding joint source; it cannot also remain inside another complete pair. A separately sourced Lockstep can replace or augment a compatible coordinating interface only if the original timing structure is declared in that design; do not credit two complete synchronization systems without explaining their connection.

#### Care, preparation and transport


Transport supports both hind roots, keeps them unloaded, and avoids forcing the paired joints into a storage posture they cannot tolerate. After preparation, a matched bridge maintains their relative orientation. Preservation maintains usable anatomy, not a permanently charged spring. Damaged limbs may have reproductive value while failing direct-product inspection.

#### Actual customers and ordinary output


| Customer | Output | Reason to buy |
|---|---|---|
| Two Nets | Ordinary complete sets | Last Laugh conversions and Third Friend tins. |
| Cinderhouse | Observed complementary founders | Continue a high-return line without demanding final phenotype in the new parent. |
| Foundry fitters | Springknees and alignment work | Better landing or a corrected setup without requiring a whole new racer. |

**Construction opportunity.** Enabling: a visible lane obstruction with sufficient takeoff, clearance and landing. Non-enabling: a closed ceiling or insufficient closing speed. Alternatives: an ordinary pass, lower preload, a different line or landing preparation. A Hopper need not wait for a rival to use an authorized route.


**Appearance and provenance.** Joint saddle marks and source tags remain identifiable after grafting. A historic-looking Hopper kit and an authentic founder-descended line are different claims.


**Unresolved rather than assumed.** Specific trait distributions, linkage, inherited progressive-joint expression and use-life. No automatic leap, passing, recovery or victory bonus.

### Glassfoot stick insect


`W16.DONOR.GLASSFOOT` · **Tire/contact function; alternate preparation supplies a contact brake**

Prior reference: [W14.PART.STICKTOE](reference/W15/reference/RRR_Wave_14_Foundations.md) · [W14.PART.GRIPSTRIP](reference/W15/reference/RRR_Wave_14_Foundations.md) · [W15.SYSTEM.FIELD](reference/W15/RRR_Wave_15_Foundations.md)

Biological/source-hobby anchors and limits: [W16.S05](RRR_Wave_16_Research.md#w16-s05)

**Recognition.** A twig-like body carries distinct contact regions at the feet. Artists should distinguish a load-bearing heel region, a terminal attachment region and the articulating foot that changes their exposure. A glossy enlarged blob cannot represent every contact function.


**Local ecology.** Glazed overgrowth supplies feeding foliage and routes over smooth panes, stems and dusty ledges. Contact tissue matters to both movement and staying attached. A humid wall changes the observation conditions without permanently improving the insect’s heredity.


#### Population and source knowledge


**Dewglass runners.** Smooth leaves and glass make attachment and release easy to compare.


**What can be observed.** Watch a planted foot unload and peel away on a known surface.


**What it does not prove.** Condensation is a condition, not inherited superior adhesion.


**Outer hanging-frame stock.** Dusty ledges and broken stems offer varied fouling and foot placement.


**What can be observed.** Compare repeated usable steps after crossing loose residue.


**What it does not prove.** A clean individual may simply have groomed recently; origin alone does not certify self-cleaning material.


#### Life stages and their different uses


| Stage | Role | Observable evidence | Useful decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Egg | Individual laid eggs or a labeled family cohort. | Source and age evidence, not performance bands. | Buy as future stock only with plant and rearing provision. |
| Growing nymph | Pads, limbs and feeding structures develop together. | Stage and successful molts; preserve vertical clearance. | A crowded molt can impair a genetically promising foot. |
| Mature adult | Primary contact source and reproductive stock. | Paired foot condition and comparable attachment/release. | Choose the anatomy’s direct use or future line contribution. |
| Post-pad preparation | The specified pads are missing. | Actual supported footing and remaining intact contacts. | Provide a usable standing and feeding arrangement; do not sell as an intact six-foot donor. |

#### Reproductive unit and inheritance


This named working Glassfoot form uses sexual egg production with recorded maternal identity and controlled mating when exact parentage is claimed. No generic parthenogenesis flag is inferred from the insect family. A future uniparental line would need its own inheritance and fertility definition. Contact geometry, surface properties and release mechanics may segregate differently; native pad anatomy can combine strong planted friction with little adhesive resistance when unloaded. That is a reason to permit genuine multi-property improvements.

#### Selectable contributions


##### Supported purchase


**Inherited contribution.** Material and microstructure response under specified normal and shear loads.


**Field screen.** Observe reliable planting without repeated slipping.


**Controlled observation.** Measure the designated foot region at equal area, orientation, load and surface.


**Confounder to exclude.** A wider prepared pad or extra preload raises contact without changing the inherited tissue.


**Construction or business use.** Fast runners and ordinary club contact sets.


##### Release response


**Inherited contribution.** The foot/interface’s ability to disengage under the intended unloading direction.


**Field screen.** Watch the transition to the next step, not maximum hanging force.


**Controlled observation.** Compare release latency and required unloading action under stated conditions.


**Confounder to exclude.** Incorrectly reversed preparation can make good material appear slow.


**Construction or business use.** A missing release contribution for an adhesive but cadence-limited line.


##### Fouled-surface usefulness


**Inherited contribution.** Retention and restoration of useful contact through a declared contamination challenge.


**Field screen.** Observe progression across a dusty boundary and later clean steps.


**Controlled observation.** Record clean baseline, stated fouling and permitted cleaning separately.


**Confounder to exclude.** Recent grooming and unknown residue thickness.


**Construction or business use.** Dust-tolerant editions; not an all-weather immunity.


##### Channelled contact form


**Inherited contribution.** A proposed structural variant whose inheritance and function must be observed independently.


**Field screen.** Visible channels identify the form, not its quality.


**Controlled observation.** Compare channel orientation, remaining area, purchase and release.


**Confounder to exclude.** Preparation cuts can imitate natural channel patterns.


**Construction or business use.** Natural-variant collection and tested surface-specific preparations.


#### A purposeful selection project


A high-purchase line may retain a modest specimen for quick release. Split siblings between controlled contact readings and reproduction; removed-foot assays cannot be recorded as a second living animal. Improvement within the same qualification band remains visible. Ordinary sets, individually sized pairs and supported Gripstrips give nonpremium output specific uses.

#### Anatomy allocation and donor consequence


List actual heel/toe regions and limb-pair allocations. A supplied contact set can contain differently prepared pair groups; its mass and regions are counted once. Gripstrip production allocates source contact material to a different support and engagement direction, not an extra copy. Donor capacity and preparation area must not be silently equated with six identical maximum-size products.

#### Care, preparation and transport


The working root and hydrated contact face need different supports from a dry horn. Keep contact faces separated from packing fibers and forceful adhesion to their own tray. Routine cleaning can restore fouled function; torn working material is not restored by pressing a universal clean button. A maintained ordinary preparation should tolerate normal hobby exchange without a new assay every time.

#### Actual customers and ordinary output


| Customer | Output | Reason to buy |
|---|---|---|
| Needle & Thread makers | Adequate purchase with quick release | Let the supplied drive become useful steps. |
| Apron fitters | Appropriate Gripstrips | Control approach engagement through material plus mounting. |
| Saltglass collectors | Verified natural channel or color form | Appearance can be the primary purchase; its race properties remain separately observed. |

**Construction opportunity.** Enabling: a fast drive bottlenecked by contact release. Non-enabling: inadequate drive delivery or a suspension fault. Alternative: clean or reorient current pads before replacing the source. A component can genuinely grip well and release well; no obligatory unrelated weakness is invented.


**Appearance and provenance.** The stock card distinguishes natural channels, cuts made during preparation and applied coloration. A worn pad can still document a historic construction, but is not sold as a fresh high-performance contact surface.


**Unresolved rather than assumed.** The exact sexual profile is a proposal for this form, not a claim about every phasmid. Quantitative inheritance, material lifetime and channel-form frequencies need later authoring.

### Furnace cicada


`W16.DONOR.CICADA` · **Motor / drive; adapted output can also serve a complete Flyer construction**

Prior reference: [W14.PART.REDLINE](reference/W15/reference/RRR_Wave_14_Foundations.md) · [W15.FAMILY.NEEDLE](reference/W15/RRR_Wave_15_Foundations.md)

Biological/source-hobby anchors and limits: [W16.S02](RRR_Wave_16_Research.md#w16-s02) · [W16.S07](RRR_Wave_16_Research.md#w16-s07)

**Recognition.** A stout segmented body with ribbed thorax, folded translucent wings and a visibly different root-feeding juvenile. The useful drive is prepared contractile tissue with working attachments. Do not draw a sound chamber as an electric motor whose noise determines its power.


**Local ecology.** Rooted signal posts and recovering vegetation around old heat routes provide a source ecology. The thermal refuge is useful only while living roots and suitable laying growth remain available. Adults advertise their presence; the juvenile supply is less conspicuous and can be damaged by indiscriminate excavation.


#### Population and source knowledge

**Local ecology.** Cooling ponds with supported emergence edges and sufficient aquatic prey supply juveniles; adults range over nearby open air and return to suitable reproductive waters. Water quality, prey and emergence surfaces are real requirements. A clear-looking pond or an attractive blue surface does not certify productive rearing.


#### Population and source knowledge


**Sheltered cooling-basin margins.** Short protected passages make controlled takeoff and turning visible.


**What can be observed.** Inspect symmetrical expansion, root movement and clean short maneuvers.


**What it does not prove.** Shelter can make weak flight appear stable; compare condition and load before claiming superior control.


**Open spillway margin.** Longer exposed passages offer sustained-motion observations.


**What can be observed.** Watch repeated forward travel and supported turns, then inspect roots.


**What it does not prove.** Wind assistance and recent feeding can inflate apparent endurance; the location does not manufacture a “speed species”.


#### Life stages and their different uses


| Stage | Role | Observable evidence | Useful decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Egg | Future aquatic stock on the profile’s suitable laying substrate. | Recorded source and viable handling conditions, not guaranteed hatch. | Secure rearing capacity and prey provision. |
| Aquatic juvenile | Predatory growth and successive juvenile molts. | Body size/stage and available feeding space. | Avoid incompatible mixed-size densities; use an appropriate nursery or buy later-stage stock. |
| Final emergence | Leaves water and forms the adult without a pupal stage. | Emergence support, completed withdrawal and wing expansion. | The nursery must reserve dry clearance before the juvenile reaches it. |
| Soft-wing adult | Expanded surfaces and roots continue conditioning. | Soft or folded margins, pale appearance and weak initial movement. | Do not transport it as folded loose stock or grade it against a conditioned flier. |
| Conditioned/reproductive adult | Usable wing/root donor, observed flyer or breeding individual. | Intact surfaces, controlled flight and reproductive history. | Keep, measure, breed, prepare or display; the donor’s flight is not automatically the host’s eventual performance. |

#### Reproductive unit and inheritance


Sexual reproductive adults produce aquatic offspring. Wing geometry, structural stiffness, root response and endurance are quantitative or structural inherited contributions with development-sensitive expression. A crumpled emergence can ruin a valuable wing without proving an inferior line. Offspring of adults with excellent flight still require appropriate aquatic and emergence conditions. Conditioned adults may be acquired directly; the Flyer hobby does not require every entrant to own a pond.

##### Useful surface and torsional form

**Controlled observation.** Compare the prepared surface/root under a named load and motion protocol.

**Controlled observation.** Measure support and motion range with the intended linkage, not wing span alone.


**Confounder to exclude.** Damaged root or insufficient muscle supply.


**Construction or business use.** A supported aerial build and precision fitting service.


##### Repeated-work endurance


**Inherited contribution.** Usable material and physiological support over the intended work sequence.


**Field screen.** Watch repeated activity; one fast dash is insufficient.


**Controlled observation.** Measure changes across a defined duty with condition and supply tracked.


**Confounder to exclude.** Recent activity, feeding or wind.


**Construction or business use.** Longer aerial routes and dependable flight components.

**Inherited contribution.** Family-linked development plus the specific nursery’s conditions; estimate neither from genes alone.


**Field screen.** Observe several real emergence outcomes.


**Controlled observation.** Record conditioned usable adults, failed expansion and causes across cohorts.


**Confounder to exclude.** Poor perch spacing can make a good line look genetically weak.


**Construction or business use.** An emergence specialist earns by preserving usable output, not giving an invisible flight tier.


#### A purposeful selection project


Retain prospective founders before consuming full root assemblies. A supplier can split a project between breeding adults, conditioned wing sets, supported juvenile transfer and paid emergence. Weak immediate flight after a poor emergence does not automatically eliminate reproductive value, but any claim needs the surviving animal’s actual readiness. A wing-only visual specimen can remain desirable while failing powered-flight product requirements.

Conditioned adults need protected wing volume. A prepared array supports each surface unloaded and protects its active roots. A repaired membrane may regain a limited declared use, but altered mass, balance or tearing must be reassessed. Native roach tissue does not regenerate an unlimited supply of rare wing panels.

## 5. Material classes and the life of a preparation


A commercial part can contain several material classes at once. The class of a durable plate does not cancel the needs of its living interface. A complete component is ready only when every required working element is suitable. These profiles define what must be maintained; they do not prescribe real medical media, surgery or transplantation.

Ordinary kit contents come with the appropriate supported condition and an intelligible bench procedure. Difficult material is a specialization, not a compulsory laboratory that every customer must build. The mass-damper distinction remains functional: a supported moving assembly addresses rebound; a store of energy is not a damper merely because it is heavy. [W16.S03](RRR_Wave_16_Research.md#w16-s03) · [W16.S15](RRR_Wave_16_Research.md#w16-s15)

### Active contractile root


`W16.MATERIAL.ACTIVE`

Example preparations: [W14.PART.REDLINE](reference/W15/reference/RRR_Wave_14_Foundations.md) · [W14.PART.TORQUE](reference/W15/reference/RRR_Wave_14_Foundations.md)

**Physical subject.** Living work-producing tissue with supported attachments and exchange interfaces. Relaxed muscle is not a charged battery; its source of work remains the host’s supply.


**Ready condition.** Intact roots, available supply/control interface and an observed ordinary working response at stated readiness. Appearance alone cannot certify output.


**Holding.** An appropriate fictional buffered cradle maintains exchange and supported shape. No temperatures, reagents or real transplantation recipe are specified. Holding consumes declared care and supplies.


**Working on a host.** A seated root must also be supplied and supported; an installed but unready drive is not fully available output. The component’s identity and inherited observations persist through remounting.


**Ordinary service and restoration.** Restore recoverable readiness, clean external connections, adjust the load path or replace a separately supplied interface. A recovered output is restoration, not genetic improvement.


**Loss of function.** Dead or irreversibly torn working tissue is not restarted by refilling the reserve. Remaining structural material may have a specifically described use, but is no longer sold as the same active drive.


**Transfer and arrival requirements.** Dispatch condition, maintained support, maximum authorized unsupported interval and arrival test belong to the particular preparation. Expiry limits are empirical design parameters for later calibration, never silently infinite.


**Accessible participation.** Ready components include ordinary initial support and a known bench procedure. The buyer need not construct a tissue laboratory to fit an entry drive.

<a id="w16-econ-01"></a>

**W16.ECON.01 — Each sheet distinguishes a provider’s own stock from customer-owned stock and bought inputs.**

A fitting business need not finance a breeding operation.

<a id="w16-econ-02"></a>

**W16.ECON.02 — Quotes, agreed payments, earned receipts, retained inventory and capital assets are distinct quantities.**

A completed recipe, a future kit and an unsold insect are not spendable money.

<a id="w16-econ-03"></a>

**W16.ECON.03 — The three budget sheets use illustrative credits and declared outcome counts, not prices or rates measured in the prior prototype.**

Arithmetic feasibility does not certify balanced production.

<a id="w16-econ-04"></a>

**W16.ECON.04 — Labor is paid to a separate household/labor account in these cash sheets; its living costs are not also deducted from workshop cash as the same payment.**

No apparent margin depends on pretending the maker works for free or double-counting household spending.

<a id="w16-econ-05"></a>

**W16.ECON.05 — Existing stock used in production has an acquisition or production cost, including failed output where relevant.**

Breeding one’s own donors does not make their material free.

<a id="w16-econ-06"></a>

**W16.ECON.06 — Negative-budget scenarios remain negative; do not rewrite the historical Wave12 150-paid/112-received result.**

A later viable specification does not turn an earlier loss into unearned profit.

<a id="w16-econ-07"></a>

**W16.ECON.07 — Before committing, distinguish positive eventual margin from enough working cash to reach delivery.**

A fallback costing 212 cannot be paid entirely from 180 without a disclosed financing or staged-payment route.

<a id="w16-econ-08"></a>

**W16.ECON.08 — A funded request has finite quantity and relevant acceptance; replenishment needs another identified source of funds.**

No tank, recipe publication or observed line prints money automatically.

<a id="w16-econ-09"></a>

**W16.ECON.09 — A recipient assembles the actual delivered kit; the seller’s original racer remains a separate completed project.**

Commercial success leads back to the kit-racing hobby rather than ending at a receipt.

A one-period positive example is not a guarantee of permanent passive income.

## 7. Construction opportunities and useful alternatives


These are focused opportunity briefs, not a complete map or scripted equipment test. Every construction needs situations where its anatomy can work, situations where that special function does not help, and legitimate alternatives. A high-quality part can be genuinely superior without creating an impossible maneuver.

<a id="w16-opportunity-needle"></a>
### Needle & Thread


**Enabling case.** A clear pace section where foot release limits otherwise useful drive cadence.


**Nonenabling case.** Restricted supply or a loaded root cannot use the faster step cycle.


**Legitimate alternative.** Restore contact condition, change leverage or use the existing body with another pair.


**Sources and services.** Glassfoot release line and measured Cicada drive; ordinary complete-kit purchase remains valid.


<a id="w16-opportunity-hop"></a>
### Last Laugh / Third Friend


**Enabling case.** A pass or marked branch has actual load, air and landing room; synchronization affects unwanted rotation.


**Nonenabling case.** No viable landing or insufficient approach; adding return alone does not create the opportunity.


**Legitimate alternative.** A clean ground pass, lower preload, another route or better receiving joints.


**Sources and services.** Hopper source, optional Clockleaf timing preparation, separate reservoir and Keel.


<a id="w16-opportunity-heavy"></a>
### Foundry Heavy


**Enabling case.** Forceful launch or recovery on a loaded body with useful contact and an adequate support path.


**Nonenabling case.** A heavier frame on a lightly loaded smooth sprint adds little of the intended benefit.


**Legitimate alternative.** Relocate the load, reduce carried equipment, change feet, or select a different delivery range.


**Sources and services.** Scarab drive and internal ribs; armor remains a separate allocation.


<a id="w16-opportunity-glass"></a>
### Glassline


**Enabling case.** A readable disturbance can be detected before actual corrective limbs must act.


**Nonenabling case.** The build has no actuator or surface available to use sensor information.


**Legitimate alternative.** Guardknuckles on a suitable wall, a physical brace, or a changed line.


**Sources and services.** Sumpfly matched pair and workable contact.


<a id="w16-opportunity-apron"></a>
### Apron


**Enabling case.** Actual raised approach contact or rail sliding gives pad or guide material work to do.


**Nonenabling case.** No contact means no special effect from either preparation.


**Legitimate alternative.** Different engagement, longer landing, ordinary slowing or another permitted path.


**Sources and services.** Appropriate material profile, fit session and actual course geometry.


<a id="w16-opportunity-sky"></a>
### Skyhook


**Enabling case.** A complete aerial gate route requires sustained force, climb/turn control and a supported landing transition.


**Nonenabling case.** Four loose wings or passive vanes without drive, roots and control cannot supply sustained flight.


**Legitimate alternative.** A conditioned ready kit, paid emergence instead of owning a pond, or a separate ground-hopper project.


**Sources and services.** Glasswing emergence, Cicada flight drive, Orchard or other adequate reserve, actual control.

### Workers are product stock, not a universal breeding pair


`W16.AMEND.05`

**Earlier ambiguity.** A Nectar Crop donor could be treated as interchangeable with an adult founder of a solitary insect.


**Replacement definition.** Orchard breeding units identify queen status, mating evidence, worker/brood support and transferred provisions. Two crop workers do not form a complete colony. A divided colony transfers actual individuals and leaves an actual remainder.


**Purpose.** Give the colony specialist a genuine productive role without imposing that role on every sprint racer.

### Structural durability does not make the whole graft immortal


`W16.AMEND.07`

**Earlier ambiguity.** A durable plate could be interpreted as proving that its living root never needs care.


**Replacement definition.** The rigid/elastic core and living interface have separate condition requirements. Service can retain an original core while replacing a separately sourced interface, with that replacement disclosed.


**Purpose.** Enable repairs and heritage objects without infinite rare-tissue renewal.

### Damage changes use, not every kind of value


`W16.AMEND.08`

**Earlier ambiguity.** Loss of a working limb could make the whole donor worthless or guarantee that it remains a useful breeder.


**Replacement definition.** Assess the remaining anatomy, reproduction requirements and support capacity of the specific form. A damaged but reproductively capable specimen can matter; lost or impaired reproductive function is not ignored.


**Purpose.** Keep alternative uses honest and prevent every damaged capture becoming guaranteed cash or useless debt.

### Population tendencies are not environmental stat awards


`W16.AMEND.09`

**Earlier ambiguity.** A specialized site could become a location that adds a gene when visited.


**Replacement definition.** Local frequency, source history, developmental conditions and current observations remain separate. The same site can yield ordinary or exceptional animals; a project selection never edits the existing population.


**Purpose.** Knowledge improves search and selection while captures retain stable identities.

### Care plans are chosen commitments, not magic pause modes


`W16.AMEND.10`

**Earlier ambiguity.** Maintenance could be understood as freezing active juveniles or all expenses.


**Replacement definition.** Maintenance halts new starts, not current development. Appropriate holding, transition capacity and support remain actual obligations. Default offline pause is a player-time rule; it does not selectively advance output while stopping care.


**Purpose.** Carry the milestone’s accessible stopping rule without inventing costless biology.

### Operating remainder is not lifetime profitability


`W16.AMEND.11`

**Earlier ambiguity.** A positive receipt or a post-sale cash balance could be presented as a sustainable business.


**Replacement definition.** State scope, paid operator remuneration, operating outlays, capital, retained inventory, accepted demand and cash timing. Distinguish a conditional operating surplus from lifetime return or equilibrium prices. Retain the Wave12 negative example unchanged.


**Purpose.** Produce usable budget design rather than retroactively hiding the known loss.

### Appearance can complete a project without a performance claim


`W16.AMEND.12`

**Earlier ambiguity.** A attractive marking or historic tin could need a fabricated speed or universal price multiplier to be considered useful.


**Replacement definition.** Appearance, provenance and applied finish have their own intended buyers and completed display outcomes. Natural variation needs its own evidence before inheritance is claimed. No exact antique is made genuine by an anniversary label.


**Purpose.** Preserve collector and finisher livelihoods alongside racing and production.

## 10. Editorial cases for downstream interpretation


These are authored reference questions with expected interpretations, not executed gameplay experiments, acceptance by real users or biological validation.

| Case | Situation | Required interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| W16.CASE.PARENT | A wild-caught adult lays eggs after sharing a case with a selected male. | Record observed contact, not verified paternity. Existing reproductive history may matter. Ordinary offspring remain useful without an invented exact pedigree. |
| W16.CASE.HOPPER | A high-return project receives a low-return but precisely synchronized hopper. | It can be a valuable complementary founder while failing the intended premium component target. Use observed evidence and a bounded retained-stock plan. |
| W16.CASE.PAD | Two feet stick equally strongly in a static demonstration. | They may release differently or use different regions at running loads. A single adhesion comparison does not establish equal racing contact. |
| W16.CASE.MOLT | A late Clockleaf becomes an adult during an unplanned long journey. | Its juvenile coupling order may fail, while the same individual can remain reproductive stock. Do not award the removed juvenile part or erase the animal. |
| W16.CASE.DETACHED | A prepared Lockstep’s living donor would have molted by now. | The detached preparation follows its support and condition profile. It does not transform merely because the donor’s stage advanced, and it is not automatically permanent. |
| W16.CASE.WAX | Every suitable larva is prepared into reserves. | That cohort supplies no unharvested adults. A replacement target requires actual retained larvae or separately acquired adults, not a cloned parent token. |
| W16.CASE.QUEEN | A customer buys two high-delivery workers and an empty hive. | The customer has crop-donor stock, not a self-sustaining reproductive unit. Buy a specified nucleus or supply the missing real reproductive/caregiver inputs. |
| W16.CASE.WINGS | An excellent newly emerged Glasswing is pressed into a flat component tray. | Strong inheritance does not prevent developmental handling damage. The full array needs expansion, conditioning and actual sweep/root support; later recovery is product-specific. |
| W16.CASE.SENSOR | A Gyropod-equipped racer is suspended with no usable corrective actuator. | Information alone cannot exert the missing force. The part is not necessarily defective; the construction lacks a usable response in this situation. |
| W16.CASE.SHELL | A historic horn core is intact but its active root is no longer serviceable. | It can be preserved as an object. Working restoration requires a real replacement or repair path and disclosure; neither automatic resurrection nor total loss of collection value is required. |
| W16.CASE.CARE | A breeder switches from production to maintenance while young remain. | No new starts occur, but young still develop and occupy suitable stages; real care or an appropriate service continues. |
| W16.CASE.CASH | Two tins are finished but only one customer has paid by the accounting boundary. | Record one receipt, two sets of incurred costs, and the remaining living kit as cared-for inventory. Its listed price is not spendable income. |
| W16.CASE.GROWTH | A profitable selected pair leaves no affordable room for the next maximum brood. | The breeding business cannot commit on expected mortality. Reduce starts, finish current obligations, obtain real supported capacity, or decline a new order. |
| W16.CASE.FORM | A channelled pad looks like an existing high-performance donor photograph. | The form justifies inspection, not a grade. Establish its actual loaded purchase, release and fouling behavior; later pedigree evidence answers a separate inheritance question. |
