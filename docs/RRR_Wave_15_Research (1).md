# RRR — Wave 15 Research Register

**Purpose:** connect source evidence to authored progression changes without pretending the research tested RRR. **Checked:** 11 September 2026.

## Evidence discipline

Primary hobby documentation establishes source functions. Empirical research supplies findings within particular samples and methods. Developer descriptions and design accounts supply practitioner rationale or a stated product loop, not independent proof of success. New RRR anatomy, services, progression policies and customer systems remain authored working proposals.

Publisher abstracts were sufficient for the narrow reported observations below; they are not substituted for full methodological review. The GDC entry uses its official session description, not unviewed talk content. No empirical percentage is turned into an expected RRR retention gain.

## Ryan, Rigby & Przybylski (2006) — The Motivational Pull of Video Games

`W15.S05` · **Evidence class:** `primary_empirical_research`

**Source.** `https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11031-006-9051-8`

**Observed statement.** Across the reported studies, perceived competence and autonomy were associated with enjoyment; a multiplayer-community study also examined relatedness and future play.

**Authored design use.** Evaluate ownership of decisions, understandable improvement and meaningful social recognition separately.

**Limit.** Associations in those studies do not prove that these RRR revisions improve retention; NPC recognition is a design hypothesis, not the same evidence as human multiplayer relatedness.

**Read scope.** Publisher abstract and bibliographic information; no full-method replication.

## Andersen et al. (CHI 2012) — The Impact of Tutorials on Games of Varying Complexity

`W15.S07` · **Evidence class:** `primary_empirical_research`

**Source.** `https://grail.cs.washington.edu/projects/game-abtesting/chi2012/`

**Observed statement.** Eight tutorial designs across three games produced effects that depended on complexity; the simpler games did not show the same significant engagement benefit as the complex game.

**Authored design use.** Expose help where a decision needs it; preserve experimentation and advanced depth without an obligatory complete lecture.

**Limit.** Results from those games supply a question to test, not a universal tutorial length or expected percentage gain.

**Read scope.** Authors’ project abstract and resource listing.

**Checked.** 2026-09-11

## What this research does not establish

No proof that breeding alone explains Mewgenics’ reception; no reconstruction of its exact inheritance rules; no copied Factorio quality percentages; no claim that physical self-assembly effects transfer unchanged to a digital roach; no claim that NPC recognition is equivalent to human multiplayer relatedness; no complete source-game item database inferred from one image; no diagnosis of player addiction; no measured RRR retention result.
