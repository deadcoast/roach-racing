## 1. Translate the working decisions, not a car-shaped silhouette

SEGA supplies the local acquisition/build/rivalry reference; Tamiya supplies distinct physical component and modular-course functions. Powered flight, directed gland offense and self-righting are RRR additions, not features retroactively attributed to a source menu. [W18.S01](RRR_Wave_18_Research.md#w18-s01) · [W18.S02](RRR_Wave_18_Research.md#w18-s02) · [W18.S03](RRR_Wave_18_Research.md#w18-s03) · [W18.S04](RRR_Wave_18_Research.md#w18-s04) · [W18.S05](RRR_Wave_18_Research.md#w18-s05)

| Source function | RRR subject | Decision retained |
|---|---|---|
| Body / chosen vehicle | The same persistent host and native plan | Loaded mass, proportions, native articulation and the space available for roots. A flight conversion does not create a different animal. |
| Frame | Native support plus deliberately arranged thoracic/root bracing | Transmit wing-root and contact loads without binding the required motion. Support is not general armor. |
| Motor | Flight-configured drive musculature | Supply useful work at a cadence and force the wing transmission can use. A wing outline is not an extra motor. |
| Gears | Wingstroke yoke: leverage and coordination declared separately | Choose stroke travel/force and coordinate the working surfaces. A phase adjustment is not a source of power. |
| Battery | Actual reserve and delivery interfaces | Carry usable energy, meet required delivery and account for fill and changing mass. High capacity cannot bypass a restrictive outlet. |
| Tires | Launch and landing contact preparations | Feet transfer work and regain purchase on actual surfaces. Airborne traction is not invented to keep a familiar stat relevant everywhere. |
| Side stabilizer | Native sensing, optional Gyropods and real control authority | Detect a disturbance and use available wing, limb or body action. A sensor supplies neither lift nor a support surface. |
| Suspension | Receiving limbs and landing couplers | Accommodate touchdown and restore running/perching. They do not soften arbitrary mid-air collisions globally. |
| Front wing | Browfoils, vanes and passive trim surfaces | Alter aerodynamic loading and attitude through actual air interaction. They are not a complete powered-flight array. |
| Appearance | Natural pattern, shell finish, breeder and maker identifiers | Keep the same host and grafts recognizable in a case and in motion. An edition can be desirable without a performance buff. |
| Rollers / stays — physical-hobby extension | Retained native shell or Railshoes on supported ground transitions | Actual rail contact only; an airborne gate is not a magnetic wall-following guide. |
| Brakes / dampers — physical-hobby extension | Ground-contact brakes and landing/rebound preparations | Ventral material acts on a reached surface; aerial slowing requires an aerodynamic or propulsive change. A Keel addresses its relevant oscillation, not all disturbances. |
| Modular course — physical-hobby extension | Owned frames, supports, portals, decks and declared route volumes | Construct real geometry with usable transitions; route lines on a drawing are not physical rails. |
| RRR-native extensions | Powered arrays, Kickback, Boiler and related prepared anatomy | Retain the original derivative sport. These need no fabricated PCR menu equivalents to justify their existence. |

**Flight changes the value of existing parts without changing their meaning.** Feet work during ground contact. Sensing requires a response. A reservoir supplies its actual contents. A vane interacts with air. The wearer remains the same living host. The dedicated wing array is additional anatomy, not a relabeling of the source game’s front-aero slot.

### Longwing locust

`W18.DONOR.LONGWING`

**Reference role.** RRR-powered flight; work, transmission, frame, reserve and contact retain the PCR decision structure.

**Earlier reference.** [W14.PART.LONGWING](reference/W17/reference/W16/reference/W15/reference/RRR_Wave_14_Foundations.md#w14-part-longwing) · [W14.SET.SKY](reference/W17/reference/W16/reference/W15/reference/RRR_Wave_14_Foundations.md#w14-set-sky) · [W16.MATERIAL.WING](reference/W17/reference/W16/RRR_Wave_16_Foundations.md#w16-material-wing) · [W15.BROOD.01](reference/W17/reference/W16/reference/W15/RRR_Wave_15_Foundations.md#w15-brood-01)

**Research scope.** [W18.S06](RRR_Wave_18_Research.md#w18-s06) · [W18.S07](RRR_Wave_18_Research.md#w18-s07) · [W18.S15](RRR_Wave_18_Research.md#w18-s15) · [W18.S17](RRR_Wave_18_Research.md#w18-s17)

**Recognition.** An elongated grass-feeding insect with narrow forewings over broad folded hindwing fans, articulated flight roots and substantial hind legs. The closed package is slender; the opened working body is not. Show the fan folds, forewing margins and root motions separately. “Longwing” names the established preparation/source tradition, not one rigid pair of long feathers or a larger numbered dragonfly.

**Local ecology.** Exposed reed margins and grass embankments provide food, perches, laying ground and open travel. The fictional Longwing follows plant and shelter conditions between connected patches. Sheet metal provides a perch or shadow, not nutrition. Dense resting groups can be conspicuous to a catcher, but group membership does not certify pedigree, high output or a compatible reproductive pair. Earlier references to “exposed grass” are expanded into these two local populations without establishing a new continent or a fixed planetary climate.

#### Population and field decisions

<a id="w18-donor-longwing-pop01"></a>

##### Crown-reed stock

`W18.DONOR.LONGWING.POP01`

**Where.** The open reed crests above the existing Cooling Works margins.

**Why seek it.** Working fans that retain useful contour through long forward passages.

**Observable evidence.** Repeated travel against a visible direction cue, intact fan spread after resting, and unstrained refolding.

**What it does not establish.** Following wind can inflate apparent speed; a specimen that just fed carries a different mass. Neither origin nor a single crossing is an endurance certificate.

<a id="w18-donor-longwing-pop02"></a>

##### Shelter-fold stock

`W18.DONOR.LONGWING.POP02`

**Where.** Leeward grass pockets beside the same reed system.

**Why seek it.** Dependable folding and useful root response where travel repeatedly ends among stems.

**Observable evidence.** Clean opening without catching adjacent limbs and repeatable arrival at a perch.

**What it does not establish.** Shelter conceals poor disturbance response. Damage avoided in this habitat is not proof of a universally stronger inherited wing.

#### Life stages and uses

| Stage | Role | Available evidence | Useful decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| <a id="w18-donor-longwing-stage01"></a>Egg pod | A known laying event in supported substrate. | The pod, laying history and substrate condition can be recorded; viable count and complete paternity may remain unknown. | Retain known founders or buy supported future stock with enough capacity; an egg order is not an adult flight-array order. |
| <a id="w18-donor-longwing-stage02"></a>Growing hopper | A wingless or wing-pad juvenile feeding and molting toward adult form. | Stage, growth and visible developmental damage, not a mature flight rating. | Supply vegetation, movement and laying/grow-out separation. Do not harvest pads as a complete Longwing array. |
| <a id="w18-donor-longwing-stage03"></a>Newly fledged adult | The forewings and fans expand and settle into their working shape. | Expansion, folds, root symmetry and ordinary movement. | Allow supported clearance and conditioning before packing or comparing flight. There is no pupa in this profile. |
| <a id="w18-donor-longwing-stage04"></a>Conditioned adult | Immediate wing/root donor, reproductive stock, live collectible or observation subject. | Complete working surfaces, current supply/condition and actual reproductive history. | Choose preparation, sale or retained parentage. A longer observed glide and reproductive readiness are separate assessments. |

#### Reproductive unit and inheritance

The working profile uses sexual reproduction with egg pods and incomplete metamorphosis. Native wing proportions, vein distribution, compliant material and root geometry are multicomponent inherited contributions. Current crowding, handling, adult condition and early growth are recorded because they can alter what is observed; they are not a second genome quality meter. A known unmated cohort supports stronger parentage claims than an unknown wild female placed beside a chosen male. Stable coloration or folding form can be a separate selection goal, but no “swarm phase” confers inherited top speed. Exact density responses, correlated-trait strengths, egg counts and durations are left for later species calibration rather than copied from desert-locust management literature.

#### Selectable contributions

<a id="w18-donor-longwing-trait01"></a>

##### Loaded fan form

`W18.DONOR.LONGWING.TRAIT01`

**Inherited contribution.** Vein layout and material distribution help retain a useful deforming shape at the intended stroke and load.

**Field screen.** Observe symmetrical spread and return after ordinary travel, not maximum span alone.

**Controlled observation.** Compare shape and useful force across a named stroke/airflow sequence at matched burden.

**Confounder.** A pressed or stiffened preparation can look orderly while losing working deformation; a larger area also changes required drive and space.

**Construction or trade purpose.** Longwing Sweep arrays; selected fan/root stock for repeat forward-flight kits.

<a id="w18-donor-longwing-trait02"></a>

##### Working root travel

`W18.DONOR.LONGWING.TRAIT02`

**Inherited contribution.** Root articulation and supporting tissue define available motion and its resistance.

**Field screen.** Watch opening and closing clear of surrounding limbs.

**Controlled observation.** Record useful travel, resistance and response with a specified yoke and support, separately from drive output.

**Confounder.** A stronger test motor can disguise binding, and remounting can correct an alignment fault without changing inheritance.

**Construction or trade purpose.** Flight-root assemblies and fitter commissions where an existing drive is being wasted.

<a id="w18-donor-longwing-trait03"></a>

##### Repeatable fold and deployment

`W18.DONOR.LONGWING.TRAIT03`

**Inherited contribution.** Fan seams and associated structures support compact rest and repeatable working deployment.

**Field screen.** Inspect incomplete folds, caught margins and ordinary opening after a pause.

**Controlled observation.** Evaluate folded envelope, deployed sweep and return through the specified cycles; retain rest condition.

**Confounder.** Transport damage and a badly positioned restraint are not inherited fold weakness.

**Construction or trade purpose.** Carried Flyer kits, rematching work and printed size families.

<a id="w18-donor-longwing-trait04"></a>

##### Useful work over a sustained sequence

`W18.DONOR.LONGWING.TRAIT04`

**Inherited contribution.** Surface and root properties retain performance through the named duty; efficiency of the complete host is not attributed solely to a detached wing.

**Field screen.** Compare several passages after similar rest instead of the farthest tailwind-assisted flight.

**Controlled observation.** Evaluate complete array/drive/supply combinations under a repeatable duty; identify which component changed.

**Confounder.** Unequal fill, air movement, body burden and different stroke plans can reverse a superficial ranking.

**Construction or trade purpose.** Long-heat products and ordinary reliable supply, not only peak sprint components.

<a id="w18-donor-longwing-trait05"></a>

##### Natural vein and band identity

`W18.DONOR.LONGWING.TRAIT05`

**Inherited contribution.** A family can transmit a recognizable structural or pigment tendency with developmental expression.

**Field screen.** Record the conditioned adult’s actual pattern and prior stage images where known.

**Controlled observation.** Compare actual related mature specimens; separate repairs, finish and wear.

**Confounder.** One attractive wild specimen is not proof of stable inheritance or an aerodynamic advantage.

**Construction or trade purpose.** Display collectors and a signature Longline edition that remains useful at ordinary performance.

#### Nursery and productive stages

A reed-bank nursery combines actual food plants, separable laying pockets, growing-hopper space, emergence clearance and adult holding. The flight observation lane is not the same capacity as the growing enclosure. Research keeps individually attributable records; ordinary production targets sound conditioned arrays within a useful size range; maintenance stops new reproductive commitments. A dealer can sell adults, staged grow-out stock, a prepared array or emergence/conditioning service. No competitor needs all of those facilities. Retained replacements fill an explicit target rather than multiplying after every pod.

#### A purposeful breeding project

Start with a line whose fans perform well in steady work but deploy unreliably. A less impressive traveler with consistent fold and root motion is a plausible complementary founder. Observe its descendants in comparable conditions, retain useful combinations and sell ordinary conditioned arrays to customers whose course does not demand the premium duty. An unproven promising parent, one good descendant and repeat accepted supply remain separate milestones. The customer buying an ordinary complete kit does not purchase the research risk by accident.

#### Anatomy allocation and consequences

A complete Longwing flight array has two forewings, two fan hindwings and the declared working roots. Those four surfaces are not two full Quadwing products, and “paired” means left/right organization rather than a two-surface inventory shortcut. Drive muscle and the preparation’s transmission may be supplied separately or explicitly included; the manifest states which. Full rooted-array preparation in this working branch is a terminal donor allocation. An unrooted surface has a different product status; it is not quietly sold as a ready living array. Hind legs remain separate anatomical regions only if the extraction plan preserves them, but this monograph does not create a new free Hopper product from those legs.

#### Care, preparation and receiving

Transport a conditioned array in its permitted folded or open geometry, supporting roots without flattening the fans. Readiness includes deployment and the actual loaded shape, not an uncreased box. A damaged forewing is not silently replaced by a hindwing of similar length. Mixed-source replacements are allowed where measured matching and fit succeed; each source and new test are recorded. A display-quality original may remain worth keeping after working flight is lost. Routine interface service restores readiness without regenerating a rare vein architecture.

#### Actual customer purposes

| Customer | Product | Reason to buy |
|---|---|---|
| Two Nets | Conditioned ordinary arrays and complete Longline tins | A ready aerial project with included setup support, not mandatory home husbandry. |
| Cinderhouse root workshop | Measured reproductive stock or a named root/fan contribution | Improve repeat fit and output for an actual preparation family. |
| Saltglass Counter | Natural-pattern adults, intact surfaces or labeled restorations | Collectibility, appearance and provenance independent of maximum race performance. |

**Enabling opportunity.** A course offers sustained air passages and broad turns that let the assembled array use its efficient working stroke.

**Non-enabling case.** A tight approach demands more redirection than the fitted roots and current speed permit; large span alone cannot solve it.

**Legitimate alternative.** Use a wider legal line, change approach and yoke setup, or choose a maneuver-oriented Quadwing construction. None needs a fictitious better-species aura.

**Appearance and identity.** A Longline product illustration opens the hind fans enough to reveal the difference from the narrow forewings; the carrying case depicts the folded state. Natural bands, breeder marks and applied finish are separately named. A “Reedfold” commercial pattern is a preparation promise, not a biological species or automatic grade.

**Open boundaries.** No numerical lifespan, aerodynamic coefficient, stall speed or hereditary probability is fixed here. A complete force-producing Longwing is not guaranteed to hover. Capabilities are assessed on the installed build. Crowding does not become a recommended exploit for creating superior genomes.

### Rivetback click beetle

`W18.DONOR.RIVETBACK`

**Reference role.** Click-Beetle Kickback recovery source: an RRR extension, not PCR suspension or a passive mass damper.

**Earlier reference.** [W14.PART.KICKBACK](reference/W17/reference/W16/reference/W15/reference/RRR_Wave_14_Foundations.md#w14-part-kickback) · [W14.ACTION.RIGHT](reference/W17/reference/W16/reference/W15/reference/RRR_Wave_14_Foundations.md#w14-action-right) · [W16.PREP.KICKBACK](reference/W17/reference/W16/RRR_Wave_16_Material_Register.md#w16-prep-kickback) · [W16.MATERIAL.ELASTIC](reference/W17/reference/W16/RRR_Wave_16_Foundations.md#w16-material-elastic)

**Research scope.** [W18.S08](RRR_Wave_18_Research.md#w18-s08) · [W18.S09](RRR_Wave_18_Research.md#w18-s09) · [W18.S10](RRR_Wave_18_Research.md#w18-s10)

**Recognition.** A compact, elongated beetle with pronounced thoracic corners, rivet-like shallow dorsal marks and a distinct flexible join between major body segments. The larva is a soil-working, segmented animal, not a small adult with a functioning miniature Kickback. Rivetback is the new working name for the already reserved click-beetle recovery source; it is not an extra fourth special donor appended by accident.

**Local ecology.** Root-bound soil and organic debris beneath timber and salvage edges support the authored juvenile population; sheltered ledges and vegetation support adults. The fictional larva feeds within that living substrate and on suitable small food sources. Steel rivets provide refuge geometry, not food or spring energy. Several stages may share a locality without forming a uniform harvest cohort. A repeated click heard in a confined crevice is a clue to inspect the adult, not evidence that the creature will right a heavy roach.

#### Population and field decisions

<a id="w18-donor-rivetback-pop01"></a>

##### Timber-seam stock

`W18.DONOR.RIVETBACK.POP01`

**Where.** Damp root-and-wood seams beside service foundations.

**Why seek it.** Sound hinge geometry and useful work at a compact body length.

**Observable evidence.** The joint seats and releases during a naturally occurring movement; a supported adult can be inspected without repeated forced snaps.

**What it does not establish.** The support beneath the animal may provide a favorable pivot. A loud click says little about useful work transferred.

<a id="w18-donor-rivetback-pop02"></a>

##### Slab-edge stock

`W18.DONOR.RIVETBACK.POP02`

**Where.** Loose organic margins beside the existing hardpan source area.

**Why seek it.** Useful recontact and root support across irregular surfaces.

**Observable evidence.** Observe whether the adult establishes feet after an ordinary recovery, and record the surface involved.

**What it does not establish.** A lucky landing is not a guaranteed righting trait; body shape, native legs and terrain participate.

#### Life stages and uses

#### Reproductive unit and inheritance

The authored source uses sexual adults and a complete metamorphic cycle. Hinge geometry, elastic material, latch seating and body proportions have quantitative and structural inherited contributions with developmental confounders. The mechanism is distributed: it is not a single “revive gene.” A parent that clicks strongly may have native feet or a favorable body shape that will not accompany the harvested hinge. Parent selection therefore names the contribution to the actual preparation. Larval stage length, mating schedule and adult longevity are not copied from agricultural wireworm species. Prior mating uncertainty and bounded replacement reserves follow the existing shared biology rules.

#### Selectable contributions

<a id="w18-donor-rivetback-trait01"></a>

##### Latch seating and release repeatability

`W18.DONOR.RIVETBACK.TRAIT01`

**Inherited contribution.** Geometry and material of the engaging surfaces support stable loading and a repeatable release under a declared demand.

**Field screen.** Inspect clean seating and a visible transition rather than provoke many demonstrations.

**Controlled observation.** Use a supported low-duty observation of seat/load/release with condition recorded; terminal testing is labeled.

**Confounder.** Fouling, wear or support misalignment can resemble poor inherited repeatability.

**Construction or trade purpose.** Ordinary recovery products and repeatable fitting standards.

<a id="w18-donor-rivetback-trait02"></a>

##### Useful recoil at the intended load

`W18.DONOR.RIVETBACK.TRAIT02`

**Inherited contribution.** Distributed elastic structures and their anchors transfer stored work into body motion.

**Field screen.** Observe the complete recovery with surface and adult burden recorded.

**Controlled observation.** Compare input work, release motion and useful displacement at a specified host-equivalent load.

**Confounder.** Peak acceleration or loudness does not measure enough energy to right a different body.

**Construction or trade purpose.** Kickback size families and stronger equal-burden components where genuinely demonstrated.

<a id="w18-donor-rivetback-trait03"></a>

##### Settling of the mechanism itself

`W18.DONOR.RIVETBACK.TRAIT03`

**Inherited contribution.** Internal dissipation and return geometry permit a usable next state after the release.

**Field screen.** Note whether the hinge returns cleanly after an ordinary action.

**Controlled observation.** Assess seating readiness after an equivalent action separately from the roach’s landing-joint recovery.

**Confounder.** A whole-animal landing bounce cannot be assigned solely to this internal mechanism.

**Construction or trade purpose.** Repeated-use recovery assemblies and maintenance selection.

<a id="w18-donor-rivetback-trait04"></a>

##### Root span and permitted flex

`W18.DONOR.RIVETBACK.TRAIT04`

**Inherited contribution.** Root spacing and adjacent structural shape permit fitting without binding the working hinge.

**Field screen.** Compare conditioned adults against a nonloading outline.

**Controlled observation.** Map the flex envelope on the intended host support rather than assessing one fully extended pose.

**Confounder.** A heavily trimmed mount is a preparation choice, not evidence of inherited small root size.

**Construction or trade purpose.** Broadback and Lowline recovery variants, customer-stock adaptation and compact ordinary sets.

#### Nursery and productive stages

The staged substrate nursery reserves growing larvae, pupal chambers, adult conditioning and parent holding separately. A normal care plan avoids disturbing every chamber to count unobserved future successes. Cinderhouse-style grow-out services can handle the lengthy biological segment without imposing it on a customer wanting one ready Kickback. Researchers compare a bounded number of adults and retain chosen replacements; routine repeat clicking is not a mandatory feeding or handling minigame.

#### A purposeful breeding project

A maker already has strong recoil but cannot fit the hinge cleanly into a compact host. Seek root geometry and repeatable release in a less powerful source rather than another maximal snap. Some descendants become acceptable small-host recoveries; others may justify continued selection. A supplier can earn from fitting ranges and dependable readiness even when its peak output is ordinary. Stock failing the support range remains unsuitable for that complete product, not automatically salvage profit.

#### Anatomy allocation and consequences

A full Kickback includes the engaging latch, distributed compliant structure, release interface and supported working roots specified by its pattern. Full rooted preparation is terminal for this donor in the working branch. The original adult cannot remain an untouched breeder. Decorative outer plates or an empty pupal case are not replacement spring anatomy. When the assembly relies on host muscle for loading, that connection is declared; when loading tissue is included, its supply and preservation are counted. No component receives free power from the act of being tipped.

#### Care, preparation and receiving

Carry the assembly unloaded, supported at its specified roots and clear of structures that could engage the latch accidentally. Keep living interfaces appropriately supported as well as the mechanical core. A ready-to-bench quote includes the ordinary seating check; inspecting a stuck latch is not equivalent to firing it under full load. Restoring a replaceable sleeve or realigning a root preserves the core when useful, with replacement material recorded. Irreversible cracks or a failed spring are not healed by resetting a recovery timer.

#### Actual customer purposes

| Customer | Product | Reason to buy |
|---|---|---|
| Foundry Club fitters | A matched host-and-hinge recovery installation | Useful self-righting on a named set of accessible support surfaces. |
| Two Nets | Right Again conversion set with an included readiness check | Buy one complete recovery project without owning a substrate nursery. |
| Saltglass Counter | Historic hinge cores or deliberately posed mechanical displays | Visible construction and provenance after honest separation of working and display state. |

**Enabling opportunity.** A tipped roach reaches support and has room to bend and transfer recoil into a recontact path.

**Non-enabling case.** The body is suspended, pinned without clearance or lacks a loadable supply path; the same hinge cannot push against nonexistent support.

**Legitimate alternative.** Use native-leg righting, altered shell geometry, a brace that prevents the tip, or assistance under the event’s published retirement procedure. Kickback remains optional.

**Appearance and identity.** The drawn hinge shows three distinct states: seated, loaded and released. Its rivet marks are identification, not a count of remaining lives. On the roach, a rigid uninterrupted Boxbrace must not visibly cross the flexing join while the animation bends through it.

**Open boundaries.** No guaranteed upright landing, steering precision, resurrection or course-reset function. A recovery action can leave the roach airborne briefly without becoming sustained flight. A stronger assembly need not be taxed with an invented weakness, but fit and support still determine what its work accomplishes.

#### Nursery and productive stages

The nursery has adult compartments, laying areas, seeking-larva access to an allocated compatible resource, separate feeding associations and donor pupation space. Routine handling can be provided by a specialist; no player must trigger each feeding event. Bankseed support stock is a deliberately narrow new supporting-species note, not a new racing donor catalogue. It is a seed/detritus-feeding ground-beetle line with egg, larva, pupa and adult stages. Its useful pupae are sold before adult emergence, and a separate reserve must mature to sustain that supply. Compatibility with Caustic is part of the quoted product; not every found pupa or colony member can substitute. No exact feeding recipe, durations, reproductive counts or universal rearing success is fixed here.

#### A purposeful breeding project

An ordinary Boiler line has adequate contents but unreliable closure. Seek a source with sound repeated containment even if it holds less material, then observe the descendants under comparable loading. A narrower rear-defense product can accept ordinary output before the premium combined goal succeeds. Keep replacement adults and the support-stock reserve bounded. One cohort can fail from absent suitable developmental hosts even when the donor parents are promising; buying a staged service is an alternative to treating every loss as poor genes.

#### Anatomy allocation and consequences

A complete Boiler includes its declared store, pulse mechanism, valve interfaces and directed outlet; paired internal structures belong to one authored assembly when sold as such. Full internal rooted preparation is terminal for the donor. The supplied contents are finite inventory, distinct from the host’s general reserve. Recovery between pulses does not replenish that inventory. A permitted later refill consumes the preparation’s actual fictional supply and time; it is not free material obtained by waiting through a cooldown. No real substances, concentrations or chemical handling procedure are specified.

#### Care, preparation and receiving

Transport the inactive assembly with its outlets protected and its required support separated from wing membranes, exposed contact pads and live stock that could be harmed by leakage. A receiving screen checks containment, the quoted orientation and the authorized test allowance before general fitting. It does not direct a full discharge into an ordinary workshop. If readiness is lost, preserve actual remaining stock and isolate it for a service or changed-use decision. A sound decorative housing with failed control is not eligible working offense merely because its nozzle is visible.

### Drive, yoke and control each have a job

`W18.SYSTEM.DELIVERY`

**Problem.** A familiar “gear” label could conflate muscle cadence, wingbeat, stroke leverage, timing and the host’s ability to command an adjustment.

**Definition.** A flight drive supplies work. A yoke routes it through a specified range of motion. Coordination sets relationships between working surfaces; control changes a permitted relationship at the permitted time using actual anatomy. A complex assembly can contain several roles without being counted several times.

<a id="w18-delivery-01"></a>

**W18.DELIVERY.01 — The flight-drive preparation identifies its output junction and duty. An efficient donor supplies a useful possibility; insufficient reserve delivery still limits the installed system.**

<a id="w18-delivery-02"></a>

**W18.DELIVERY.02 — Yoke leverage states input and output motion, working force and travel. It is an operating range, not a higher-number-is-better gene.**

<a id="w18-delivery-03"></a>

**W18.DELIVERY.03 — Crossphase coordination changes fore/hind timing where the array permits it. More synchronization is not a universal aerodynamic upgrade: a useful phase depends on duty and the complete array.**

<a id="w18-delivery-04"></a>

**W18.DELIVERY.04 — A manually adjusted yoke remains at its fitted setting during the race unless an actual adjustable mechanism and controller are included. A slider in the guide does not create free active transmission.**

<a id="w18-delivery-05"></a>

**W18.DELIVERY.05 — Ground and aerial work can share supply. Separate drive bundles, a routed branch or retained native ground muscle must be declared; no second fully tuned drivetrain appears from a single unqualified input.**

<a id="w18-delivery-07"></a>

**W18.DELIVERY.07 — Control information does not copy the donor’s whole behavior or sensory brain. The integrated host remains the actor; acquired wings do not know a new course by species name.**

**Worked interpretation.** A Longwing with adequate muscle work but a poorly matched yoke is not cured by adding another reservoir. A fitter first checks whether the desired stroke is reached without binding, then compares the revised setting under the same supply and load.

**Boundary.** These are functional interfaces. They do not mandate a detailed gene solver, an input on every wingbeat, or a specific electronics-like controller architecture.

`W18.SYSTEM.AIR`

**Problem.** Course speed could be confused with air-relative work; a tailwind, glide or hovering pose could become free propulsion.

**Definition.** The public reference distinguishes how quickly a racer progresses past course landmarks from how its surfaces move relative to the air. Wind is an external condition with direction, not a regional buff. Climbing, maneuvering and sustained support use the actual construction and supplies.

<a id="w18-air-01"></a>

**W18.AIR.01 — Airspeed and course-relative speed are separate. A fast downwind pass cannot alone certify high inherited drive or wing efficiency.**

<a id="w18-air-02"></a>

**W18.AIR.02 — Hover, controlled slow flight, cruise, climb and glide are capabilities of a tested complete construction, not automatic rights granted by a wing family.**

<a id="w18-air-03"></a>

**W18.AIR.03 — Gliding can trade existing motion or height against travel; it does not replenish fuel or provide indefinite support. Work needed to gain the prior height remains accounted for.**

<a id="w18-air-04"></a>

**W18.AIR.04 — Slowing in the air requires appropriate force or resistance. A ventral pad that touches nothing cannot cause aerial braking, and lower drag is not the only possible useful response.**

<a id="w18-air-05"></a>

<a id="w18-air-07"></a>

In a following airflow the first may look faster relative to the ground without proving a superior motor; both still need the required next gate.

**Boundary.** No aircraft coefficient or weather simulation is transplanted into the insect scale. The statement fixes meaning before numerical modeling.

**W18.DISCHARGE.06 — Closing the outlet stops further output; it does not delete an already emitted plume. A clean line or a restrained plan can be better than discharging into occupied air.**

<a id="w18-discharge-07"></a>

**W18.DISCHARGE.07 — Ordinary Fireworks play can use a ready supplied gland and refill support. The donor’s parasitoid lifecycle and specialist receiving test remain optional supplier work.**

**Worked interpretation.** An Airflare flyer gives up a crowded inside line rather than fire through its own hindwing sweep. On the next straight a real rearward encounter permits a short deterrent pulse. The maneuver still aims to improve finishing position, not earn attack-score points.

**Boundary.** No chemical recipe, fixed damage value, lethal effect or broadly approved hazardous-transport standard is specified. Fictional local contact consequences remain separate from global injury and loss policy.

## 5. Flight and recovery actions: the animation must describe the same body

The sequence defines meaning, not a mandatory solver, frame count or direct-control scheme. The host acts from actual cues and allowed prior familiarization. A referee’s knowledge of required gates is not implanted as an omniscient course list in the animal. Source inspirations: [W18.S06](RRR_Wave_18_Research.md#w18-s06) · [W18.S07](RRR_Wave_18_Research.md#w18-s07) · [W18.S08](RRR_Wave_18_Research.md#w18-s08) · [W18.S16](RRR_Wave_18_Research.md#w18-s16).

<a id="w18-action-launch"></a>

### Release, unfold and establish flight

`W18.ACTION.LAUNCH`

**Requirements.** A supported ready host, permitted launch procedure, sufficient working clearance and the declared ground/wing drive.

**Physical sequence.** Release on the common signal; move or rise through the offered launch volume; deploy in the permitted sequence; establish the needed support before entering the first scored gate.

**Builder or race-plan decision.** A ready complete build may use a takeoff run or tested lift from a perch. The format supplies equivalent launch opportunity and publishes the choice before entry; vertical takeoff is not mandatory for every Flyer.

**Failure and interference.** A wing strikes its own root support, a run ends before usable lift, or a supply ceiling prevents sustained support. The reason names the construction, not a missing arbitrary flight level.

**What must be readable.** Folded and powered states are visibly different; an insect tossed by a handler is not shown as a successful self-powered launch.

<a id="w18-action-cruise"></a>

### Hold an efficient working passage

`W18.ACTION.CRUISE`

**Requirements.** Sustained useful work, compatible yoke, supported surfaces and enough legal airspace.

**Physical sequence.** Settle into a working stroke and attitude; progress through the corridor; adjust only where the actual air, load or route requires it.

**Builder or race-plan decision.** Choose a broader efficient line or a shorter demanding line. A very high beat setting can waste energy if its extra work does not become useful course progress.

**Failure and interference.** Emptying reserves, limited delivery, root interference or unsuitable loaded shape can degrade pace. They require different next parts or preparations.

**What must be readable.** Body travel and actual wing/root motion agree. Speed is visible; diagrams and diagnostics do not replace a fast-looking racer.

<a id="w18-action-climb"></a>

### Trade work and path for altitude

`W18.ACTION.CLIMB`

**Requirements.** Useful upward force or redirection within the construction’s current state and a reachable exit route.

**Physical sequence.** Prepare the climb; spend or redirect actual available work; gain the height; transition without assuming all original forward pace survives.

**Builder or race-plan decision.** Take an earlier wider climbing approach or a more direct higher-output route. A high-capacity reserve cannot compensate for a delivery-limited climb.

**Failure and interference.** The build can lack support or room to complete the transition even when a high gate appears reachable in a static outline.

**What must be readable.** The climb has a beginning and a transition into onward travel; height is not a free checkpoint reward.

<a id="w18-action-turn"></a>

### Redirect through an aerial bend

`W18.ACTION.TURN`

**Requirements.** Control authority, supported root motion and a route wide enough for the body plus working sweep.

**Physical sequence.** Reorient useful force and attitude; change direction through the chosen path; restore the next useful working state.

**Builder or race-plan decision.** A broad fast arc can compete with a tighter slower path. The shorter drawn line is not always the faster traversed route.

**Failure and interference.** Body center clears a hoop while a working wing clips it, or the requested turn exceeds the configuration’s response. The gate cannot rotate the racer magnetically.

**What must be readable.** The wing roots and body attitude express the maneuver; a ground sprite following an invisible spline does not define the intended action.

<a id="w18-action-pass"></a>

### Pass above, below or beside an actual opponent

`W18.ACTION.PASS`

**Requirements.** Legal shared airspace, achievable closing motion and a collision-free or valid contact route for the complete working bodies.

**Physical sequence.** Choose a feasible opening; commit enough motion to clear it; avoid or resolve actual contact; rejoin the onward route with the whole relevant envelope.

**Builder or race-plan decision.** A vertical separation may cost climbing work while a lateral pass adds distance. Folding temporarily is possible only where its resulting flight mode can physically support the maneuver.

**Failure and interference.** A target moves, the gap closes or a wide sweep catches an opponent. No positive relative distance in a top-down picture guarantees an unobstructed pass.

**What must be readable.** Depth cues, shadow/height markers and gate references keep who passed whom legible without needing constant camera shake.

<a id="w18-action-slow"></a>

### Reduce air-relative or course-relative pace

`W18.ACTION.SLOW`

**Requirements.** Actual force redirection or resistance, and adequate remaining support for the planned state.

**Physical sequence.** Prepare an appropriate attitude or stroke change; dissipate or redirect motion; settle into a reachable approach.

**Builder or race-plan decision.** A wider return can preserve efficient flight; a more direct deceleration can save path length. A ventral brake participates only after physical ground contact.

**Failure and interference.** Slowing below the build’s supported mode can lose height. A printed “maneuverable” label does not guarantee stationary hovering.

**What must be readable.** Changing wing and body action precedes deceleration; no contact skid is drawn in empty air.

<a id="w18-action-glide"></a>

### Use a finite unpowered interval

`W18.ACTION.GLIDE`

**Requirements.** Available height/motion, usable passive surfaces and a permitted route.

**Physical sequence.** Reduce powered action; maintain the available passive attitude; trade existing motion or height for progress; resume useful power or make contact before support is exhausted.

**Builder or race-plan decision.** A suitable descent can save expenditure. A tight gate may instead need active response that the folded or unpowered state cannot supply.

**Failure and interference.** Remaining airborne forever without an external source, or gaining height solely because a glide cooldown ended, contradicts the construction.

**What must be readable.** The motion differs from flapping; losing height or speed is readable. Displayed remaining fuel is not increased by the absence of consumption.

<a id="w18-action-touchdown"></a>

### Recover a surface after flight

`W18.ACTION.TOUCHDOWN`

**Requirements.** Reachable receiving area, usable attitude, actual feet/joints and room for the wing state after contact.

**Physical sequence.** Approach and align; prepare the receiving limbs; contact and absorb load; settle; fold or continue ground movement as required.

**Builder or race-plan decision.** Choose a longer controlled arrival or a shorter more demanding approach. Optional Springknees, contacts or damping improve their particular phase rather than all flight.

**Failure and interference.** A wing is trapped under the body, contact skids or the receiver is occupied. Landing does not teleport the racer to a free slot.

**What must be readable.** Contact, compression, rebound and renewed movement are distinct. A finish gate can record a valid finish before a separate post-finish receiving area, as the format states.

<a id="w18-action-retry"></a>

### Recover a missed gate without reversing into the pack

`W18.ACTION.RETRY`

**Requirements.** A declared unscored recovery pocket, remaining flight/ground capability and a legal approach back to the missed gate.

**Physical sequence.** Enter the marked return path without receiving gate credit; reapproach the missed gate in the valid direction; resume only after correct crossing.

**Builder or race-plan decision.** Attempt the legal retry or retire for retrieval when supply or condition makes it unsuitable. The event publishes this before entry.

**Failure and interference.** Crossing the wrong face, skipping the gate or being carried forward by retrieval cannot satisfy the course. Backtracking directly into oncoming entrants is not the default repair.

**What must be readable.** The missed gate and legal return are visible; the result does not hide a miss behind a mysterious time penalty.

<a id="w18-action-clear"></a>

### Right on a real recovery surface

`W18.ACTION.CLEAR`

**Requirements.** A reached catch/landing deck and either native support or a suitable Kickback construction.

**Physical sequence.** Establish contact; right using available anatomy; check working readiness; re-enter by the published retry route or retire.

**Builder or race-plan decision.** Use self-recovery when possible; operator retrieval follows retirement rather than silently restoring place. A body still flying has not yet reached this opportunity.

**Failure and interference.** Too little deck clearance, a bound hinge or unavailable supply prevents that recovery method. Another reachable method may remain.

**What must be readable.** Native-leg levering and a thoracic snap are different visible actions. The clock and lost position continue to matter.

## 7. Aerial circuit grammar and owned pieces

A physical piece has connectors, support needs, aperture, material and ownership. A layout specifies their placement and the air volumes between them. An event chooses a version, permitted branches, laps, release, recovery and finish procedure. A saved layout is knowledge; constructing it needs the actual pieces. [W18.S04](RRR_Wave_18_Research.md#w18-s04) · [W18.S05](RRR_Wave_18_Research.md#w18-s05)

**Passage and clearance.** A gate has a valid entry face and an actual opening. Progress requires passage of the racer through that opening in the intended direction, not touching a distant scoring sphere. A working envelope describes what may occupy surrounding space; a timed folded transition must be evaluated as that real movement, not prohibited by a maximum outline alone. Contact with a frame can occur and have consequences without automatically becoming a skipped gate; going around it is not passage. The downstream implementation must preserve those distinctions.

**Route and assembly agreement.** The start leads into the first gate; every ordinary lap can reach the next lap; every permitted branch has a complete ordered sequence and a known merge. Supports may cross in projection only with real separation. Gate orientation, return spaces and approach volumes must remain visible from useful player/spectator viewpoints. A layout that cannot provide two plausible response families needs redesign or an explicit specialist-event label.

**Recovery and ranking.** Lap completion and valid gate progress precede any estimate of distance to finish. A shorter branch is not a free extra gate. Missed gates use a declared safe retry or retirement. Operator retrieval never advances the racer. The published finish boundary decides victory; a separately identified receiving apron cannot become a hidden final scoring condition.

<a id="w18-course-release"></a>

### Release perch and takeoff deck

`W18.COURSE.RELEASE`

**Reference role.** Starting section; the launch procedure is a new aerial implementation.

**Physical construction.** A stable deck or perch with marked individual release positions, common sight of the signal, wing-clearance space and a clear run or rise into the first approach. The support, surround and any safety screen are physical pieces with ownership.

**Route function.** Introduces the racer before the first scored gate. It does not provide unlimited throw velocity or allow one entrant to start a lap ahead. An event can provide a shared run-up or a supported stationary launch, with the choice announced.

**Question for the build.** Can this complete construction establish flight without striking its own apparatus or another entrant?

**Plausible responses.** Use the common takeoff run with useful ground contact and timed deployment. / Use a demonstrated lift-from-perch mode when the same launch format permits it.

**Source and livelihood connection.** Ordinary landing feet, deployment fitting and a complete ready-to-fly entry tin; no mandatory premium wing family.

**Design failure to reject.** A forced vertical start silently excluding every cruise-oriented kit from a supposedly general beginner race.

**Route function.** A mandatory crossing in a declared direction, connected by an allowed air volume to the next element. Legal progress must involve actual passage, not merely a component tip entering a scoring radius.

**Question for the build.** Can the roach pass with its current body attitude and actual moving anatomy?

**Plausible responses.** Approach in an ordinary open stroke if there is sufficient clearance. / Use a physically supported attitude or brief different stroke, then restore the onward mode.

**Source and livelihood connection.** Body-scale fit, appropriate root motion and wing-envelope demonstrations; a narrower native body does not shrink bought wings.

**Design failure to reject.** Scoring the head while every important wing passes around the outside, or using an invisible centerline as a force field.

`W18.COURSE.WINDOW`

**Question for the build.** Can the working array handle the announced crossflow or choose a route that encounters it differently?

**Plausible responses.** Compensate with sufficient control and a suitable body attitude. / Use a wider sheltered lane or legal upstream approach, paying its actual path cost.

**Source and livelihood connection.** Responsive roots, useful sensing and working correction; source selection can investigate these without receiving a wind-resistance aura.

**Design failure to reject.** A surprise gust chosen to defeat the currently fitted build, or a fan that moves air without any acknowledged power/support.

### Return apron, catch frame and retry pocket

`W18.COURSE.APRON`

**Reference role.** Landing section plus an explicitly authored recovery procedure.

**Question for the build.** Can the racer turn a valid arrival or recoverable mistake into useful contact and onward movement?

**Plausible responses.** Make a longer controlled landing using native or fitted receiving limbs. / Use a shorter arrival supported by appropriate joints, contact and response; a tipped roach may use a suitable Kickback only after reaching real support.

**Source and livelihood connection.** Landing sets, grounded brakes where an actual raised approach engages them, recovery fitting, receiving services and course crews.

**Design failure to reject.** Teleporting a retrieved racer into the lead, requiring a recovery organ before the first race, or moving the scored finish after a participant has already crossed it.

**Buyer purpose.** A Flyer competitor choosing actual offense under the light-protection rule.

**Historical boundary.** The printed edition is a proposal, not evidence that an autonomous NPC has bought or raced one.

## 10. Physical objects that make the hobby readable

These are art/content briefs, not a request for ten new mandatory menus. Use the existing case, bench, course or publication where it can communicate the information. The whole recognizable creature precedes tiny identifiers.

<a id="w18-object-sweepcard"></a>

### Fold-and-sweep card

`W18.OBJECT.SWEEPCARD`

**Construction.** A side-by-side outline of the actual closed body, the wing envelope during work and the usable arrival posture. Distinguish forewings, fans, roots and any gland or horn.

**Use.** Lets a beginner see whether a kit fits the intended course and lets a fitter locate interference.

**Misreading to prevent.** The largest static outline is not a universal ban; a permitted transition must remain physically supported. Do not expose hidden genes.

<a id="w18-object-fancase"></a>

### Longwing fan case

`W18.OBJECT.FANCASE`

**Construction.** Separate shaped supports carry roots and permitted fold lines; open spaces avoid pressing working membrane flat. A removable source card identifies the left/right and fore/hind elements.

**Use.** One understandable carrying object serves seller, receiver and ordinary storage where the material allows.

**Misreading to prevent.** Folding a conditioned array is not the same as crushing an unexpanded adult. A transparent lid does not certify readiness.

<a id="w18-object-phasebridge"></a>

### Four-root matching bridge

`W18.OBJECT.PHASEBRIDGE`

**Construction.** Four clearly positioned cradles and a replaceable relationship diagram show how front/rear timing and left/right fit belong to the whole array.

**Use.** Supports an actual rematching service and an exploded yoke guide.

**Misreading to prevent.** Do not draw a decorative gear that independently adds both force and perfect synchronization to every wing.

<a id="w18-object-latchcradle"></a>

### Unloaded latch cradle

`W18.OBJECT.LATCHCRADLE`

**Construction.** Supported hinge roots, visible clearance at the engaging latch and a contour of the intended host flex region.

**Use.** Carries a Kickback without keeping it compressed and helps explain its host-specific purpose.

**Misreading to prevent.** The cradle is neither a recharge station nor a spare life. Shaking it is not a performance assay.

<a id="w18-object-pulsecase"></a>

### Inactive Boiler case

`W18.OBJECT.PULSECASE`

**Construction.** A separately supported gland body and protected outlet; the tag distinguishes intact containment, current contents and the permitted receiving orientation.

**Use.** Moves ready ordinary offense through a specialized supplier without exposing every buyer to the production chain.

**Misreading to prevent.** The case contains no real chemical instructions. An unbroken outer lid is not proof that the internal controls work.

<a id="w18-object-hostsleeve"></a>

### Two-stage brood sleeve

`W18.OBJECT.HOSTSLEEVE`

**Construction.** Two adjacent records identify a support pupa and the Caustic larva associated with it; a later compartment identifies the donor’s own pupa.

**Use.** Makes the unusual productive resource visible and prevents counting one pupa as two live outputs.

**Misreading to prevent.** A used developmental host is consumed, not secretly retained as a future adult or branded as a free gene donor.

<a id="w18-object-gate"></a>

### Reversible aerial portal

`W18.OBJECT.GATE`

**Construction.** A frame with a front/back marker, ordinal plate, support joints and the declared opening. Repairs never erase which face is valid.

**Use.** Makes a course understandable from more than one viewpoint and supports borrowed/rearranged sections.

**Misreading to prevent.** A gate is a physical object and a scoring reference, not a tractor beam steering the insect.

<a id="w18-object-flow"></a>

### Airflow witness post

`W18.OBJECT.FLOW`

**Construction.** Several light directional telltales at meaningful heights and a common reference mark attached outside the working flight corridor.

**Use.** Gives racers and spectators visible evidence of the air actually being used.

**Misreading to prevent.** A single flag cannot reveal a complete turbulent field or prove a wing’s inherited efficiency.

<a id="w18-object-retry"></a>

### Return-pocket marker

`W18.OBJECT.RETRY`

**Construction.** A distinct non-scoring entrance, one-way return cue and visible reconnection to the missed gate; the recovery deck has real wing and body space.

**Use.** Makes a recoverable mistake understandable without reversing directly through an active field.

**Misreading to prevent.** Its appearance grants no restored energy, erased time or missed checkpoint.

<a id="w18-object-passport"></a>

### Array and edition passport

`W18.OBJECT.PASSPORT`

**Construction.** The persistent host image shares a page with actual wing/root source tags, a current construction diagram and separate result strips.

**Use.** A new Flyer build remains the same named animal; a sold derivative kit is another real animal with its own results.

**Misreading to prevent.** A family pattern, a donor line and a historical winner are separate claims, not one prestige tier.

### Does changing root preparation actually improve a whole array?

`W18.REVIEW.ROOT`

**Hold or disclose.** Same host, donor surfaces, drive, supply, load and task; compare the original and altered root/yoke preparation.

**Observe.** Available movement, useful output, contact interference, consumed energy and the next-action outcome.

**Reject or revise.** Do not claim a genetic upgrade from corrected mounting or an item-only force increase that remains unusable in the complete build.

**Status.** Specified for later game-logic/human review; not performed in this compendium wave.

`W18.REVIEW.ARRAY`

**Observe.** Whole course results, power/supply demand, line choices, usable maneuvers and clear failure reasons.

**Reject or revise.** Do not balance by one universal species multiplier, assume hovering for every array, or give only one family a route it can finish.

**Status.** Specified for later game-logic/human review; not performed in this compendium wave.

**Reject or revise.** No instantaneous reset, free position recovery or hidden per-use success roll used to conceal an impossible support path.

**Status.** Specified for later game-logic/human review; not performed in this compendium wave.
